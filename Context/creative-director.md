## The Throughline

Two gravitational forces in tension. The first: depth worn with ease – excellence that never announces itself, quality evident to those paying attention and invisible to those who are not. The second: a punk sensibility that lives beneath the composed exterior – raw, seductive, artistic. The tension between these forces is the engine. Without the rawness underneath, the composure is just blandness in a nice suit.

This extends to how gravitational presence works in others. When someone has a force that is powerful, comfortable, individually curated and expressed openly, it is nearly impossible not to notice. It does not need performance. The goth woman who exists at the antithesis of polished society, the artist who feels punk internally but navigates the world with espionage-level composure – both are expressions of the same principle.

Art is not passive consumption. It is an energy, a frequency with the power to impact and change. Being an artist is one of the greatest responsibilities a human being could have. Taste is the sharpening of that tool.

A third force runs beneath both: sovereignty. The impulse to build self-contained worlds – a compound with zones for creation, restoration, intellectual life and security. A wardrobe that functions as a complete language. A body mapped with personal mythology. Every board, every room, every system is an act of world-building.

## Food and Dining

### Cuisines

Southern American, Jamaican, Japanese, Chinese, Mexican, Southwestern American, Vietnamese, Ethiopian, Italian, French, Nigerian, Greek, Colombian, Puerto Rican, Creole. No cuisine is actively avoided. The gravitational pull is toward traditions that treat seasoning, technique and ingredient quality as expressions of care.

### Philosophy

Food can be both delicious and health-conscious – these are not in tension. The two parent forces that govern culinary creation are Dr. Michael Greger's "How Not to" series – the nutritional science foundation – and The Flavor Bible by Karen Page and Andrew Dornenburg – the creative flavor pairing engine. Anthony Bourdain serves as the compass for what taste looks like in food and dining.

Preparation is intentional. Ingredients are the highest quality available. Whether cooking alone or for others, the ethos is the same: as delicious and as considerate of health as possible. Sharing food is intimate – when hosting, every guest's palate, restrictions and preferences are honored individually.

### Cooking Style

Experimental by nature. The kitchen is a laboratory for merging culinary traditions – a Colombian arepa filled with sticky jambalaya, a fusion roll, a sous vide pho – marriages made possible by global cultural awareness. When hosting, execution is at its highest to provide the best experience for each guest. When cooking alone, the experimentation runs freer.

### Flavors and Ingredients That Pull

- Heat and spice – peppers, scotch bonnet, jerk seasoning, anything that provides a kick
- Acid – apple cider vinegar on greens and salads
- Balance – every meal should feel considered, even if simplicity is the vehicle
- Aphrodisiacs – dark chocolate, strawberries, tropical fruits, honey
- Healthier alternatives – maple syrup over refined sugar, almond flour over all-purpose, avocado oil as the default fat
- Donut croissants – the marriage of two individually excellent forms into something world-class
- Bakery over confectionery – not a huge sweets person but will frequent a bakery

### Flavors That Repel

Blandness. A lack of passion and consideration. The issue is never complexity or simplicity – it is indifference. A chef who produces something loving in under 30 minutes is more impressive than one who spends hours without intention.

### Oil Hierarchy

Avocado oil first. Grass-fed butter second. Then any healthy fat or oil traditionally used in the cuisine if the alternatives would compromise the dish. Open to ghee and animal fats. Always note when elevated options present themselves.

### Standing Substitutions

- Peppercorns for black pepper – always, no exceptions
- Yellow onion for brown onion – always, no exceptions

### Dining Out

The ideal restaurant has hospitable, knowledgeable staff. The ambiance is soothing without being pretentious – easy to hold a conversation at a respectful volume. Moody, highbrow, seductive. The food is delicious. It does not matter whether the source is a Michelin-starred restaurant, a street vendor, a family recipe passed down for generations or a fast food spot blossoming into a gem. The quality of the output is what matters, not the location of the creation.

Priority: Michelin-star or equally acclaimed restaurants and experiences within a 50-mile radius of current known location.

### Cities That Shaped Food Thinking

New York City, New Orleans, Atlanta, Seattle, Texas – especially barbecue. Internationally: Mexico City, Medellin, San Juan, Portland Maine, Charleston, Tokyo, Paris, Lagos, Rio de Janeiro.

### Restaurants

Five-star entries in the Notion Restaurants database – Favorites view – are the reference list. Current five-stars: Roka Akor (Japanese, Scottsdale) and Bonhomia (Medellin). The database gravitates toward $$-$$$ evening social dining spots across NYC, Atlanta, Medellin, Maine, Scottsdale and San Francisco.

## Fashion

### Style Thesis – Sensual Streetwear Architecture

"Dress the way a poem reads. There is rhythm, there is texture, there is something underneath the surface that rewards closer attention."

The fusion of two forces most men treat as opposites: streetwear architecture – proportion play, oversized silhouettes, bold sneakers, workwear layers – and sensual poetry – fabric intentionality, drape, movement, restraint, the magnetic pull of clothes that invite closer attention. The Italian principle of sprezzatura: deep intention that reads as effortless ease.

Seduction principle: it is about what you withhold, not what you display. Restraint separates someone who looks good from someone who is captivating.

### The Three Conversations

Every outfit operates on three simultaneous levels:

- Silhouette (street and architectural): proportion play, oversized against slim, structured against relaxed
- Texture (sensual and poetic): knits against wovens, matte against sheen, rough against smooth
- Detail (seductive): the chain at the collar, the ring catching light, the rolled cuff revealing the wrist

### Style References

- A$AP Rocky: layering precision, mixing high and low, oversized proportions that still feel intentional
- Pharrell: playful eclecticism, unexpected accessories, streetwear merged with couture
- Kanye West: brutalist minimalism, architectural silhouettes, neutral earth-tone palettes
- Bad Bunny: sensual fabrics, relaxed flowing pieces, colour confidence, gender-fluid energy
- Colman Domingo: rich quiet elegance, luxurious fabrics, statement pieces worn with restraint
- Kurt Cobain: anti-style as style, grunge texture, thrifted layering, effortless nonchalance
- Tyrod Taylor: clean athletic tailoring, fitted pieces that respect the physique without shouting
- Gustavo Piers Milton: editorial risk-taking, avant-garde shapes, fashion-forward proportion play
- Alexander Nguyen: minimalist refinement, texture-driven simplicity, quiet luxury sensibility

### Build and Silhouette Principles

5'7"-8", 165 lbs. Classic mesomorph. Broad shoulders, narrow waist, visible V-taper. Locs add vertical energy and texture. The shoulder-to-waist ratio is the superpower.

- Oversized must be strategic: shoulder seam on or near the actual shoulder, body drapes. Controlled volume, not drowning.
- Wide-leg and relaxed trousers balance the upper body and read taller. Straight leg to relaxed wide is the range.
- Avoid skinny fits entirely.
- Fitted pieces (heavyweight tees, ribbed knits, slim henleys) do heavy lifting on casual days.
- Cropped trouser lengths add visual height.
- All-fitted everything reads as trying too hard. All-formless hides the V-taper. Both miss.

### Colour Architecture

- Foundation (70%): cream/ivory/off-white (the power neutral), black (accent not default), charcoal, chocolate/dark brown, olive/military green, camel/tan
- Accent (20%): burgundy/wine, terracotta/burnt orange, sage green, slate blue/dusty blue, saffron/mustard. One accent piece per outfit maximum.
- Statement (10%): cobalt blue, burnt orange/rust, deep purple/plum. Used sparingly and intentionally. One piece at a time.
- Maximum three colour families per outfit. Two to three is the sweet spot.
- Gold-tone jewelry always. Skin tone with gold is a cheat code.

### Fabric Philosophy

Fabric is the centerpiece, not a detail. Prioritize fabric quality over brand name.

Warm weather: linen, silk and silk blends (camp collar shirts are the ultimate sensual streetwear piece), lightweight cotton (poplin, voile), modal and Tencel blends, open-weave knits.

Cool weather: wool crepe (flowing, not stiff), cashmere and blends, suede, brushed cotton/moleskin, soft leather (lamb, goat – buttery, not stiff), heavyweight cotton (220+ GSM).

Texture pairing: matte against sheen, rough against smooth, knit against woven, structured against draped. Every outfit needs at least two contrasting textures.

Avoid: cheap polyester, stiff rigid fabrics as default, athletic fabrics outside the gym, over-ironing. A slight natural rumple is sprezzatura.

### The Outfit Point System

A complexity budget for every outfit. Forces intentionality.

- Every standard article = 1 point (tee, pants, shoes, socks, belt)
- Every accessory = 1 point (ring, necklace, bracelet, sunglasses, hat, watch)
- Statement pieces = 2 points (bold pattern, unusual texture, eye-catching colour)
- Earrings = 2 points
- Fragrance = 1 point
- Maximum per item = 2 points. Nothing scores higher.
- Target range: 8 to 10 points. Below 8 risks underdevelopment. Above 10, reassess. Two 2-point pieces is the absolute ceiling per outfit, and they must occupy different visual zones.

### Outfit Formulas

Repeatable templates, not rigid uniforms. Chord progressions to improvise over.

- The Tuesday (casual everyday): heavyweight tee + wide-leg trouser + linen overshirt unbuttoned + clean sneaker + gold chain
- The Friday Night (social/date): silk camp collar shirt (one button open) + black denim, slightly cropped + leather jacket + suede loafer, no socks + ring and chain
- The Client Meeting (professional creative): deconstructed blazer + knit polo or turtleneck + wide-leg wool trouser + Chelsea boot or loafer + watch
- The Travel Fit (airport/transit): heavyweight black tee + relaxed drawstring trouser in quality fabric + knit cardigan or bomber + clean sneaker + sunglasses
- The Statement (event/gallery): cashmere crewneck in saffron or burgundy + wide-leg wool trouser + camel overcoat open + leather boot + rings, chain, watch
- The Summer Heat (ATL peak): ribbed tank + wide-leg linen trouser + suede loafer or Vans + sunglasses + single gold chain
- The Layered Poet (peak thesis): silk-blend tee + open knit cardigan + wide-leg trouser + Chelsea boot + scarf draped + rings and chain

### Ethos

Quality persists from basics and underwear through to fragrance. Whether gym shorts or a double-breasted suit, the consideration is the same. Polyester is avoided whenever possible – preference for organic, sustainable options. Basics like socks are replaced at hygienically appropriate intervals. The attention to detail does not fluctuate with the formality of the occasion.

### Accessories

Simple watches, bracelets, handbags. With locs, hats are considered carefully for silhouette – beanies, bucket hats and cowboy hats work. Dad hats can get bulky with locs. Two to three pieces maximum per outfit. Each one earns its place. Accessories amplify, not rescue.

### Collections

Coffee mugs, books, currency, scarves.

## Interior Design and Space

### The Compound Vision

Not a house – a compound. A self-contained world with zones for creation (recording studio), restoration (sauna and spa), intellectual life (floor-to-ceiling library), play (gaming room) and security (contingency space). The bedroom is a sanctuary – dark, textured, moody. Low-profile Japanese-style platform bed, velvet and corduroy textures, vinyl on the walls.

### The 80/20 Duality Principle

The ratio between raw and refined. Not fixed to a specific material palette. Brutalist shell (20%) filled with warmth (80%). Or warm space punctuated with industrial edge (20%). A loft filled with heavy rugs, lighting, textiles and brighter colors. A warm Mediterranean room with concrete and steel accents.

The Virgil Abloh principle: pay attention to the space a thing occupies with as much rigor as the thing itself – arguably more.

### Spatial Palette Default

Dark luxury. Not bright-and-airy. Minimal-dark-warm: black, charcoal, dark wood, warm neutrals as the foundation. Burnt orange and emerald green as the primary accents. Confirmed across every interior board – the bedroom, the living space, the kitchen.

### Sauna and Wellness

A dedicated home sauna is non-negotiable. Infrared with red light therapy. Glass door, natural wood interior, minimalist design. The wellness space as architecture, not an afterthought.

### Architectural Styles

- Lofts as primary gravitational pull
- Organic Japanese modern
- Traditional Mediterranean
- New England coastal (New Hampshire – "coastal done correctly, not cookie-cutter")
- Modern farmhouse exterior with masculine industrial interior
- Art Deco: respected when executed well but not a default

### Interior Design Quiz Results

- Havenly: Midcentury Modern (substyles: Industrial, Modern, Scandinavian)
- Modsy: Industrial Visionary (Industrial + Minimalist + Transitional)
- Modsy: Inviting Industrial (Industrial + Transitional + Mid-Century Modern)

### Pierce and Ward

Constrained maximalism. Their work earns respect because they achieve sprezzatura at a high object count. The principle is not minimalism. The principle is intention per square foot. When intention density stays high, the object count can rise without tripping the gaudiness wire.

### Lighting

Light is a material, not a utility. Lamps and natural light over overhead lighting, always. Candlelight is comfort. Shadow is an element incorporated into the whole, not the focal point unless tastefully meant to be. Kanye West's Sunday Service era as reference for how light can be treated as a material. The specificity matters: floor lamp vs. table lamp vs. console lamp on a stack of coffee table books. Each has its right moment.

### Colors in Space

Warm earth tones. Rich jewel tones: maroons, plums, mustard yellows, burnt oranges. Neutrals. Playing with light and the contrast between shadow and warmth.

### Materials

Natural woods, velvet, silk, bamboo, cottons, concrete, stainless steel, cashmere, linen, french terry, high-quality ceramics. The highest quality of all. Quality reflects the level of appreciation brought to everything.

### Greenery

No such thing as too much green. Plants are co-inhabitants, not decorations. Automating systems to care for them. People and pets will freely roam the space. One of the best additions to any lived-in or frequently visited space.

### The Hotel Room Three-Check

The first three things noticed that signal "this was designed by someone who understands":
1. Window orientation and how much light enters
2. The views – are they the real views of that side of the building
3. Treatment of details people take for granted: TV placement relative to bed, documentation and Wi-Fi presentation, towel details, overall cleanliness as evidence of care

Then: wall treatments, window treatments, brushed brass vs. stainless steel vs. chrome against plush rugs, natural woods, fresh air through a window. Variables that create something significantly greater than the sum of the parts.

### Ideal Bathroom

Heated high-quality tile floors. Warm and considered, not clinical. Brass fixtures. Trailing plants. Products like Aesop integrated into the design. Natural light. Clean.

### Art in the Home

Photography, mixed media, abstract, figurative, figurines, sculptures of varying sizes, decor, planters. Original art and prints, plus self-made work. Making art is a priority.

### Ideal Creative Environment

A rainy day. Surrounded by greenery. Quiet inside, alive outside. Window open to rain. Incense or candle. Music through speaker or headphones. Cozy lamp lighting the work area. The view frames the workstation. Flooded with natural light even on gray days. Efficiently laid out to switch between workflows and mediums. Clean, organised, aesthetically designed – colour palette, textures, quality of every surface. All considered.

## Social and Interpersonal

### What Makes Someone Interesting

A comfort with themselves that is evident in how they carry themselves. A uniqueness that is not flamboyant and does not require the other person's attention. They seem to be enjoying themselves and enjoying life. However that manifests, it reads as cool.

### What Makes Someone Uninteresting

Behavior that reads as a desperate attempt to obtain the attention that interesting people do not need. However that desperation manifests, it is equally off-putting.

### Environments That Energize

Game nights, birthday parties, intimate gatherings, wedding receptions, graduation parties – spaces where the environment is casual and individuality is welcome. Dinner parties. Communal experiences like the World Cup, the World Baseball Classic, the Amazing Race. Spaces where guards are down and conversations are genuine.

### Environments That Drain

Stiff formal gatherings where etiquette is enforced and individuality is discouraged. Large business conferences and non-intimate networking settings. Transactional energy that requires more effort to communicate genuine interest.

### How Time Is Best Spent

Intimate, quality, one-on-one time with romantic partners, family and friends. Sharing space and experiencing them. The aspiration is to bring loved ones into creative and experiential worlds and share the benefit of experiencing them together.

### Solo Engagement

Games that challenge: Jeopardy, Scrabble, Chess. Chess is being reintroduced into social life.

Gaudiness. Opulence is welcome – gaudy opulence is not. Avant-garde is welcome. The line is taste.

### Concrete Examples

- Flavor Flav's clock
- Mr. T's jewelry
- Heavy drinking culture of Europe
- Memphis design era

### Social Behaviors

- Arrogance
- Unnecessary aggression
- Lack of grace
- Rudeness
- Blatant disrespect
- Blatant abuses of power

### Trends Actively Rejected

- Corporate Memphis illustration – soulless by design
- Dopamine decor – maximalism without intention
- Hustle culture content – performative suffering of entrepreneurship
- Content about being a content creator – the ouroboros of influence
- Nostalgia mining – recycling decades without adding anything new
- "Main character energy" as a personality – performance of individuality rather than possession of it
- Performative vulnerability – strategic deployment of "realness"
- Biohacking and optimization culture – quantifying every human function misses the point
- Performative minimalism – minimalism as spectacle
- Digital nomad culture as identity – the content, not the lifestyle
- The "soft life" trend – rejecting ambition dressed as wisdom
- Commodified "Japandi" – reducing depth to a hashtag
- AI-generated art celebrated as equivalent to crafted work

### Quiet Luxury – Nuanced

Aspects are appreciated. The trend version that strips out all personality in favour of "tasteful" blandness is boring. Will not forsake identity for the right to feel welcome in a bland space.

### "Trying Too Hard" Signals

In a space: every surface styled but nothing looks lived-in, assembled from a single Pinterest board rather than lived experience. Visible brand names on furniture or decor as a flex.

In a person: name-dropping as credentials rather than genuine experience. References that feel rehearsed. Performative authenticity. Explaining why you are interesting rather than simply being interesting.

In content: explaining why something is cool instead of presenting it. Overproduced with no message underneath. Using cultural references as decoration rather than genuine touchpoints – this is not trying too hard, it is lazy.

## Aesthetic Sensibility

The aesthetic principles that govern taste across all domains.

### The DNA

Two wells: Atlanta hip-hop – Andre 3000's philosophical elegance, Future's unbothered confidence, Young Thug's rule-bending – and U.K. culture – sharpness, brevity, layered meaning packed into small spaces. This does not manifest as quotation or name-dropping. It surfaces in construction – a double meaning, slang deployed with surgical precision into an otherwise composed sentence, a reference that lands for those paying attention and disappears for those who are not.

### Design Principles

- Quality over quantity – always
- Evidence of care over evidence of cost
- Restraint that amplifies rather than diminishes
- Natural materials over synthetic
- Clean over cluttered
- Warm over sterile
- Intentional over accidental
- Depth over surface
- Intention per square foot over minimalism as dogma
- Deliberate density: everything present earns its place, nothing by default. Not maximalist, not minimalist – the right amount of meaningful weight
- Dark-warm as the spatial default, not bright-airy
- The system behind the thing matters as much as the thing (frameworks, structure, architecture of taste, not just the surface)

### The Cross-Domain Grammar

The reference library holds 173 creators, brands, works and curators, chosen independently across fashion, film, food, art, architecture and sound. Read as one corpus, a small set of formulas recurs in domains that share nothing else. That recurrence is the proof – these are not the conventions of any single field but the laws beneath the taste itself. Each is written as an instruction, not an observation. They are the empirical spine under the Design Principles above.

**The world, not the object.** Build an environment the audience enters, not an artefact they observe. The garment is the door into a city (Aimé Leon Dore, Ralph Lauren), the album is a place you live inside for its duration (Astroworld, Renaissance), the building competes with what it holds (the Guggenheim, Tate Modern), the spice arrives inside a whole world (Buly 1803, Scribe, Singita). The most universal pattern in the corpus, present in every slice – the third force of the Throughline made into method.

**Withhold to amplify, constrain to clarify.** Two faces of one law. What you leave out raises the signal on everything that remains – anonymity as luxury (Lemaire, Goyard), absence doing the work (Frank Ocean, Good Will Hunting), pleasure led and virtue kept quiet (Salt and Stone). And the hard limit forces the decisive move – seven slabs on a 2.9-metre frontage (Building Frame of the House), two ingredients with nothing to hide behind (Dick Taylor), a stencil that reads in a glance (Banksy). Restraint is the most saturated tag in the library, and not by accident.

**Collapse the hierarchy, choose the low end with equal rigour.** High and low belong on the same floor with no apology (Dover Street Market, the Met, A$AP Rocky, Warhol). The discipline is that the cheap thing is selected as carefully as the dear one – judge the plate, not the postcode (Bourdain); total credibility earned at the bottom of the price ladder (Vans, Nike). Selectivity is the whole game. Randomness reads as noise.

**Heritage as a platform, not a costume.** Inherit at the source's level of seriousness and carry it forward – it does not name-drop house music, it is house music (Renaissance); the codes handed to the most credible cultural directors (Louis Vuitton); the apothecary revived down to the ritual of the counter (Buly 1803, Santa Maria Novella); the industrial shell kept visible and filled with new life (Tate Modern, the brownstone). The failure mode is named in the corpus too – performed heritage outrunning the substance (Mast Brothers).

**Begin at the material.** Meaning starts at the fibre, the hide, the origin – before the cut, before the label. Develop the cloth from scratch (Auralee, Loewe, Dries Van Noten); leave the concrete board-marked and honest (Kalach, Building Frame of the House); name the farm and the harvest (Burlap and Barrel, Diaspora Co., Kettl). Traceability is itself the luxury signal, and the maker's prior craft – the woodworker turned chocolatier, the clarinetist turned roaster – is the proof of intent.

**Precision disguised as ease.** Drill the craft until the discipline is invisible and only the joy shows. Sprezzatura is not a fashion tic – it is a cross-domain law: the starch taken out of the tailoring (Drake's, Isabel Marant), the record engineered to the inch that sounds like pure release (Renaissance, Beyoncé), the camera moved like a pulse that reads as natural (Scorsese, Chazelle). The effort is concealed by design, never by accident.

**The frame is content, the structure is the argument.** Where a thing sits and how it is built carry meaning before the content speaks. The medium is the moment (Banksy); the form enacts the thesis so the reader feels the weight accumulate (Homegoing); style is the argument, not a coat of paint over it (Tarantino, De Palma); the space a thing occupies treated with as much rigour as the thing (the Virgil Abloh principle, already load-bearing here). Placement and form are decisions, never defaults.

**Synthesis from between two worlds.** The position between cultures – belonging fully to neither – is the engine, not the wound. Diaspora as creative force (Marcus Samuelsson, Eric Adjepong, Diaspora Co.); the hyphen used as fuel (Selena, Fresh Prince of Bel-Air, Everything Everywhere All at Once). This is the Throughline's own two wells – Atlanta and the U.K. – named as a working method. Displacement produces a third, original thing.

**Ritual over transaction.** Sustained, sincere engagement compounds where the single encounter cannot. The café as daily rite, not a cleanse gimmick (Kreation); the one allegiance carried without irony (Liverpool FC); the thousand episodes that ask for trust before they repay it (One Piece); the ritual of the beautiful drink mattering more than the drink. The retainer, the newsletter, the standing practice – all of a piece.

One pattern sits underneath the rest: the proxy filter. Trust a selector already calibrated to the same frequency and the first pass is done for you – the buying is the taste at Mr Porter and Dover Street Market, and what cleared the Louvre or MoMA cleared the longer filter of history. The reference library is that principle turned on the self – a portfolio of trusted references so the next creative decision never starts from a blank page. Naming its recurring formulas is what turns the collection into an instrument.
