# Crate and Barrel

**What** – The lifestyle retailer founded December 1962 by Gordon and Carole Segal, both 23, in 1,700 square feet in Chicago's Old Town – the store that introduced affordable, design-forward European housewares to the mass American audience. The name is literal: merchandise displayed on the shipping crates and barrels it arrived in. Gordon ran it as CEO until 2008; the store was briefly managed by a young Harrison Ford. Legally Euromarket Designs Inc., majority-owned by Germany's Otto Group since 1998 and wholly since 2011; over 100 stores today, with CB2 as the modern sister concept.

**Why aligned** – Design democratization done with conviction. The Segals saw well-designed, honestly priced European goods on their honeymoon and built the machine that put them in ordinary American kitchens – taste scaled without being flattened. The founding display move is a standing merchandising lesson: the packaging as the fixture, the logistics made visible and charming.

**The proposition** – Bring good design to the many and let the presentation carry the story. Curate from craft sources the customer cannot reach; price for the household, not the collector; make the store itself the argument. Six decades on, the crate-display origin remains the brand's best idea – authenticity as a set design that happened to be true.

**Canon** – the 1962 Old Town opening; the crates-and-barrels display as founding merchandising; the Otto Group transition (1998, 2011); CB2.

**Pull for** – merchandising, design-democratization, heritage, retail-theatre, accessible.
