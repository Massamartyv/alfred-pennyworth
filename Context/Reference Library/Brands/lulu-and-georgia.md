# Lulu and Georgia

**What** – The Los Angeles home decor house founded 2012 by Sara Sugarman, named for her grandfather Lou and father George – a third act in a family that has designed houses and hotels since 1955. Furniture, rugs and decor built on exclusive designer collaborations, curated collections and vintage sourcing, under the banner "bring beauty to your home."

**Why aligned** – Lineage worn as proposition. The brand is not a startup story but an inheritance – taste transmitted through a family business and given an e-commerce body – and the naming itself encodes it. The model matters too: the curated edit plus the designer collaboration, taste as the product rather than the catalogue.

**The proposition** – Curate from a lineage, not a trend feed. The name honors the source of the eye; the collections keep the eye visible – globally sourced, artisan-made, spanning bohemian to midcentury without losing coherence; the vintage program roots the new pieces in provenance. The Tallulah Collection, drawn from the grandfather's antique rugs, is the thesis in one product line: the family archive as living inventory.

**Canon** – the Tallulah Collection; the exclusive designer collaborations; the 1955-to-present family design lineage the brand is built on.

**Pull for** – curated-decor, lineage, designer-collaboration, merchandising, warmth.
