# Motown

**What** – Berry Gordy's hit factory, founded January 12, 1959 on an $800 family loan, headquartered at Hitsville U.S.A., 2648 West Grand Boulevard, Detroit – the house where an assembly-line system for making records produced more than 110 Top Ten hits between 1961 and 1971. Now a Universal Music Group label, returned to standalone status in 2021. Sylvia Rhone – carded in this library – ran the Motown/Universal Motown era from 2004 to 2011.

**Why aligned** – Systems as creativity. Motown is the standing proof that process and soul are not enemies: Gordy borrowed the automobile assembly line's discipline and produced the most emotionally durable pop catalog in American music. For a portfolio built on the conviction that systems beat one-off solutions, this is the founding case study – creative output industrialized without being flattened.

**The proposition** – Build the factory, not just the hits. A repeatable production system, ruthless internal quality control and deliberate artist development turned a family loan into a culture-defining institution – and made Black excellence a mainstream American export on its own terms. The extraction for any venture: the system is the asset; the hits are its evidence.

**Canon** – Hitsville U.S.A.; the $800 founding loan (1959); the 110-plus Top Ten hits of 1961-1971; the 2021 return to standalone status.
