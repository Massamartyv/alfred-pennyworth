# Def Jam Recordings

**What** – The label that institutionalized hip-hop: co-founded 1984 by Rick Rubin, then an NYU student running it from his Weinstein Hall dorm room, with Russell Simmons. First releases were LL Cool J's "I Need a Beat" and the Beastie Boys' "Rock Hard"; a Columbia/CBS distribution deal followed in 1985. Jay-Z became its defining 2000s artist and later its president. Now a Universal Music Group label under chairman-CEO Tunji Balogun (2022), repositioned toward the next generation of global Black music with signings like Muni Long and Coco Jones.

**Why aligned** – The proof that a culture-defining institution can start in a dorm room with a sound and a conviction. Def Jam did not sign into an existing market; it built the market's institutional spine, era after era – from Rubin's drum machines to Jay-Z's presidency to the current global-Black-music repositioning. Reinvention without losing the name's meaning.

**The proposition** – Own the culture, not just the catalog. The label's durability comes from treating hip-hop as a civilization to be built rather than a genre to be exploited – artist development as institution-building, the roster as an argument about where the culture goes next. Each era hands the keys to someone who embodies the moment.

**Canon** – the Weinstein Hall dorm founding (1984); "I Need a Beat" and "Rock Hard" as the first releases; the Jay-Z era, artist to president; the Balogun-era global repositioning.

**Pull for** – culture-making, artist-development, institution-building, reinvention, positioning.
