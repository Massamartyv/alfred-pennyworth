# Brutalist

**What** – The postwar movement of raw concrete and exposed structure – the name from béton brut, raw concrete – running roughly from the 1950s into the 1970s. Massive, sculptural, monumental, the material left unfinished and the construction logic made the whole expression. Reyner Banham gave it its critical frame as the New Brutalism.

**Why aligned** – The 20% in the duality principle made literal – "brutalist shell filled with warmth." It also surfaces in the fashion register as Kanye's brutalist minimalism, architectural silhouettes and neutral earth tones. The pull is the honest, sculptural mass, never the cold institutional grey it gets reduced to.

**The filter** – Raw concrete as truth, mass as form, structure left exposed and sculptural. Read it correctly only when warmed – board-marked concrete against wood, textile, plants and lamplight, the 20/80 ratio holding. Played cold and whole, it becomes the bunker cliché the taste rejects.

**Canon** – Le Corbusier's Unité d'Habitation, Marseille (1952); the Barbican Estate, London; the National Theatre, London; Boston City Hall; Safdie's Habitat 67, Montreal. Board-marked concrete, deep shadow, monumental cantilevers.

**Pull for** – materiality, raw-and-warm, sculptural-mass, structural-honesty, monumental.

**Trend read** – A strong revival across design – "brutalist" furniture, type and interiors – though much of the trend is surface styling. The enduring value is the material honesty, not the look. Pending enrichment for current-cycle signal.
