# Sylvia Rhone

**Who** – The record executive whose career is a study in curation as power: Wharton economics graduate (1974) who entered at Buddah Records as a secretary the same year, rose through Atlantic's Black music division, and then ran labels for three decades – EastWest (chairwoman-CEO, 1990), Elektra (1994-2002, "the most powerful woman in the music business" per the Los Angeles Times), Motown/Universal Motown (2004-2011) and Epic (president 2014, chairman-CEO 2019, stepped down September 2025). Her own framing of the milestone is the accurate one: Epic was "the third time in my career that I've been the first woman and first Black person to serve as CEO of a major record label owned by a Fortune 500 company."

**Why aligned** – The executive as cultural facilitator. Rhone's authority was never a genre lane – Missy Elliott and Metallica sat on the same desk – and she spent a career refusing the industry pattern that confined Black executives to Black acts. Her 2026 line is the thesis: "We make the algorithm, the algorithm doesn't make us." Taste asserted over the feed – the conviction this entire library is built on.

**The filter** – Anti-siloed A&R. Genre categories ("what's pop, what's hip-hop, what's rock") matter less than the actual fan; the executive's job is to identify, develop and guide across the whole map. The rosters carry the proof: En Vogue, Gerald Levert, Pantera and AC/DC at EastWest; Missy Elliott, Busta Rhymes, Tracy Chapman, Metallica, Third Eye Blind and Jason Mraz at Elektra; Akon, India.Arie, Erykah Badu and Lil Wayne in the Motown years; Future, 21 Savage, Tyla, Giveon and Andre 3000 sustained and expanded in the Epic era. Pull her when the question is how a curator holds range without losing identity.

**Canon** – the triple first (EastWest 1990, Elektra 1994, Epic 2019); the Elektra era at the peak of her industry power; Billboard Women in Music Executive of the Year (2023); Berklee honorary doctorate (2019); the BET Ultimate Icon Award (June 2026).
