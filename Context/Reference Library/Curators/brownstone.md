# Brownstone

**What** – Not a movement but a building typology and urban vernacular: the mid-19th-century rowhouse faced in brown Triassic-Jurassic sandstone, dense in New York – Brooklyn, Harlem, the West Village – and in Boston. Stoops, parlor floors, high ceilings, bay windows and cornices, dressed in Italianate, Romanesque Revival and Greek Revival skins.

**Why aligned** – The historical urban fabric the travel instinct keeps reaching for – "historical parts of town," the steady pull toward New York and its older neighborhoods. The brownstone is craft, patina and street life built into the typology itself, the stoop as social architecture.

**The filter** – Read it as typology, not pastiche. The grammar is the row, the stoop, the vertical parlor-to-garden section and the warm sandstone street wall. Honor the period bones – plaster moldings, original parquet, marble mantels – and layer dark-warm modern life inside rather than gutting the character out.

**Canon** – Brooklyn's brownstone belts – Park Slope, Bedford-Stuyvesant, Fort Greene; Harlem's striver's rows; Boston's South End. Italianate stoops and bracketed cornices; the parlor-floor piano nobile; original sandstone facades.

**Pull for** – historical-fabric, urban-vernacular, materiality, warmth, rowhouse-typology.

**Trend read** – Restoration over reinvention is the dominant current mode – period brownstones renovated with contemporary, often dark-warm, interiors behind preserved facades. Pending enrichment for current-cycle signal.
