# Speakeasy

**What** – The interior aesthetic of concealment and earned entry, born as literal hidden bars during American Prohibition (1920-1933) and surviving as a design language: Art Deco and Bauhaus blended under an Edwardian opulence. Its defining move is architectural secrecy – the bookshelf that swings open, the phone booth that is a door, the unmarked entrance inside another business.

**Why aligned** – Secret rooms sit in the operator's own dream-home programme, and this is the style that theorizes them. The draw is intimacy engineered through concealment – a room that rewards knowing, where dim light and dense material make the space feel confided rather than displayed. World-building at residential scale.

**The filter** – Hidden or disguised entry as the threshold ritual; low, warm, low-contrast light; dark wood, exposed brick, leather and plush textile; period furnishing and craft-cocktail codes carrying the era without costume. The current migration matters: the vocabulary has left the bar and moved into the house, as concealed home-bar rooms and hidden lounges (live in 2024-2026 residential coverage). Pull it for any space meant to be discovered rather than presented.

**Canon** – the Prohibition-era hidden bar as the founding typology; the Art Deco-Edwardian material blend; the contemporary residential home-bar migration.

**Pull for** – concealment, moody-intimacy, ritual, world-building, hospitality.
