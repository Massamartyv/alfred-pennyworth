# Ambient Lighting

**What** – The base layer in the three-layer model that governs modern lighting design – ambient for overall illumination, task for work, accent for drama. The retail framework has no named coiner, but its documented ancestor is lighting designer Richard Kelly (1910-1977), who delivered the founding tripartite theory in a 1952 Cleveland lecture: ambient luminescence ("the uninterrupted light of a snowy morning in open country"), focal glow ("the pool of light at your favorite reading chair") and play of brilliants (crystal chandeliers, Times Square at night). Kelly lit for Philip Johnson, Louis Kahn and Eero Saarinen and is credited as the founder of the architectural lighting profession.

**Why aligned** – Creative-director.md states the doctrine outright: light is a material, not a utility. Lamps and natural light over overhead lighting, always; candlelight as comfort; shadow incorporated as an element; Kanye West's Sunday Service era as the reference for light treated as material. Kelly's framework is that doctrine given professional grammar.

**The filter** – Build in layers and name each layer's job. Ambient sets the room's emotional temperature – in this house it comes from lamps, natural light and flame, never a single overhead source; task light earns its focus; accent is the play of brilliants, used sparingly. The extraction beneath the framework: shadow is part of the composition, and the specificity matters – floor lamp, table lamp, console lamp on a stack of books each have their right moment.

**Canon** – Kelly's 1952 lecture and three tenets; his collaborations with Johnson, Kahn and Saarinen; the ambient-task-accent model as the standing industry descendant.
