# Tropical Modernism

**What** – The mid-20th-century movement that took modernist principles into tropical climates and let the climate redesign them, emerging amid postwar modernization and decolonization across the Global South. Its pioneer is Geoffrey Bawa (1919-2003), Sri Lanka's most iconic architect, who began by emulating Le Corbusier and ended by folding the International Style into Ceylonese materials, monsoon logic and vernacular tradition.

**Why aligned** – The style that solves the compound's favorite problem: how a building opens to nature without surrendering form. Verandas, courtyards and water features are not decoration here – they are the cooling system. It is nature-integration with an engineering argument, the same instinct Fallingwater and the biophilic references carry, executed at the scale of climate.

**The filter** – Local materials – timber, stone, terracotta; plans kept fluid for light and cross-ventilation; deep overhangs, shaded walkways, courtyards as rooms; orientation and water doing the work of machinery; pitched roofs and wide eaves worn as modernist form. The deepest move is humility toward site – Kandalama is built to disappear into its mountainside. Pull it whenever indoor-outdoor living needs rigor instead of romance.

**Canon** – Lunuganga Estate, Bawa's own garden residence near Bentota; the Kandalama Heritance Hotel (1994), absorbed into the rock face; the Sri Lanka Parliament Building; Seema Malaka Temple.

**Pull for** – indoor-outdoor, climate-responsive, passive-cooling, landscape-integration, materiality.
