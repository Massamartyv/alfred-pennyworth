# Outdoor Showers

**What** – The open-air bathing fixture, with a deeper lineage than its beach-house image suggests: Greek and Roman public baths ran decorated outdoor facilities, William Feetham patented the hand-shower prototype in 1767, and the French military fielded the first rain-style showers around 1860. The direct residential ancestor is the Cape Cod and New England tradition – the sand-and-salt rinse before entering the summer cottage – since spread through the Hamptons and outward nationally.

**Why aligned** – Bathing returned to the elements. The operator's dream programme treats bathing as event – the courtyard bathtub, the shower with a commanding view – and the outdoor shower is the humblest, oldest version of the same conviction: water, air and sky make an ordinary ritual ceremonial.

**The filter** – The arc worth extracting is functional-to-amenity: what began as a rinse station is now designed space – built-in benches, towel hooks, privacy screening, upscale material treatment. The Cape origin holds the honest core: a threshold ritual marking the passage from outside to in. Pull it for bathing-as-experience, coastal living and any feature where practicality can be elevated without losing its plainness.

**Canon** – the Greek and Roman bath precedent; Feetham's 1767 patent; the French military rain shower (c. 1860); the Cape Cod sand-rinse tradition and its Hamptons elaboration.

**Pull for** – open-air-bathing, ritual, coastal, elemental, threshold.
