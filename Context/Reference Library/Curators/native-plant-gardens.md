# Native Plant Gardens

**What** – The habitat-driven gardening movement that plants for ecological function: species native to a region over ornamental exotics, on the premise that local insect and bird life evolved alongside them and depends on them. Its leading figure is entomologist Douglas Tallamy (University of Delaware), author of *Bringing Nature Home* and *Nature's Best Hope* and co-founder of Homegrown National Park – the project to convert tens of millions of acres of American lawn into connected wildlife habitat. Tallamy's research holds that native-genus plants provide on average 68% more insect food than introduced species.

**Why aligned** – The garden entry in the operator's dream-home programme, given a philosophy worth building on: the landscape as a living system rather than a green surface. It is the outdoor sibling of the biophilic thread – nature integrated for function, with beauty as the by-product of ecological honesty.

**The filter** – Plant what the local food web can actually eat; remove invasives; sequence natives for year-round pollinator access; treat the lawn as convertible acreage rather than default ground. The deeper extraction is the reframe – a yard judged by what it sustains, not just how it reads. Currency is strong and rising: 2025-2026 mainstream coverage (NPR, Smithsonian) treats this as an expanding movement.

**Canon** – Tallamy's *Bringing Nature Home* and Homegrown National Park; Wild Ones (founded 1977 after a Lorrie Otto lecture, 91 chapters by 2024); the Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center (founded 1982, now the state botanical garden of Texas).
