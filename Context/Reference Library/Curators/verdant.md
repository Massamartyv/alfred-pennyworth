# Verdant

**What** – The current decor-content term (live 2025-2026: Decorilla's "Verdant Living," DecoFond's verdant trend coverage) for green-saturated, plant-dense interiors – the living room as "a genuine extension of the garden." Named honestly: it has no founding designer, no critical-press pedigree and no canonical project; one source in the space states outright it is a rebrand of biophilic design. The look is real even though the label is marketing.

**Why aligned** – Indoor greenery and indoor trees sit in the operator's own dream-home programme; this card names the aesthetic register of that instinct. Where Biophilic (carded separately) carries the theory – Fromm's 1973 coinage, E.O. Wilson's 1984 hypothesis, Stephen Kellert's applied principles – Verdant is the surface expression: how the green-saturated room actually looks.

**The filter** – Plant density as composition, not accessory – layered potted arrangements, vertical and hydroponic planting, wall gardens, terrariums; plant-derived color from sage through moss to deep leaf; natural surfaces (clay, limewash) echoing outdoor texture. Use the surface-versus-philosophy distinction as the working frame: pull Verdant for the look, pull Biophilic for the reasoning – and hold that the sources themselves mostly flatten the two together. Pantone's Greenery (2017 Color of the Year) is the earlier, formally named moment in the same territory – lineage context, not origin.

**Canon** – none industry-recognized; the register is defined by current decor coverage rather than landmark projects. The theoretical canon belongs to Biophilic.
