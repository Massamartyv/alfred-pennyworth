# Smart Home

**What** – Residential technology as a design consideration. The lineage: X10, the first home-automation product, launched 1975 by Pico Electronics, controlling lamps and appliances over power-line signalling; the term "Smart House" coined 1984 by the National Association of Home Builders; the mainstream voice-assistant era opened by Amazon Echo (2014) and Google Home (2016). The 2026 design position is settled: intelligent comfort with the intelligence hidden – controls finished as architectural details in brass, steel and ceramic, adaptive lighting shifting by mood and hour.

**Why aligned** – Creative-director.md is explicit: no wires; technology should be as useful as possible and stay out of the way when not needed – the setup chosen to preserve flow in the workspace. The governing principle from current practice matches it exactly: exposed technology reads as dated, concealed technology ages well.

**The filter** – Technology earns its place by disappearing into service. Automate the ambient conditions – light, temperature, sound – so the room responds without being operated; finish the visible touchpoints as objects worthy of the interior; never let a gadget become decor. The dream-home programme's central vacuum and built-in dustpan belong to this register: infrastructure engineered into the architecture rather than appliances added to it.

**Canon** – X10 (1975); the NAHB "Smart House" coinage (1984); Amazon Echo (2014) and Google Home (2016) as the voice inflection; the concealed-over-visible principle of current practice.

**Pull for** – concealed-technology, integration, intelligent-comfort, restraint, engineering-of-ease.
