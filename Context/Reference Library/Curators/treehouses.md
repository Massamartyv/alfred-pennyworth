# Treehouses

**What** – The elevated dwelling built in and around living trees, matured from backyard play structure into a serious architectural and luxury-hospitality typology. Its defining modern figure is Pete Nelson – author of *Treehouses: The Art and Craft of Living Out on a Limb* (1994), founder of Nelson Treehouse and Supply, host of *Treehouse Masters* (2013-2018) and creator of Treehouse Point (2006), the treehouse-only retreat near Fall City, Washington.

**Why aligned** – Childhood wonder engineered to adult standard. The treehouse is the purest case of the dwelling surrendering to its site – the tree is the structure, the canopy is the room – and the hospitality tier proves wonder and luxury are not opposites.

**The filter** – Build with the tree, not on it; let height do the atmospheric work a designed interior usually labors for; keep the craft visible. The hospitality exemplars show the range of the register – from the Mirrorcube's reflective disappearance to Chewton Glen's full-service suites – all trading on the same asset: the feeling of being held somewhere impossible. Pull it for nature-immersion, elevated perspective and the engineering of delight.

**Canon** – Treehotel, Harads, Sweden, with the mirrored Mirrorcube; Chewton Glen, England, 14 luxury treehouse suites; Loire Valley Lodges, France, 18 lodges each designed by a different artist; Post Ranch Inn, Big Sur; Nelson's Treehouse Point.

**Pull for** – nature-immersion, elevated-perspective, hospitality, world-building, wonder.
