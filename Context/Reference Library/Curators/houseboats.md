# Houseboats

**What** – The floating-dwelling typology, carried by three distinct regional traditions. Kerala's kettuvallam – Malayalam kettu, tied with ropes, and vallam, boat – built nail-free for the 18th and 19th-century rice and spice trade and converted to touring houseboats in the 1990s. Amsterdam's woonboten, born of postwar housing shortage and now a regulated icon of the canal city. Sausalito's float homes, a 1960s-70s countercultural colony on Richardson Bay that still reads as painted non-conformity.

**Why aligned** – Dwelling as adaptation. Each tradition is the same move under different pressure – trade, shortage, counterculture – and each turned constraint into a vernacular with its own beauty. Water living is the dream-home instinct for water (the indoor pond, the bathing courtyard) taken to full commitment.

**The filter** – The rope-tied kettuvallam as craft logic – construction method as aesthetic; the woonboot as density solved with charm; Sausalito as identity expressed through dwelling. Pull the shared extraction: smallness offset by the borrowed vastness of the water, and the discipline that everything aboard must earn its place. A note held from the research: the "3000 BC" origin claim tourism sites attach to Kerala boat-building is unverified marketing – the load-bearing history is the trade era and the 1990s conversion.

**Canon** – the kettuvallam of the Kerala backwaters; Amsterdam's canal woonboten; the Sausalito float-home community.

**Pull for** – floating-dwelling, water-living, regional-vernacular, adaptive-reuse, smallness-done-well.
