# Virtual Reality

**What** – The medium of computer-generated, fully immersive environments a user enters as if present inside them. Its lineage runs from Ivan Sutherland and Bob Sproull's head-mounted display (1968), nicknamed "The Sword of Damocles" for its ceiling-suspended weight, through Jaron Lanier's VPL Research, which popularized the term "virtual reality" (around 1987), to the modern consumer revival led by the Oculus Rift, crowdfunded on Kickstarter in 2012 and acquired with its maker by Facebook in 2014.

**Why aligned** – The reference for presence as the ultimate design goal – the medium that replaces the frame with the room. VR is the purest expression of immersion: not depicting a world but placing the body inside one. It is the boundary condition for world-building and spatial direction, where atmosphere, scale and embodiment become the entire experience.

**The filter** – Design for presence, not for the screen. Read VR for what total immersion demands – a continuous world, believable scale, the body as the camera, comfort as a hard constraint. The discipline is environmental: every direction is spatial, and the goal is the felt sense of being somewhere, not the picture of it.

**Canon** – Sutherland's Sword of Damocles (1968); Lanier and VPL Research; the Oculus Rift as the consumer revival; immersion and presence as the organizing principles of the medium.

**Pull for** – immersive-atmosphere, presence, world-building, spatial-direction, embodiment.
