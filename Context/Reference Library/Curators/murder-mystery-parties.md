# Murder Mystery Parties

**What** – The participatory social format in which guests take on assigned characters and, over a dinner or party, play out and solve a staged murder. It descends from parlor games and the deduction logic of the board game Cluedo – devised by Anthony Pratt and first manufactured in 1949 – and was commercialized as boxed kits, notably Decipher's "How to Host a Murder" series in the 1980s.

**Why aligned** – The reference for turning guests into the entertainment. A murder mystery party is hospitality designed as a game: the host builds the frame – roles, secrets, a structure of reveals – and the guests supply the performance and the fun. It is the clearest small-scale model of designed participation, where the experience is co-authored by the people inside it.

**The filter** – Hand the audience their roles and let them perform the night. Read the format for participation design – assign clear characters, seed secrets and motives, structure the reveals, then step back. The host's craft is the scaffold, not the show; the magic is that the guests become the cast. Ritual, role and a controlled unfolding of information do the work.

**Canon** – the Cluedo deduction lineage (1949); Decipher's "How to Host a Murder" boxed kits of the 1980s; the dinner-party-as-game structure of roles, secrets and staged reveals.

**Pull for** – participation, role-play, ritual, hospitality, narrative.
