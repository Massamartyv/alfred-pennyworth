# Solariums

**What** – The all-glass room – glass walls and glass roof – built to maximize sunlight and unobstructed sky. Latin for "place of sunlight"; wealthy Romans glazed south-facing winter rooms (the heliocaminus, "solar furnace"), and early-20th-century hospitals used solariums as recovery wards for tuberculosis, pneumonia and pleurisy patients – a genuine medical lineage in which light was the treatment.

**Why aligned** – The sunroom sits in the operator's dream-home programme, and the solarium is that instinct at full strength: light not as a window's allowance but as the room's entire material. The healing lineage matters – this is a typology founded on the conviction that sunlight is physically restorative, the same premise the biophilic thread carries.

**The filter** – The distinctions are the discipline. A solarium is glass on every plane including the roof; a sunroom keeps a solid roof and generous windows; a conservatory is the same glazing wearing 16th-century-derived ornament – finials, ridge crests, Palladian arches – from its origin protecting explorers' citrus. Pull the solarium when the brief is total light capture and sky as ceiling; pull the sunroom when the room must stay a room; pull the conservatory when heritage decoration is the point.

**Canon** – the Roman heliocaminus; the early-20th-century hospital solarium wards; the modern residential all-glass addition as standard glazing-industry vocabulary.
