# West African Modern

**What** – A name that lives in two registers, and the card holds both honestly. In decor media, "West African Modern" names an interiors direction built on Ankara prints, Kente cloth, Adinkra symbols, mud cloth, carved wood and warm red-yellow-orange palettes. Critical design press does not use the label at all – ArchDaily and Dezeen organize this territory as Contemporary African Architecture, discussed through named practitioners rather than a shared style canon.

**Why aligned** – The bridge register: heritage carried forward as design authority rather than costume. The same diaspora thread that runs through Eric Adjepong, Marcus Samuelsson and Homegoing in this library, expressed in space – pattern and symbol systems with centuries of meaning, deployed with modernist discipline.

**The filter** – Two extractions, kept separate. From the decor register: textile as the load-bearing surface, symbol systems (Adinkra, Kente) as ornament with grammar, earth palettes with saturated accents. From the practitioner register: the real canon is individual bodies of work – David Adjaye's civic monuments, Kunlé Adeyemi's NLÉ and the Makoko Floating School, Papa Omotayo's MOE+ in Lagos, Dele Adeyemo's ecological practice. Do not treat the label as a settled movement with shared formal principles; treat it as a doorway to named practices.

**Canon** – Adjaye's National Museum of African American History and Culture, Washington; the National Cathedral of Ghana designs (2018); the Edo Museum of West African Art, Benin City; Adeyemi's Makoko Floating School, Lagos; Adjaye's Royal Gold Medal (2021), the first to an African architect.
