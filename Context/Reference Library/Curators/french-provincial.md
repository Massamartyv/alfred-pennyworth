# French Provincial

**What** – The style of the manor houses and farmhouses of the French countryside – Provence, Normandy, the Loire – and its later revivals. Symmetry, steep hipped roofs, natural stone and stucco, tall shuttered windows, and a refined rusticity that reads as old money rather than new.

**Why aligned** – An operator-added reference, not named in the aesthetic operating system. It earns its place on warmth, patina and craft – natural stone and aged wood, evidence of care over evidence of cost, rustic refinement rather than gloss. The same pull toward historical fabric and natural material that runs through the wider taste, in a softer, more pastoral register.

**The filter** – Refinement worn rustic. Honest natural materials – limestone, plaster, reclaimed oak, iron – arranged with quiet symmetry and allowed to age. Read it for the patina and the proportion, not for ornate "shabby chic" decoration. Restraint keeps it from tipping into the twee.

**Canon** – Provençal mas and bastide farmhouses; Norman manor houses; steep hipped roofs, dormers, limestone and lime render, shuttered casements, herringbone parquet, weathered terracotta.

**Pull for** – materiality, warmth, rustic-refinement, craftsmanship, patina.
