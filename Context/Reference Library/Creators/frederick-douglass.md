# Frederick Douglass

**Who** – Born enslaved in Talbot County, Maryland around 1818, escaped in 1838, and became the most consequential orator of the American nineteenth century. Three autobiographies across forty years, each rewritten as the man and the country changed. Founded and published *The North Star* from 1847. Died 1895.

**Why aligned** – Three reasons, and the third is the one almost nobody knows.

He owned the press. Abolitionist patrons wanted him to tell his story on their platforms in their framing, and were reportedly uncomfortable when his speech grew too polished to serve as evidence. He left and started his own paper. That is the entire argument for owned audience and owned catalogue, made in 1847, at considerably higher cost than it carries now.

He rewrote himself three times. *Narrative*, *My Bondage and My Freedom*, *Life and Times* – the same life told at 27, 37 and 63, each version reflecting what he had become rather than preserving what he had been. A body of work that revises itself in public.

And he understood the image. Douglass sat for more portraits than any other American of his century – by his own design, and he wrote and lectured on photography as a democratic technology. He grasped that the caricature was doing political work and that a photograph, dignified and unsmiling and endlessly reproducible, was the counter-argument. A man who theorised his own visual direction in the 1860s.

**The formula** – Own the means of distribution before you need them, because the moment you need them is the moment the terms turn against you. Let the self-authored account be the evidence and refuse to let anyone else hold the pen. Treat the image as argument rather than decoration and direct it deliberately. Revise the record as you grow rather than defending the earlier version out of consistency.

The rhetorical instrument itself: build the case on the audience's own stated principles and then close the gap between what they profess and what they permit. He almost never argues from outside their frame. He argues from inside it, which leaves no exit.

**Canon** – *Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass, an American Slave*, 1845. "What to the Slave Is the Fourth of July?", Rochester, 5 July 1852 – arguably the finest speech in American English. *The North Star*, from 1847. "Pictures and Progress", the photography lecture, delivered from the early 1860s. *Life and Times of Frederick Douglass*, 1881.
