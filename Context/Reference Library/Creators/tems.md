# Tems

**Who** – Temilade Openiyi – the name means "the crown is mine" in Yoruba – Nigerian singer-songwriter and producer, and the first female Nigerian artist to win a Grammy: Best Melodic Rap Performance (2023) for "Wait for U" with Future and Drake, followed by Best African Music Performance (2025) for "Love Me Jeje," the first Nigerian win in that category.

**Why aligned** – Understatement as power. Tems does not oversing; the production leaves negative space around a smoky lower register, and the restraint is the signature. She is also the model of the writer behind the hit – her pen on Rihanna's "Lift Me Up" alongside Ludwig Göransson and Ryan Coogler, her voice as the gravity of "Essence" – influence that compounds off her own catalog as well as on it.

**The formula** – Own the means of production and hold the register low. She self-produces or co-produces much of her catalog, keeps the vocal conversational where peers would belt, and lets the space do the emotional work. The arc proves the model: "Essence" (2020, her first Hot 100 entry at number nine), the Grammy features, then Born in the Wild (June 2024, RCA and her own Since '93 imprint) as the debut album that converted feature-fame into an authored body of work.

**Canon** – "Essence" with Wizkid; "Wait for U" and the 2023 Grammy; the "Lift Me Up" writing credit; Born in the Wild (2024) and the 2025 "Love Me Jeje" Grammy.

**Pull for** – restraint, negative-space, self-production, voice-and-tone, cultural-bridge.
