# Do Ho Suh

**Who** – South Korean artist (born 1962) who recreates the spaces he has lived in – homes, corridors, staircases – as full-scale translucent fabric sculptures.

**Why aligned** – The reference for architecture as memory and as skin. Suh rebuilds rooms in stitched translucent polyester, 1:1 and ghostly, so a space becomes portable, foldable and haunted by who lived in it. It reframes the built environment as something carried rather than fixed – the most emotionally precise answer to "what does a space hold" on the Architecture shelf.

**The formula** – Render space as a second skin. Reconstruct a real architecture at full scale in translucent fabric so it is present and vanishing at once. Let the material make displacement visible and let exact measurement carry memory.

**Canon** – Seoul Home/L.A. Home (1999); the Hub and corridor fabric works; Some/One, the floor of dog tags rising into a hollow armour; the staircase pieces.
