# Paula Scher

**Who** – American graphic designer, partner at Pentagram New York since 1991. BFA from the Tyler School of Art at Temple University. She designed book covers at Random House, landed at CBS Records in 1972 and spent the decade art-directing album sleeves at volume, then co-founded Koppel and Scher with Terry Koppel in 1984. Art Directors Club Hall of Fame 1998, Chrysler Award 2000, AIGA Medal 2001, Type Directors Club Medal 2006 as its first woman recipient, National Design Award for Communication Design 2013. Books: *Make It Bigger* 2002, *MAPS* 2011, *25 Years at the Public: A Love Story* 2020.

She is very often described as the first female partner at Pentagram. The better sourcing puts her second. Worth recording, because the claim travels further than the fact and repeating it in a room of designers is a cheap way to lose the room.

**Why aligned** – She is the proof that typography alone can carry an institution. No symbol, no mascot, no mark doing the work – the wordmark set with enough conviction becomes the identity, and the system flexes for decades without diluting. That is the exact argument Five Points makes about its own surfaces, and Pentagram Work pages are already the ratified reference for the studio site. Scher is the partner whose output most defines what that restraint looks like when it chooses to be loud rather than quiet.

She also opened Pentagram to cultural identity – theatres, museums, opera houses – rather than corporate identity alone. That move, taking a discipline built for one category and pointing it at another, is the same move Five Points makes taking operating-system thinking into med spas and country clubs.

**The formula** – Let the type be the image. When a wordmark is set with conviction, the identity needs no symbol beside it. Take scale as a material in its own right: at architectural size, letterforms stop being text and become space. Design serious, not solemn – her distinction, and the useful one. Serious work applies known solutions professionally and safely. Play is the state of not knowing what you are doing, and that is the only place breakthroughs come from, which is why she argues for deliberately working outside competence. Treat the constraint as the engine rather than the obstacle. And repeat with variation across decades: the painted maps are one idea worked for thirty years, and they compound in a way that scattered one-offs never do.

**Canon** – The CBS Records sleeves of the 1970s. The Public Theater identity of the mid-1990s, wood type and compressed grotesques fusing high and low into a system that reset what a cultural institution could look like, and the *25 Years at the Public* account of it. Citi, 1998. Tiffany and Co. Microsoft. Shake Shack. The Museum of Modern Art. The High Line. The Metropolitan Opera. The painted *MAPS* series. *Make It Bigger*, which is as much about the politics of client work as the design of it.

**Pull for** – typography, art-direction, high-low, maximalism, spatial-direction.
