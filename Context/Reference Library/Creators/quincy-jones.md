# Quincy Jones

**Who** – The producer-arranger who worked at every altitude of American music (March 14, 1933 – November 3, 2024): 28 Grammys from 80 nominations, EGOT completed in 2016 with the Tony for The Color Purple revival, and the production chair on the best-selling album of all time.

**Why aligned** – The polymath standard. Jones moved through jazz arranging, pop production, film scoring, television and publishing without any of them reading as a side project – breadth as mastery, the Leonardo da Vinci card's principle in a single musical life. And he is the canon reference for creative leadership at scale: the producer as architect who makes room for genius rather than competing with it.

**The formula** – The arranger's ear applied to everything. Hear the whole record before anyone plays; cast collaborators like an orchestrator voices instruments; manage ego as a production discipline. The famous instruction taped to the We Are the World studio door – "Check Your Ego at the Door," with its companion line "We don't want to make a hunger record in tuxedos" – is the whole method in one gesture: 46 stars, one night, one take at humility. Pull him whenever a project needs many brilliant inputs bent to one coherent sound.

**Canon** – the Michael Jackson trilogy – Off the Wall (1979), Thriller (1982), Bad (1987); We Are the World (1985), produced and conducted; the 28 Grammys; the 2016 EGOT.

**Pull for** – polymath, producer-as-architect, ego-management, breadth, cadence.
