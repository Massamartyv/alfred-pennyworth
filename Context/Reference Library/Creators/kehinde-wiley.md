# Kehinde Wiley

**Who** – American painter (born 1977, Los Angeles), working between New York and Paris. Famous for placing young Black subjects in the compositional language of Old Master European portraiture, against lush decorative grounds.

**Why aligned** – Wiley does not petition to enter the canon, he occupies it and rewrites the power inside the frame – the same reclaimed-dignity, Blackness-as-heroic-force charge that runs through Django and the wider identity work. Heroic posture as a political act.

**The formula** – Take the visual grammar of power – Velázquez, Rubens, Titian – and put the people it excluded at its center, scaled up and framed in ornament. Beauty as confrontation, the decorative as armor.

**Canon** – *President Barack Obama* (2018), National Portrait Gallery. *Rumors of War* (2019), the 27-foot bronze equestrian answer to Confederate monuments, now at the Virginia Museum of Fine Arts.

**Pull for** – portraiture, reclaiming-the-canon, art-direction, heroic-posture, ornament.
