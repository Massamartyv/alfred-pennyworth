# Malcolm X

**Who** – Malcolm Little, then Malcolm X, then el-Hajj Malik el-Shabazz. 1925 to 1965. Educated himself in Norfolk Prison Colony by copying out the dictionary by hand, page after page, because his written vocabulary could not carry what he could already say. Became the national spokesman of the Nation of Islam and its most effective organiser, broke with it in 1964, made the Hajj that year, founded the Organization of Afro-American Unity, and was assassinated at the Audubon Ballroom on 21 February 1965 at 39.

**Why aligned** – He is the reference for the thing almost nobody manages: total public commitment to a position, and total public revision of it when the evidence changes. Most people avoid the first, and the ones who manage it almost never survive the second, because the brain resists inconsistency harder than it resists error. Malcolm did both at full volume, in front of the largest available audience, knowing exactly what it would cost him. Munger's inconsistency-avoidance tendency has no better counter-example.

He is also the sharpest available answer to the question of who the work is made for. He never once translated himself downward to be understood by the room that was uncomfortable. The room came to him.

**The formula** – Educate yourself past the point where anyone can dismiss you on the merits, and do it in the least glamorous way available. Take a position and hold it in public where retreat is expensive. Then revise it in public when you are wrong, and treat the revision as the strongest possible evidence of seriousness rather than as damage to manage.

The rhetorical instrument: plain images that survive being repeated. Chickens coming home to roost. The ballot or the bullet. By any means necessary. Every one is short, concrete and impossible to soften in the retelling, which is why they outlived the speeches that carried them. He also spoke to audiences as adults, assuming they could follow an argument, which is the same refusal to condescend this brand claims as a decision default.

**Canon** – "The Ballot or the Bullet", Cleveland, April 1964. "Message to the Grassroots", Detroit, November 1963. The 1963 *City Desk* and Louis Lomax television interviews, which show him using broadcast as a weapon a decade before that was a common skill. The letter from Mecca, 1964, which is the revision in its rawest form. *The Autobiography of Malcolm X*, with Alex Haley, 1965 – noting it is a collaboration completed after his death and shaped by Haley's hand, which is worth holding when reading it as a primary source.
