# Everything Everywhere All at Once

**What** – Daniels (Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert), 2022. A five-star entry in the culture.md media canon. A middle-aged Chinese-American laundromat owner discovers she can access skills from parallel versions of herself across a multiverse – and that this power exists precisely because her life in this timeline is the most unremarkable of all of them.

**Why aligned** – The film holds the weight of unlived lives as its central subject. Every parallel universe Evelyn glimpses is a version of herself where she chose differently: a movie star, a chef, a warrior. The ache is not that those lives were better – the film is careful about this – but that they existed at all, that choice forecloses possibility in both directions. Chaos is the path to meaning here not because chaos is noble but because nihilism, taken all the way to its logical end, loops back into love. The thesis: if nothing matters, then the only thing that matters is what you choose to love.

**Why it lands** – The everything-at-once form is not chaos for its own sake – it is the only form that could carry the film's content. The absurdist registers (googly eyes, bagels, hot dog fingers) and the emotional registers coexist at the same volume without one undermining the other. That tonal discipline is the craft lesson. When you trust the emotional truth of what you are saying, you can be as formally strange as the work requires. Genre, tone and register are tools; the constraint is emotional honesty, not decorum.

**Canon** – the rock scene (two rocks on a cliff in a universe where life never evolved); "be kind, especially when it's difficult"; the laundromat finale.

**Pull for** – narrative, identity, tonal-range, formal-experimentation, emotional-honesty, chaos-and-meaning.
