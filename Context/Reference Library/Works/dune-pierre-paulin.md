# Dune

**What** – Pierre Paulin's seating landscape, designed circa 1970 within the Pierre Paulin Program (1968-1972) – a whole-room modular soft-seating environment too ambitious for its era. Never produced in Paulin's lifetime (he died in 2009); revived posthumously by the family operation led by his son Benjamin Paulin, at roughly €5,000-8,000 per module. The revival's exact start year is inconsistently reported and left unrecorded here.

**Why it lands** – Furniture dissolved into topography. Dune abolishes the sofa as an object in a room and replaces it with terrain – seating as landscape, the floor rising to hold you. Its second life is a study in how design history gets re-canonized: championed by Frank Ocean and Kanye West – both carded in this library – it became a musician-culture grail and then a viral aspiration, one TikTok tribute passing 17.6 million views and a dupe market (Sohnne and others) springing up beneath the authentic family production. A 1970 idea whose audience arrived 50 years late.

**Canon** – the Pierre Paulin Program (1968-1972); the posthumous family production; the Ocean and West associations; the TikTok moment and its dupe economy as proof of cultural arrival.
