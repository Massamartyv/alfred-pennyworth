# Homegoing

**What** – Yaa Gyasi, 2016. The most reference-worthy entry in the personal Literature database. Two half-sisters born in 18th-century Ghana – one who marries a British colonizer, one who is imprisoned in the dungeons of a slave castle – never meet. From that single divergence, the novel traces seven generations across continents and centuries: from the Gold Coast to the American South to modern-day Harlem and Stanford.

**Why aligned** – The structure is the argument. Each chapter is its own contained world – a discrete generation, a discrete character – and the accumulated weight of all of them is the actual novel. What Gyasi is making is a case about history as inheritance: the ways trauma, dispossession and longing travel through generations not as memory but as physical fact, as the disposition of the body, the shape of the wound. The Notion summary states it plainly: "It is a book about how history lives in the body long after the mind has tried to forget." The divergence at the origin – one sister into the relative protection of colonial alliance, one into the hold of a slave ship – is never resolved or reconciled. The novel holds both lines without privileging one or manufacturing a meeting. That refusal is the formal discipline.

**Why it lands** – Use structure as argument. The chapter-per-generation form is not just an organizational convenience; it enacts the thesis by forcing the reader to experience the accumulation directly. Each generation arrives fresh, but the reader carries the weight of every preceding one. For any project that wants to demonstrate how something accrues across time, this is the structural reference: do not tell the reader that weight accumulates. Build the form so they feel it accumulate.

**Canon** – the divergence at Maame's choice; the dungeon section; the letter-writing scene across the generational break; the final reunion at the water.

**Pull for** – narrative, structure-as-argument, diaspora, multi-generational, historical-weight, Black-Atlantic-tradition.
