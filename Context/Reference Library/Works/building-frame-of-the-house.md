# Building Frame of the House

**What** – A 60-square-meter Tokyo residence by IGArchitects (principal Masato Igarashi), completed 2023. On a sliver of a site – roughly 2.9 meters of frontage by 16 meters deep – seven staggered concrete slabs replace conventional rooms, so the whole house reads as one continuous volume.

**Why aligned** – The frame is the architecture. The structural members that usually vanish behind finish become the entire aesthetic proposition – honesty over decoration, evidence of care over evidence of cost, intention per square foot pushed to its limit. The raw board-marked concrete is the 20% brutalist shell waiting to be warmed.

**Why it lands** – Privacy through level change instead of walls. Misaligned planes and shifting floor heights generate distinct zones inside a single open room, light moving down through the section. Constraint becomes the design engine – the tighter the site, the clearer the idea.

**Canon** – 60 sqm, 2.9m frontage; seven staggered floor slabs; exposed board-marked reinforced concrete; black-metal stairs and ladders; no fixed partitions. Published in ArchDaily, Dezeen and Designboom, 2023.

**Pull for** – materiality, spatial-direction, structural-honesty, small-space-ingenuity, constraint-as-engine.

**Trend read** – Part of a continuing Japanese tradition of extracting spatial richness from micro-urban sites, where section does the work that floor area cannot. Pending enrichment for current-cycle signal.
