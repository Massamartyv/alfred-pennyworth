# Chess

**What** – The two-player strategy game that emerged in India as chaturanga (around the 6th to 7th century CE), passed into Persia as shatranj, and took its modern form in Europe after about 1475, when the queen gained full-board range and the bishop its long diagonal. Sixty-four squares, fixed rules, the object to checkmate the opposing king.

**Why aligned** – The reference for infinite depth from minimal, fixed rules. Chess is the canonical proof that constraint is the source of richness, not the enemy of it – a tiny rule set and a small board yield a space no one has exhausted in fifteen centuries. It is the enduring symbol of strategy, foresight and the contest of pure intellect.

**Why it lands** – Fix the rules tightly and let the depth come from play, not from more pieces. The elegance is in how little it takes to generate the inexhaustible – each piece a different constraint, the whole game emerging from how they interact. The lesson for any designed system: depth is engineered through clean, asymmetric constraints, not through volume.

**Canon** – chaturanga and the Indian origin; the late-15th-century European rule changes that produced the modern game; the standing role as the universal symbol of strategy and intellect across art, film and literature.

**Pull for** – strategy, constraint-as-depth, timeless-system, mastery, positioning.
