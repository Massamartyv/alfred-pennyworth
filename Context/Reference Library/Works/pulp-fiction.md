# Pulp Fiction

**What** – Quentin Tarantino, 1994. Interlocking Los Angeles crime stories – two hitmen, a boxer, a crime boss and his wife – told out of chronological order so the pieces resolve into a whole only in retrospect. Palme d'Or at Cannes 1994; Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay.

**Why aligned** – The reference for structure as the argument and dialogue as the main event. The anthology-within-a-feature form, the refusal of chronology, the long conversations that are the set pieces rather than the breathers – the style is not applied to the content, it is the content. It also models the high-low collapse the system prizes: scripture beside a foot massage, a five-dollar milkshake beside a contract killing, each given identical weight.

**Why it lands** – Reorder time so meaning is assembled, not delivered. The out-of-sequence structure makes the audience build the whole, and the payoff lands harder because they built it. Dialogue carries the tension; the violence punctuates rather than leads.

**Canon** – the "Royale with Cheese" exchange; the twist contest at Jack Rabbit Slim's; the adrenaline-shot scene; the diner hold-up that bookends the film; the briefcase that is never explained.

**Pull for** – nonlinear-structure, dialogue-as-set-piece, high-low, needle-drop, narrative.

**Trend read** – Among the most influential American films of the 1990s; its structural and dialogue innovations became standard film grammar and its imagery stays in continuous cultural circulation. Canonical and stable.
