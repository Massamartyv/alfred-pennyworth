# Fallingwater

**What** – Frank Lloyd Wright's Kaufmann House at Mill Run, Pennsylvania – designed 1935, built 1936-1938 with a guest house in 1939 – cantilevered directly over a waterfall on Bear Run. Widely named the finest work of American architecture, a museum since, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site as of 2019.

**Why aligned** – Living above and inside the sound of moving water, not across from it. It speaks to the scent memory of rain and petrichor, to "rain days are the best days," to the ideal creative environment – quiet inside, alive outside, the view framing the work. Integration taken to its most confident extreme.

**Why it lands** – Integration as confrontation. The house does not defer to the landscape, it occupies the most charged point in it. Reinforced-concrete terraces stack as horizontal planes that echo the natural rock ledges below, and the waterfall is never seen from the house, only heard – the restraint is the genius.

**Canon** – Edgar J. Kaufmann Sr. as client; reinforced-concrete cantilevered terraces over Bear Run; the hearth set on living boulders; the stair descending to the water. Owned and operated by the Western Pennsylvania Conservancy.

**Pull for** – nature-integration, materiality, spatial-direction, sense-of-place, restraint.
