# Scarface

**What** – Brian De Palma, 1983. A five-star entry in the culture.md media canon. Tony Montana arrives in Miami with nothing and climbs to the top of the cocaine trade through violence, hunger and will – then watches everything he built collapse under the weight of the man he became in the climbing.

**Why aligned** – The thesis is the climb as its own undoing. Tony Montana is not destroyed by enemies – he is destroyed by the version of himself that the climb required. The immigrant hunger that drives him from the bottom is the same force that, unchecked, makes him ungovernable at the top. The film earns its place as a cultural reference not because Tony wins but because it shows precisely why winning at that cost is the same as losing. Ambition is the film's engine; hubris is its fuel; the price of the climb is what it is actually about.

**Why it lands** – Let the character's strength become their fatal flaw without commentary. The film never editorialises on Tony – it simply follows him through to the logical end of his operating system. Hubris is not punished by fate; it is punished by itself. When a character is written with that kind of internal logic, the audience understands the ending before it arrives and watches it happen anyway. That is dramatic inevitability done right.

**Canon** – the mansion sequence; the "say hello to my little friend" staircase finale; "the world is yours" blimp.
