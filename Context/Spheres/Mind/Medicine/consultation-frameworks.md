# Consultation Frameworks

The method layer of the Medicine sphere. Five frameworks stacked, each doing a job the others cannot. Calgary Cambridge runs the conversation. SOAP charts it. The Lifestyle Medicine pillars supply the data schema. Bayesian differential reasoning turns symptoms into ranked hypotheses. The Functional Medicine Matrix synthesises everything into causes rather than a list of complaints.

The stack is durable method. It describes how a consultation is conducted, never what any particular consultation found – that belongs to Notion.

---

## Spheres Covered

Medicine. Draws on Philosophy for the reasoning discipline and Public Speaking for the communication craft that the Calgary Cambridge layer is built from.

---

## Domain Constants

**Order of operation.** Calgary Cambridge governs the shape of the encounter from first word to last. SOAP is written during and after it. The six pillars are gathered inside the information-gathering movement. Bayesian reasoning runs continuously underneath and surfaces at explanation and planning. The Matrix is assembled before any recommendation is formed, per the charter.

**The charter outranks the method.** Any framework here that would produce a recommendation the charter forbids is overruled by the charter. A beautifully structured consultation that skips the interaction check is a failed consultation.

**Nothing here is a script.** These are structures for thinking, not phrases to recite. A consultation that sounds like a form being filled in has already lost the thing Calgary Cambridge exists to protect.

## Context

## Layer 1 – Calgary Cambridge

The consultation model built by Silverman, Kurtz and Draper. Five sequential movements with two continuous threads running through all of them. The sequence is not rigid – a patient who opens with the thing they were saving for the end is followed, not corrected – but every movement gets covered before the encounter closes.

### Initiating the session

*Purpose:* establish rapport and find out why this person came, in their words, before any hypothesis has formed.

Behaviours: greet and orient before anything clinical. Open with a genuinely open question and then stop talking – the median physician interrupts inside 20 seconds and loses most of what mattered. Let the opening statement run to its natural end. Screen for further concerns before moving on, because the second and third concerns are frequently the real ones. Agree an agenda out loud, including what will not be covered today.

The failure mode here is premature closure. A hypothesis formed in the first 30 seconds contaminates every question that follows, and the questions will confirm it because they were built to.

### Gathering information

*Purpose:* build the clinical picture and the human one, in parallel, without collapsing the second into the first.

Behaviours: move from open to closed questioning in that order, funnelling rather than interrogating. Take the full symptom history – onset, character, radiation, timing, severity, aggravating and relieving factors, associated symptoms, what changed in the weeks before it started. Take the background – past history, medications and supplements in full, allergies, family history and the six pillars in the schema below.

Run the two parallel tracks deliberately. The disease track asks what is happening in the tissue. The illness track asks what this person believes is happening, what they fear it is, how it is affecting their life and what they hope will be done. Ideas, concerns, expectations, effect. A consultation that gathers only the disease track will produce a technically correct plan that nobody follows.

Signpost transitions. Summarise back periodically and invite correction, which catches errors while they are still cheap.

### Explanation and planning

*Purpose:* transfer understanding, not information, and build a plan the person actually intends to carry out.

Behaviours: find out what they already know and what they want to know before explaining anything. Chunk and check – small pieces, pause, confirm comprehension, continue. Use their vocabulary. Avoid jargon or define it in the same sentence.

Explain the reasoning, not only the conclusion. Give the differential with its rough probabilities, name what would change the ranking and state uncertainty plainly. Attach the evidence grade to every recommendation as the charter requires, and name the ladder rung being applied and why. Where a rung is being skipped, say so and say why.

Negotiate rather than instruct. Offer options with their tradeoffs, elicit preference, check for barriers – cost, time, taste, prior bad experience, the fact that they will not take anything four times a day. A plan that ignores a barrier is a plan that fails silently and shows up as non-adherence at follow-up.

### Closing the session

*Purpose:* end with a shared understanding of the plan and a clear map of what to do when it does not go as expected.

Behaviours: summarise the plan briefly. Contract explicitly – who does what, by when. Ask for a teach-back on anything that matters, which is the only reliable test of whether the explanation landed. Check for final questions.

Then safety-net, which is the load-bearing act of the close. State what is expected to happen and over what timeframe. State exactly what would mean the working hypothesis is wrong. State precisely what to do and who to contact if any of it appears, with the relevant red flags named in plain language rather than gestured at. Set the follow-up date, which the charter requires on every recommendation, and record it.

A consultation without safety-netting is not finished, however elegant everything before it was.

### Building the relationship – continuous

Runs through all four movements above. Attend to non-verbal behaviour, both directions. Accept what is said without judgement, especially about substances, adherence and the things people are embarrassed to report. Show empathy explicitly and name the emotion where it is present – acknowledgement does more clinical work than reassurance does. Be transparent about what is being thought and why. Share the reasoning as it happens rather than presenting a verdict at the end.

### Providing structure – continuous

Also runs throughout. Summarise at the end of each line of enquiry. Signpost before changing direction. Sequence logically. Manage the clock without letting the clock manage the consultation. Structure is what allows the conversation to be flexible without becoming lost.

## Layer 2 – SOAP charting

The charting discipline. Every consultation produces a SOAP entry, written to the Consultations database. Four sections, each with a rule about what may and may not appear in it.

**S – Subjective.** What the person reports, in their words where the wording carries information. The presenting concern, the symptom history, the illness track – ideas, concerns, expectations, effect. Relevant background, current medications and supplements, allergies. Pertinent negatives, which are subjective findings too and matter as much as the positives.

Rule: no interpretation lives here. "Reports crushing chest pressure on exertion" belongs in S. "Likely angina" does not.

**O – Objective.** What is measured or observed. Vitals, physical findings, laboratory values with units and their reference and functional ranges, imaging results, biometric trends drawn from the record. Anything that would read the same if a different clinician had gathered it.

Rule: values carry units, dates and the range in force at the time. A number without its range is not an objective finding, it is a rumour.

**A – Assessment.** The reasoning. The ranked differential with rough probabilities and the basis for the ranking, per Layer 4. The Matrix synthesis, per Layer 5. What would discriminate between the top hypotheses. What remains genuinely unknown.

Rule: this is a differential and never a diagnosis, per the charter. Probabilities are stated as rough and are honest about being rough.

**P – Plan.** What is being done. Each recommendation carries four things without exception: the ladder rung it sits on, the evidence grade, the specific dose or protocol with its source and the follow-up date. Also here: what was deliberately not done and why, any escalation made and the safety-netting given verbatim enough to be checked later.

Rule: a plan line missing its grade or its follow-up date is incomplete and is not charted until both are present.

The chart is written for a reader two years from now who remembers none of it. That reader is the same practice, and the charter calls the record its memory for exactly this reason.

## Layer 3 – The six Lifestyle Medicine pillars as data schema

The pillars are not a wellness checklist. They are the structured intake schema for the Rung 1 pharmacy, gathered inside the information-gathering movement and charted in S. Each pillar has fields specific enough to dose against, because "sleep more" is not a prescription and "eat better" is not either.

**Sleep.** Habitual bed and wake times, weekday against weekend. Total time in bed against estimated time asleep. Sleep latency. Night wakings, their number and their cause. Morning alertness. Light exposure in the hour before bed and in the first hour after waking. Caffeine timing relative to sleep onset. Alcohol timing. Room temperature, darkness, noise. Snoring, witnessed apnoea, daytime sleepiness. Shift or travel disruption.

**Nutrition.** Habitual pattern rather than a single recalled day. Eating window and first-meal timing. Protein per meal. Plant diversity across a week, counted as distinct species. Ultra-processed proportion. Added sugar. Fermented foods. Fibre. Fluid intake. Alcohol as both nutrition and substance. Known intolerances, elimination attempts and what they showed. Who cooks, who shops and what the food environment actually looks like on a hard day.

Gather the plate against the reference pattern in the charter, by daily and weekly frequency rather than by portion arithmetic: beans and legumes, whole grains, cruciferous vegetables, other vegetables, berries, other fruit, nuts and seeds, flaxseed, alliums, herbs and spices, and water. Each is a field with a count, and a field left at zero across a week is a finding worth naming in the Assessment rather than a gap to fill reflexively with a supplement. The counts exist to make the pattern visible and doseable, not to be scored or performed.

**Movement.** Structured training – modality, frequency, duration, intensity, load progression. Non-exercise activity, honestly – daily step count, hours seated in the longest unbroken block. Cardiorespiratory capacity where known. Strength markers. Mobility limitations, pain on movement, injury history. Recovery quality between sessions.

**Stress.** Perceived load and its sources, named specifically rather than as a single word. Diurnal shape – when in the day it peaks. Recovery practices and whether they are actually performed. Autonomic markers from the biometric stream, heart-rate variability trend in particular. Rest that is genuinely restorative against rest that is only an absence of work. Financial and occupational strain, which are medical variables whatever anybody prefers.

**Relationships.** Household composition. Close-tie count and contact frequency. Loneliness, asked directly, because it is not inferable and carries an effect size on mortality comparable to smoking. Conflict load. Caregiving burden. Belonging – community, faith, creative or professional. Support available in an actual crisis, named as people rather than as a feeling.

**Substances.** Everything ingested, in full. Alcohol in units per week and its pattern rather than its average. Nicotine in any delivery form. Caffeine in milligrams and timing. Recreational substances without judgement, because judgement produces underreporting and underreporting produces missed interactions. Prescribed medications with doses and start dates. Every supplement and botanical currently in use, including the ones nobody thinks to mention. This field feeds the interaction check directly and is the one field that is never left partially gathered.

Digital load – screens and sustained near-vision work – has no pillar of its own. Chart it under Stress, additionally under Sleep when it displaces the sleep window, and name the visual mechanism itself in the Assessment rather than stretching a pillar to hold it.

The pillars are re-gathered periodically rather than once. They drift, and the drift is frequently the antecedent the Matrix is looking for.

## Layer 4 – Bayesian differential reasoning

The method for turning symptoms into ranked hypotheses without deceiving oneself along the way.

**Generate broadly before narrowing.** List the plausible hypotheses before evaluating any of them. Include the common, the serious-though-unlikely and the treatable-if-caught. The three sets are different and all three belong on the list. Common things are common, and the base rate is doing more work than the presentation is.

**Assign rough priors.** Start from prevalence in a person of this age, sex, history and context, before the specifics of this presentation are applied. The prior is the anchor that stops a vivid symptom from dominating. Rough is fine and rough is honest – a prior stated as "roughly one in 20 in a man of this age" is more useful than a false precision and more useful than no number at all.

**Update explicitly on each finding.** Every symptom, sign and result moves the ranking, and the movement is stated rather than performed silently. Ask what this finding would look like under each hypothesis, not only under the favoured one. A finding that is equally likely under every hypothesis carries no information however dramatic it seems. Pertinent negatives update the ranking as forcefully as positives do and are recorded for that reason.

**Rank with rough probabilities and name the discriminators.** The output is an ordered list with approximate likelihoods and, next to it, the specific test, finding or trial of time that would most efficiently separate the top candidates. Naming the discriminator is what converts a differential into a plan.

**Anchoring guard.** The characteristic failure of this method is committing early and then reading every subsequent finding as confirmation. Three defences, run deliberately:

- Ask directly what would have to be true for the leading hypothesis to be wrong, and go looking for it rather than waiting for it.
- Keep the most serious plausible candidate on the list explicitly until it is actively excluded, not merely until something more likely appears. Ruling out by preference is not ruling out.
- Where a prior consultation or another clinician has already framed the problem, re-derive the differential from the findings rather than inheriting the frame. Inherited frames are the most durable anchors in medicine.

Where the ranking does not resolve, say so. An honest unresolved differential with a named discriminator and a safety net is a better clinical product than a confident wrong answer.

## Layer 5 – The Functional Medicine Matrix

The synthesis instrument. The differential asks what is happening. The Matrix asks why it is happening to this person now, and it is assembled before any recommendation is formed, per the charter.

Three organising questions, worked in order.

**Antecedents – what set the stage.** Genetic predisposition and family pattern. Early-life history. Chronic exposures. Long-standing structural or physiological vulnerabilities. Prior illness and prior treatment, including the treatment that solved one thing and started another. Antecedents are usually not modifiable, and naming them is still essential because they set the probability landscape everything else operates in.

**Triggers – what started it.** The discrete event that initiated the current picture. Infection, injury, medication started or stopped, dietary change, a move, a loss, a job, a season, a travel exposure. The question that finds triggers is not "what is wrong" but "what changed in the weeks and months before this began" – and it is asked explicitly, because people rarely volunteer the connection.

**Mediators – what keeps it going.** The reason it has not resolved. Ongoing inflammation. Sleep debt sustaining a stress response sustaining the sleep debt. Continued exposure. Dysbiosis. Nutrient insufficiency limiting a repair pathway. Secondary gain, avoidance, deconditioning. Beliefs about the illness that shape behaviour around it. Mediators are where the highest-leverage intervention almost always sits, because triggers are usually historical by the time anyone is asked about them.

Around those three, the Matrix maps the findings across the body core clinical imbalances – assimilation, defence and repair, energy, biotransformation and elimination, transport, communication, structural integrity – and holds them alongside the six pillars from Layer 3 and the mental, emotional and spiritual dimensions at the centre. The point of the map is not completeness for its own sake. It is to make visible that a single node is frequently driving findings in three others, so the recommendation can address the node rather than the findings.

Two rules govern the output.

First, the Matrix is assembled from what was gathered, never reverse-engineered to justify a recommendation already chosen. A Matrix written after the herb was selected is decoration.

Second, the synthesis names the smallest number of upstream drivers that explain the largest share of the picture. A Matrix that concludes with 11 equally weighted contributors has not synthesised anything. Three drivers, ranked, with the reasoning shown, is a result.

## Maintenance

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Consultation conducted | Run all five movements of Calgary Cambridge; a consultation without safety-netting is not closed |
| Consultation charted | Write SOAP to the Consultations database with grade, rung, dose source and follow-up date on every plan line |
| Pillar data older than six months | Re-gather the six pillars before treating them as current |
| Differential formed | Run the anchoring guard before the ranking is written down |
| Any recommendation | Assemble the Matrix first, per the charter |
| Annually | Review this stack against current Calgary Cambridge and Institute for Functional Medicine material; frameworks are revised upstream |
