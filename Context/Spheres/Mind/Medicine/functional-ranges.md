# Functional Ranges

Seed reference for the markers most commonly drawn in a root-cause practice. Conventional reference range beside a tighter functional-optimal range, with a note on what drift toward each edge tends to mean.

---

## The caveat, up front

Functional ranges are contested territory and this file says so before it says anything else.

Conventional reference ranges are built from the middle 95% of a reference population. That population is not a healthy population – it is whoever showed up to be tested, which in most laboratories skews toward people with something wrong. A conventional range therefore answers the question "is this value unusual among people who get tested" and not the question "is this value good". Those are different questions, and the second is the one a root-cause practice is asking.

Functional ranges attempt the second question. They are narrower. They are drawn from outcome associations, expert consensus and clinical convention rather than from a single agreed statistical method, and sources disagree with each other – sometimes materially. Two respected functional practitioners will publish different optimal windows for the same marker and both will cite literature. Anyone presenting these numbers as settled is overselling them.

So the values below are working values. They are honest starting points, not diagnostic thresholds, and they are to be refined against the personal baselines of the operator as the record accumulates. Where his own draws sit consistently outside a functional window across three or more panels with no corresponding symptom, the window is wrong for him and the file is corrected, with the reason noted. Personal baseline beats published optimum every time; that is the entire argument for keeping a longitudinal record in the first place.

Two rules govern every number here.

**One.** A value outside a functional range is not a finding. It is one input to a pattern, per the charter. Nothing is recommended from a single marker in isolation, and a marker inside its conventional range is not reassurance either.

**Two.** A value outside a conventional range is a different matter and is treated as such. Conventional ranges are wide precisely because their edges mark where pathology becomes likely. Crossing a conventional edge routes to a physician, not to a botanical.

Units below are US conventional. Sex-specific values are given for the adult male where the marker requires it, since that is the reference patient. Ranges vary by laboratory and by assay, so the range printed on the actual report always governs the conventional column.

## Domain Constants

Every logged value carries its units, its draw date, the laboratory reference range in force and the functional window applied. A number without those four is not usable longitudinally. The record lives in the Lab Results database in Notion; this file holds only the reference.

## Context

### Complete blood count

| Marker | Units | Conventional | Functional-optimal | Drift note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| White blood cell count | K/uL | 4.0–11.0 | 5.0–8.0 | Low drift suggests viral load, immune depletion or marrow suppression; high drift suggests infection, inflammation or a stress response. |
| Red blood cell count | M/uL | 4.5–5.9 | 4.6–5.4 | Low drift tracks deficiency or blood loss; high drift tracks dehydration or chronic hypoxia. |
| Haemoglobin | g/dL | 13.5–17.5 | 14.0–15.5 | Low drift points to iron, B12 or folate deficiency, occult bleeding or inflammation; high drift to dehydration, sleep apnoea or altitude. |
| Haematocrit | % | 38.5–50 | 40–48 | Moves with haemoglobin; a rise without a haemoglobin rise is usually plasma volume, not red cells. |
| Mean corpuscular volume | fL | 80–100 | 85–92 | Low drift is the classic iron picture; high drift is the classic B12 or folate picture and can also reflect alcohol. |
| Red cell distribution width | % | 11.5–15.0 | under 13.0 | Rises early, before the haemoglobin falls. High drift flags a mixed or developing deficiency and is an underused early signal. |
| Platelets | K/uL | 150–400 | 175–350 | Low drift suggests liver disease, autoimmune consumption or B12 deficiency; high drift suggests inflammation, iron deficiency or infection. |
| Neutrophil to lymphocyte ratio | ratio | not routinely reported | 1.0–2.0 | Computed from the differential. High drift is a general marker of systemic inflammatory and physiological stress; low drift can accompany viral illness. |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fasting glucose | mg/dL | 70–99 | 75–86 | High drift within the conventional range is the earliest routine signal of insulin resistance and precedes a raised HbA1c by years. Low drift can reflect reactive hypoglycaemia or adrenal insufficiency. |
| Fasting insulin | uIU/mL | 2.6–24.9 | 2.0–5.0 | The most sensitive routine marker of insulin resistance, and the conventional upper limit is close to meaningless. High drift moves years ahead of glucose. |
| HbA1c | % | 4.0–5.6 | 4.8–5.3 | High drift tracks average glycaemia over roughly three months. Low drift is not automatically good and can reflect shortened red cell lifespan or anaemia. |

| Potassium | mmol/L | 3.5–5.2 | 4.0–4.5 | Low drift accompanies diuretics, vomiting or magnesium depletion; high drift raises renal and adrenal questions and is checked for haemolysis artefact first. |

### Lipids and cardiovascular risk

| Marker | Units | Conventional | Functional-optimal | Drift note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total cholesterol | mg/dL | 125–200 | 160–200 | Weak in isolation. Low drift is not a goal and associates with poorer outcomes at the bottom end. |
| Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol | mg/dL | under 100 | 70–120 with particle context | A concentration, not a count. Interpreted only alongside apolipoprotein B, never alone. |
| High-density lipoprotein cholesterol | mg/dL | above 40 | 55–80 | Low drift accompanies insulin resistance and inactivity; very high drift is not automatically favourable and can be dysfunctional. |
| Triglycerides | mg/dL | under 150 | under 80 | High drift is a direct read on carbohydrate load, alcohol and insulin resistance, and it moves quickly with diet. |
| Triglyceride to high-density lipoprotein ratio | ratio | not routinely reported | under 1.5 | Computed. One of the cheapest and most informative proxies for insulin resistance available on a standard panel. |
| Apolipoprotein B | mg/dL | under 90 | under 80 | The particle count, and the better causal marker. Where it disagrees with low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, this one governs. |
| Lipoprotein A | nmol/L | under 75 | under 30 | Largely genetic and largely fixed. Drawn once for risk stratification rather than tracked, and high drift changes the aggressiveness of everything else. |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thyroid stimulating hormone | mIU/L | 0.45–4.5 | 1.0–2.0 | High drift within the conventional range is the most common missed hypothyroid picture; low drift with symptoms raises the hyperthyroid question. |

| Marker | Units | Conventional | Functional-optimal | Drift note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D, 25-hydroxy | ng/mL | 30–100 | 50–80 | Low drift is close to universal at this latitude in winter. Above roughly 100 the risk profile turns; more is not better. |
| Vitamin B12 | pg/mL | 200–900 | 600–900 | The conventional floor is far too low and functional deficiency is common in the lower third. Methylmalonic acid settles an ambiguous result. |
| Folate, serum | ng/mL | above 3.0 | 10–25 | Low drift impairs methylation and interacts with B12; very high drift can mask a B12 deficiency rather than solve one. |

### Reading a panel

Read the panel, never the row. The charter forbids a recommendation from a single marker in isolation, and this file is where that rule bites hardest, because a table of numbers invites exactly that failure. Four habits enforce it.

Read patterns across sections. A low alkaline phosphatase, a low zinc and a slow-healing wound are one finding wearing three hats. A raised gamma-glutamyl transferase with a raised alanine aminotransferase, a raised triglyceride figure and a raised fasting insulin is metabolic fatty liver, and no single one of those four says so.

Read the trend before the value. Three draws showing a marker walking steadily toward an edge is a stronger finding than one draw sitting just past it. This is what the Lab Results database exists for.

Read the confounders. Recent illness, a hard training block, dehydration, fasting duration, time of day, supplements taken that morning and the assay used all move numbers. Ask before interpreting.

Read what is missing. The most informative markers are frequently the ones nobody ordered – fasting insulin, apolipoprotein B, free triiodothyronine, red blood cell magnesium, homocysteine. Naming the absent test is part of reading the panel.

## Maintenance

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| New panel in hand | Log every marker with units, date, laboratory range and functional window to Lab Results before interpreting |
| Value outside a conventional range | Route to a physician; conventional edges are pathology thresholds, not tuning targets |
| Personal baseline sits outside a functional window across three or more draws with no symptom | Refine the working value in this file and record the reason |
| New marker drawn for the first time | Add it to the relevant table with its conventional range, a sourced functional window and a drift note |
| Annually | Review the functional windows against current literature and against the accumulated record |
