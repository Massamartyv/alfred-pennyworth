# Georgia HOA Law – Owner Rights and Remedies

> **The floor is the Georgia Nonprofit Corporation Code, and it applies to every incorporated association whether or not the community ever opted into the Property Owners' Association Act.** Records on five business days' written notice, fee-shifting against a board that refuses without reasonable basis, a special meeting on the demand of five per cent of the votes and court removal of a director on a finding of dishonest conduct at 10 per cent. Everything else – the statutory lien, the statement of account, the fine and interest caps – depends on what the recorded Declaration says, and nobody knows until it is read. This file holds the durable statutory instrument. The live case that founded it is `Context/Spheres/System/Real Estate/garden-walk-covenant-investigation.md`.

### Context

---

#### 1. The floor – records under the Nonprofit Corporation Code

Nearly every Georgia association is a domestic nonprofit corporation, so Article 16 of Title 14 applies by virtue of the corporate form and not by anything in the covenants. The two-tier structure is the whole game. Tier one costs the member nothing but a letter and five business days. Tier two – the accounting records, the board minutes and the membership list – is where a misappropriation would show, and it requires the demand to state a good-faith purpose with reasonable particularity, describe the records with the same particularity and connect the two. A purpose such as "to determine whether the funds of the association have been collected, safeguarded and expended for their authorised purposes and whether the directors have met the standard of conduct in 14-3-830" is sufficient and ordinary.

The remedy is what gives the demand teeth. Under 14-3-1604 the superior court disposes of the application on an expedited basis and, if it orders inspection, must also order the corporation to pay the costs and reasonable attorney's fees of the member unless the corporation proves it refused in good faith on a reasonable basis for doubt. The burden sits with the association. An individual may file without a lawyer; a corporation cannot appear in a Georgia court of record without one. A board that stonewalls a records demand is therefore choosing to pay a lawyer to explain why, with the fees of the other side riding on the answer.

The financial-statement provision at 14-3-1620 closes the "we were never audited" door. If no accountant reported on the statements, the president or the person responsible for the accounting records certifies them. An association that has never had a review or an audit still owes a balance sheet and a statement of operations.

#### 2. The Property Owners' Association Act – opt-in, and what it adds

The Act reaches only a development whose recorded Declaration expressly submits to it, signed by every owner at the time of recording or by the developer as sole owner. The word "homeowners association" on a letterhead proves nothing about submission. The only test is the Declaration and its amendments in the DeKalb County deed records.

Where the Act applies, the association gains a statutory lien prior to everything but taxes and a first mortgage, and the owner gains the statement-of-account rule: a written request must be answered within five business days or the lien is extinguished as to any purchaser or lender in that transaction. Late charges are capped at the greater of $10 or 10 per cent of the instalment, interest at 10 per cent a year, and no foreclosure lies below $2,000 – $4,000 from 2027, counting assessments only. Section 44-3-231 adds an itemised records duty parallel to 14-3-1601. Section 44-3-223, amended by HB 220 in 2024, lets the association go straight to injunctive relief for covenant violations without first exhausting self-help, which is a board power and not an owner one.

Where the Act does not apply, the Declaration and bylaws govern as a contract running with the land, the Nonprofit Code still governs records, meetings and directors, and any lien, late fee or interest rate has to be found in the instrument itself. There is no statutory backstop and no five-day statement rule.

#### 3. If the community is a condominium

A condominium is created by a recorded condominium declaration submitting the property to that form of ownership; it is not something a townhome subdivision drifts into. Listing sites use "condo" and "townhome" interchangeably and settle nothing. If the Declaration is a condominium instrument, the Condominium Act at 44-3-70 and following governs: 44-3-106 for detailed minutes and itemised financial records, 44-3-109 for the lien, the statement of account on request, interest capped at 10 per cent and a 30-day certified-mail notice before foreclosure. A community built before the 1975 Act may sit under the older Apartment Ownership Act unless its declaration was amended to submit. Cite the article that matches the actual instrument.

#### 4. Director duties and self-dealing

Directors act in good faith with the care of an ordinarily prudent person in a like position, may rely on officers and agents reasonably believed competent, and enjoy a presumption that their process was sound unless it was a gross deviation from the standard. That presumption is why a negligence claim against a volunteer board is hard, and why the conflicting-interest provisions matter more. Under 14-3-860 to 865 a transaction in which a director or a related person has a financial interest survives only if it was disclosed and approved by a majority, and at least two, of the qualified directors; or approved by the qualified members after notice; or approved by the superior court with the Attorney General a party; or shown to have been fair to the corporation when made. Otherwise it is void or voidable. This is the provision for a management company related to a director, a vendor that shares an address with the treasurer or a contract steered to a relative – civil, and reachable even where no theft charge would stick.

#### 5. Member power

Five per cent of the voting power may demand a special meeting by signed, dated writing delivered to any officer; if notice does not follow within 30 days the demanding members may fix the time and place and give notice themselves. Notice runs no fewer than 10 and no more than 60 days. Members remove a director with or without cause, unless the bylaws restrict removal to cause, by the votes that would have elected the director, at a meeting called for that stated purpose. Proxies are valid unless the bylaws forbid them and last 11 months. Default quorum for a corporation formed before July 2023 is 10 per cent of the votes unless the bylaws say otherwise, which they usually do.

The sharpest tool is 14-3-810: on the petition of members holding 10 per cent of the voting power, the superior court may remove a director it finds to have engaged in fraudulent or dishonest conduct or gross abuse of authority, and may bar that person from the board for a period it sets. It needs no 50-member coalition and no 90-day demand, only the 10 per cent and the evidence. The derivative suit at 14-3-740, which lets members sue on behalf of the corporation, needs five per cent or 50 members, a written demand and 90 days, and is the vehicle for recovering money rather than removing people.

#### 6. Criminal exposure and who prosecutes

Theft by taking covers a person in lawful possession who unlawfully appropriates; theft by conversion is the embezzlement statute for anyone holding funds under an obligation to apply them in a specified way. Penalties step at $1,500, $5,000 and $25,000, with two to 20 years and no misdemeanour discretion above the top figure. Forgery in the first degree reaches fabricated minutes, statements or contracts; computer theft reaches unauthorised access to a system used to move money.

The prosecuting authority is the DeKalb County District Attorney in the DeKalb Judicial Circuit, renamed from the Stone Mountain Judicial Circuit by HB 158 with effect from 2025-01-01. The office runs an anti-corruption and white-collar unit whose stated focus is losses above $100,000; a smaller case goes to a general felony division and receives the attention that implies. A report opens with the DeKalb County Police precinct investigative unit, not the District Attorney directly. The Georgia Bureau of Investigation acts on the request of a local agency, not of a homeowner. The consumer-protection division of the Attorney General has no jurisdiction over associations but publishes guidance confirming the right to see the financial documents and recommending a forensic audit as the next step. From 2026 the Georgia Property Owners' Associations Division of the Secretary of State accepts complaints within 180 days of the conduct, with a hearing officer, findings and a 15-day compliance window – the first state oversight channel Georgia has had, with its enforcement power arriving alongside registration in 2027.

#### 7. Limitation periods

Four years for injury to personalty under 9-3-31 is the usual period for fiduciary breach and conversion; six years under 9-3-24 for a claim on a written contract such as a management agreement; four years for most felonies under 17-3-1. Whether a concealed misappropriation delays accrual until discovery was not established from the session and is exactly the kind of question that decides whether a five-year-old theft is still actionable. Ask counsel before assuming any period has run.

#### 8. The reform picture, 2024 to 2027

The trend is real and modest, and it runs in both directions. HB 220 of 2024 strengthened the enforcement power of associations. SB 406 of 2026, the Georgia Property Owners' Bill of Rights Act, passed the Senate 51 to nil and the House 155 to 10 and was signed on 2026-05-12 over the opposition of the Community Associations Institute. Its attorney-fee provisions are law now for actions filed on or after 2026-07-01: certified-mail notice, a 30-day cure period, an itemised statement of fees and judicial review of their reasonableness before any fee shifts to an owner. From 2027-01-01 every association must register annually with the Secretary of State or forfeit the power to fine, lien and foreclose; financial records must be kept 10 years; the foreclosure floor doubles to $4,000 in assessments alone; partial payments apply to assessments before fines and fees; and fines require written notice of the specific provision and a cure period. Bills that would have abolished association foreclosure, allowed dissolution by a 20 per cent petition, capped fines, created an ombudsperson, monitored elections or raised the floor to $20,000 were all introduced in 2025 and 2026 and all died.

#### 9. Procedure

**The demand.** Identify the member, the lot and the deed or parcel reference. State that it is a written demand under 14-3-1602. List the tier-one records with no purpose. State the purpose for tier two in good faith and with particularity, list those records and connect each to the purpose. Request the latest annual financial statements under 14-3-1620. Propose a date no fewer than five business days out, a time within business hours and the principal office as the place, and offer to pay copying at cost. Send by certified mail with return receipt and by email, to the registered agent on the Secretary of State record, to the board at the community office and to any management company. Keep the receipt; the clock and the fee-shifting both run from a provable date. The agent of a member may make and attend the inspection under 14-3-1603 on a one-line written authorisation from the member.

**The record pull.** The Georgia Secretary of State business search at ecorp.sos.ga.gov is free and shows the legal name, control number, formation date, standing, registered agent, officers and every annual registration filed. The DeKalb County Clerk of Superior Court records portal at deeds.dekalbcountyga.gov searches deeds by grantor and grantee; plain copies are $1 a page, certified $2.50 for the first page and $0.50 thereafter. The statewide GSCCCA index at search.gsccca.org covers every county, with images on a subscription of $14.95 a month or certified copies ordered one at a time. Find the original Declaration under the name of the developer as grantor around the platting date and trace amendments forward under the name of the association.

**The petition.** A 14-3-1604 application is filed in the civil division of DeKalb County Superior Court at 556 North McDonough Street, Decatur, through mandatory e-filing, as a drafted petition rather than a published form. The civil filing fee is in the region of $213 and wants confirming. The court must act on an expedited basis. An individual may file without counsel; the association must answer through a lawyer. An agent who is not a lawyer cannot file or appear for the member – the petition is brought by the member in person or through counsel, however much of the preparation the agent has done.

#### 10. Where to get help

Atlanta Legal Aid Society serves DeKalb residents who meet income guidelines and runs a home-defence programme; confirm whether an association dispute fits intake before relying on it. The DeKalb Bar Association lawyer referral service is on 404-373-2580. Homeowner-side association counsel in metro Atlanta bills in the region of $325 to $450 an hour, and some firms quote a flat fee for a demand letter. The Secretary of State division and the Attorney General consumer-protection channel are free and build a paper trail even where their teeth are still arriving.
