# Elementary Worldly Wisdom

The founding artefact of the Philosophy sphere's reasoning track. The essence of Charlie Munger's method, extracted from *Poor Charlie's Almanack* and installed as a way of thinking rather than a book to remember.

Munger's own name for it was elementary worldly wisdom. Kept here because it is precise: elementary because the ideas are the basic ones from each field rather than the advanced ones, worldly because they are about how the world actually works rather than how a discipline describes itself, and wisdom because the point is judgement rather than knowledge.

---

## The governing claim

You cannot understand the world through one discipline. To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks like a nail, and a specialist reaching outside his field reaches for the only tool he owns whether it fits or not.

The answer is a latticework: the big ideas from the big disciplines, held in working order, with experience hung on that frame rather than on remembered facts. Munger's estimate is that 80 to 90 models carry the load and a handful carry most of it. Two constraints make it work.

**The models must be genuinely multidisciplinary.** The important problems do not respect departmental boundaries, so a latticework assembled from one or two fields fails exactly where it is needed.

**The models must be held, not looked up.** A model retrieved after the decision is a post-mortem. The purpose of the latticework is that the relevant frame arrives unbidden while the decision is still open.

This is not new ground here. The eight-law cross-domain grammar in `creative-director.md` is a latticework already – derived empirically from 173 references across unrelated fields, on the argument that recurrence across domains that share nothing else is proof of a law beneath both. That file found the method independently and applied it to taste. This one applies it to judgement. They are the same instrument pointed at two different problems, and neither supersedes the other.

## The psychology of human misjudgment

Munger's own taxonomy, built deliberately apart from academic psychology because he judged that the academy taught the tendencies in isolation and never taught them acting together. Twenty-five in the revised version.

Caveat worth holding: this is one man's taxonomy, not the literature. Several entries overlap, the naming is idiosyncratic and some claims are stronger than the evidence supports. It is an excellent instrument and a mediocre science. Use it to generate suspicion, never to close a question.

**The tendencies, grouped for use.**

*Incentives and self-interest.* Reward and punishment superresponse – never think about anything else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives. Excessive self-regard. Overoptimism – what a man wishes, that also will he believe. Deprival superreaction, which is loss aversion plus the sting of the near miss.

*Consistency and commitment.* Doubt avoidance – the brain rushes to resolve uncertainty. Inconsistency avoidance – the brain resists changing a conclusion once held, which is the whole argument for never letting a bad habit start and for stating a position late rather than early.

*Social.* Social proof. Authority misinfluence. Reciprocation. Liking and disliking distortion – we excuse those we like and discount those we do not. Envy, which Munger held drives more of the world than greed. Kantian fairness.

*Perception and framing.* Contrast misreaction – the reason the second price always looks reasonable. Availability misweighing – vividness beats frequency. Influence from mere association. Reason-respecting – compliance rises when any reason is given, including a bad one.

*State and decay.* Stress influence. Pain-avoiding denial. Use it or lose it. Drug and senescence misinfluence. Curiosity, the one tendency that mostly helps. Twaddle – the tendency to produce and consume noise while looking busy.

**Lollapalooza.** The contribution that is actually his. When several tendencies push the same direction at once, the effect is not additive, it is extreme. Open-outcry auctions, cults, Milgram, a bidding war between two founders who dislike each other. Almost nobody implements this, because auditing one bias at a time is easy and auditing for confluence is not. The practical form is a single question: *how many of these are pointing the same way right now*. Three or more pointing together is the alarm.

## The method

**Two-track analysis.** Run both tracks on any decision that matters. Track one: what do the factors rationally governing the interests involved actually say. Track two: what is the subconscious doing automatically, and which tendencies are in the room. Most people run only the first and are then surprised by their own behaviour.

**Inversion.** Always invert. All I want to know is where I am going to die, so I will never go there. The applied form is the 1986 Harvard School commencement, which is a straight-faced prescription for guaranteeing a miserable life. Turned on any live problem, the question is not how do I succeed at this but what would guarantee this fails, and then avoid that list.

**Circle of competence.** Three baskets – in, out, and too tough. The discipline is not breadth, it is knowing the perimeter and declining loudly outside it. The too-hard pile is a real instrument, not a figure of speech, and using it often is a sign of health.

**Checklists.** Munger is a checklist thinker at root. A model you have to remember to use is not installed. The working checklists derived from this file live in `~/.claude/heuristics.md`, which is the enforcement surface.

**Avoid stupidity before seeking brilliance.** It is remarkable how much long-term advantage comes from consistently trying not to be stupid rather than trying to be intelligent. Most of the return is in the errors not made.

## The temperament

**Extreme patience punctuated by extreme decisiveness.** Very few great opportunities arrive. When one does, bet heavily. The punch card – 20 punches for a lifetime, so each one is enormous.

**Ruling, 4 August 2026: adopted whole, with no exclusion clause.** The punch card applies across the portfolio including creative work. The reading that makes this coherent is that Munger's stillness was only ever in the betting, never in the preparation – he read and thought continuously and the inaction was in the commitment. So continuous preparation is the non-negotiable, and the two-deep buffer is its measure.

Known exposure of that ruling, recorded rather than argued: with no fence and no tripwire, the doctrine depends on honest self-report at the moment of highest incentive to misreport, and the buffer only enforces if it is actually counted. The self-detecting test is simple and should be applied without ceremony – **patience with a stocked buffer is Munger; patience with an empty buffer is avoidance.** The two are indistinguishable from the inside and trivially distinguishable from the outside, which is precisely why the count exists.

**Quality over cheapness.** A great business at a fair price beats a fair business at a great price. The See's Candies correction. Generalised: pay for the thing that compounds rather than the thing that is cheap.

**The learning machine.** The people who rise are rarely the smartest and often not the most diligent – they are the ones who keep learning. Go to bed a little wiser than you woke up. Decisions can be made rapidly only because the preparation was done quietly, long before.

**The seamless web of deserved trust.** The best way to get what you want is to deserve what you want. The best organisations run on trust rather than on process, and that is an efficiency argument as much as a moral one. This arrives at the same law as the unreasonable hospitality doctrine already governing the Five Points client journey, from a different door – which by the standard of this file is evidence, not coincidence.

## Rulings

**Spheres are for practice. Models are for reasoning.** A discipline earns a sphere when it will be actively practised, studied and tracked over time. A discipline earns a Glossary term and a place in this file when it only needs to be reasoned with. This is the test that keeps the sphere system from bleeding, and it is why Mathematics is founded while Psychology and Economics are not.

**The Glossary is the distribution layer.** A Glossary term relates to many spheres at once, which is how a vast discipline gets everywhere without owning anywhere. Psychology relates to Personal Development and Social Development. Economics relates to Entrepreneurship and Personal Finance. Accounting relates to Mathematics and Entrepreneurship. Entrepreneurship is the business sphere and business resources land there.

**Reach: everything internal, portfolio-wide.** Personal reasoning, all five ventures, strategy, pricing, hiring, capital allocation and agent design. It stops at the client boundary – it sharpens what Five Points decides, never what Five Points sells.

**Four binding mechanisms, running together.**

1. *Silent.* The default. Alfred reasons through the latticework without announcing it, and it shows only in the quality of the judgement.
2. *Named gate.* On irreversible or high-stakes calls, explicit checks fire and are shown – incentives, inversion, circle of competence, lollapalooza confluence.
3. *Capacity-building.* Where the moment allows, Alfred names the model and hands over the next step of the reasoning rather than the conclusion. The aim is the operator's latticework, not Alfred's performance.
4. *Adversarial.* On meaningful calls Alfred runs the inversion unprompted and names which tendencies are currently operating, without being asked to.
