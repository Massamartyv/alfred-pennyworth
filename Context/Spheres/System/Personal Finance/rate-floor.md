# Rate Floor – The Personal Reservation Price

> **The floor is $2,500 a day.** This is not a price list. It is a reservation price – the number beneath which the answer is no. The hourly figures throughout are private costing instruments and never appear in a conversation with a buyer. The Five Points fingerprint invariant holds unchanged: lead with the value equation, never with the hourly rate. This document exists so that the number is read off a page rather than decided under pressure, which is the whole point.

---

### Spheres Covered

---

Personal Finance. Adjacent to Entrepreneurship, which holds venture pricing. This file governs the operator as an independent individual only. The Five Points internal labour rate of $100 per hour and its 75% gross margin floor are a separate instrument and are not amended here.

---

### Provenance of the framework

---

The Designers Union guide was the requested framework. It exists, and it is not what it appears to be.

| Attribute | Finding |
|---|---|
| Actual title | Official DU Design Minimum Price List |
| Author | Hadeel Sayed Ahmad, founder of Designers Union |
| Body | An Arab-region design community based in Amman, Jordan. Not a US professional association |
| Published | 30 June 2018 – eight years old |
| Numeric table | Could not be extracted. Every accessible copy renders the price table as an image |

Its numbers are therefore unverified, dated and not calibrated to the US market. They are not used here.

**What does transfer is its thesis**, stated in its own words: these are "not the average pricing" but "the suggested minimum prices a designer should charge." A floor, not a rate card. That concept is the spine of this document and it is sound.

**The working method is AIGA's**, which is current, US-calibrated and arrives at the same place:

1. Break-even rate equals total overhead divided by billable hours.
2. Add a target margin of 10 to 20 percent.
3. Sanity-check against peer and survey rates.
4. Never charge beneath the calculated floor.

### The ruling – $2,500 a day

---

> **The hard floor is $2,500 a day. No exceptions, no ladder, no strategic discounts.** Set by operator ruling on 2026-09-08 after a three-round interview. Reaffirmed once after the risks below were put plainly.

| Term | Ruling |
|---|---|
| **Floor** | **$2,500 a day** |
| Hourly equivalent, private only | $500 an hour at five billable hours |
| Smallest sellable unit | **One day.** Nothing smaller is sold |
| Quoted as | Project fee, day rate or retained month. **Never hourly** |
| Escalation | **Plus 25% every three closed engagements.** $2,500, then $3,125, then $3,906 |
| Response to pushback | **Hold the rate, offer terms.** The number never moves, the payment schedule does |
| Repetition protocol | **Publish it.** The page names the number so it is confirmed rather than declared |

**Why the number is coherent.** At $2,500 a day, **10 billable days a month** clears $25,000 – the first degree of sovereignty. At $1,500 it takes almost 17. Ten days leaves the rest of the month for the ventures, which is the stated terminal picture: the studio day, no operations, no pipeline. The high floor is not appetite. It is the only version that buys back the calendar.

**Why it is fragile.** The interview recorded a flinch beginning at $75 to $100 an hour. This floor sits at five times the top of that range. A hard rule broken once teaches that the rules do not hold, which is more expensive than a lower floor kept. The escalation clause compounds a number that already sits at the top of the market.

**The named risk.** A recommendation to split the figure – quote $2,500, floor at $1,250 – was put and declined. The floor stands as ruled.

### What the floor demands of the offer

---

The hourly bands throughout this document describe how the market prices **labour**. This floor is never quoted as labour, so those bands are not ceilings on it. The operative test changes.

> Not "does this discipline bill at $500 an hour" but **"is there an outcome here a buyer will pay $2,500 for in a day"**.

That test is far more permissive, and most disciplines pass it once packaged as a result rather than as time. A sales page is not four hours of copywriting. It is a sales page.

| Hat | Sellable as labour at this floor | Sellable as a packaged outcome |
|---|---|---|
| AI and automation consulting | **Yes** – high band $300 to $600 an hour | Yes, comfortably |
| Photography | At the very top only – high band reaches $500 | Yes, once the kit exists |
| Copywriting, SEO, brand identity, Notion systems | No – high bands run $250 to $300 | **Yes** – as a defined artefact with a stated result |
| Web design, web development, project management | No | Marginal. Bundle into a larger outcome |
| Creative direction | No – high band $100 | Never priced alone. Priced into the outcome |
| Video, social, outbound, bookkeeping | No | No. **Buy these** |

**The consequence to accept.** Every engagement must now be scoped as a result with a price, before the conversation. Hourly thinking is what makes a $2,500 day feel indefensible, and outcome thinking is what makes it obvious.

---

### The decision rule

---

> Any hat whose market rate sits beneath the floor should be bought, never sold. The arbitrage is buying hours at $30 and selling days at $2,500, and it is the mechanism by which 10 billable days a month reaches $25,000.

Three verdicts are used below. They are calibrated against the derived tiers, since those describe the labour market. Read them alongside the packaging table above, which is what actually governs a quote.

- **SELL** – market high clears $250 an hour. Package as an outcome and it clears the floor.
- **HOLD** – clears the reservation floor but not sovereignty. Bundle it, never sell it alone.
- **BUY** – sits at or beneath break-even. Outsource it. Doing it personally destroys value.

### Channel discount – read before quoting anywhere

---

Rates are not portable across channels. A floor calibrated to marketplace-visible pricing underprices direct work by more than two times at the top end.

| Channel | Fee to you | Rate reality |
|---|---|---|
| Direct and agency | 0% | Full market band applies |
| Contra | 0% to freelancer – client pays the platform | Closest to direct |
| Upwork | 0 to 15% | Sits at or beneath the market low band. Creative direction median $35 against $50 to $100 direct |
| Fiverr | Flat 20% | Lowest. Structurally a discount channel |

**Rule.** Marketplaces are for repetitions and proof, never for setting the floor. Nothing on a marketplace establishes what the work is worth.

### Interview record – 2026-09-08

---

The floor was set by structured interview rather than by calculation alone. The findings govern how it is defended.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| The number wanted | $300 an hour, equivalent to $1,500 a day |
| The number ruled | **$2,500 a day**, above the want |
| Flinch threshold | **$75 to $100 an hour** – at or beneath break-even |
| Gap between want and flinch | Three to four times |
| The fear | **Legitimacy and judgement.** Not exposure – the fear of having to deliver was explicitly rejected |
| The unlock | **Demand and repetition**, chosen over proof |

**The two findings that matter.** The fear of delivery was ruled out, which means the confidence in the work is intact and only the claim about the self is contested. And demand plus repetition were chosen over proof, which is correct – a legitimacy fear discounts evidence, so external permission and sheer repetition work where a case study does not.

**Why the day rate helps.** $300 an hour and $1,500 a day are the same number and not the same act. An hourly figure is a claim about what each minute of the operator is worth. A day rate is a price for a thing. Against a legitimacy fear specifically, the second is materially easier to say – which is the argument for the published rate card doing the declaring.

### Maintenance

---

- Recalculate whenever fixed commitments move by more than 10 percent, and at every annual review alongside `benchmark-ledger.md`.
- Market bands are 2025 to 2026. Refresh annually – they date quickly, as the Designers Union guide demonstrates.
- Billable hours at 25 a week is an operator ruling, not an observation. If the actual figure proves lower, every number here is understated.
- The tax rate is an estimate. Confirm it with an accountant before it is relied upon.
- Full research and citations: `.working/rate-floor/research.md`.

- **Five Points incompatibility, recorded not resolved.** The studio sells a Recovery Week at $970 and an AI Receptionist setup at $3,000. At $2,500 a day one personal day exceeds two and a half Recovery Weeks, and a two-day receptionist build consumes the whole fee. The scopes were ruled separate on 2026-09-08 and the venture labour rate of $100 an hour is untouched. The conflict is live and needs its own ruling.
- **Escalation tracking.** Closed engagements must be counted somewhere durable, since the floor moves on the third. Notion, not this file.
