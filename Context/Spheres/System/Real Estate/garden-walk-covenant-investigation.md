Three things follow from that table before a single document is read.

First, this is a full-service association. It insures and maintains the buildings and pays the water for every unit. That puts it in the class of associations where the money is large, the volunteer board is unpaid and the temptation is real. It also puts it in the class where honest costs have risen fastest. Both readings are live.

Second, at $295 to $335 the assessment is more than double the metro Atlanta median of $135 a month, but the median is a single-family figure that buys landscaping and a gate. It is the wrong comparison. The right comparison is a full-service townhome association of the same vintage, and on that comparison the figure is ordinary.

Third, the association sues. Two owners reached the Court of Appeals against it inside 18 months and both lost on procedure. An association that pursues collections to summary judgment is assertive, which is unremarkable on its own – but it also means an owner who withholds dues in protest will be the defendant, not the plaintiff. Nobody in this investigation withholds a dollar.

### Acting as agent

---

The agency is a division of labour with a hard edge, and the edge is worth knowing before the first envelope is posted.

- **The member signs; the agent is named.** The demand in Appendix A goes out over the signature of the owner with the authorisation enclosed. Have the authorisation notarised – a bank does it for a few dollars – so the association has no reasonable basis to doubt the authority of the agent. That doubt is the single defence under 14-3-1604 that would let a refusing board escape paying the fees of the member.
- **The agent does the work.** Attends the inspection, copies, receives the response, runs the tests, keeps the evidence folder and writes every same-day email. The letter directs the association to answer both the member and the agent.
- **The agent cannot go to court for the member.** A 14-3-1604 petition to compel, a 14-3-810 petition to remove a director and any suit are filed by the member in person or through counsel; a non-lawyer cannot appear for another person in a Georgia court. From step eight of the sequence, plan on the member filing papers the agent has prepared, or on a lawyer.
- **Meetings and votes are member acts.** The five per cent demand for a special meeting, the removal vote and the 10 per cent petition are signed by owners. The agent organises; the owners sign.
- **The member carries the exposure.** A board that feels examined looks hardest at the account of the owner who asked. Keep that account current to the day, and get the informed consent of the member before the first letter, in writing, because the first fine or the first lien lands on their lot and not on the agent.
- **One channel may be open to the agent directly.** The reporting on SB 406 says the Secretary of State division takes complaints from any person residing in a covered community, not only owners. If that survives a reading of the division page, a resident agent can file in their own name from 2027, and possibly now. Confirm before relying on it.

### The hypothesis, stated precisely

---

"Corrupt and pocketing money illegally" is a conclusion. The investigation has to separate four states of the world that look identical from the kerb:

| State | What it is | Standard of proof | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Misappropriation | Money leaving the association for the private benefit of someone – theft by taking or conversion | Beyond reasonable doubt for a prosecution; preponderance for a civil claim | District Attorney, civil action, fidelity-bond claim |
| 2. Self-dealing | Contracts, fees or employment steered to directors, relatives or allied firms at above-market terms | Preponderance – a conflicting-interest transaction not properly disclosed and approved is voidable | Derivative action, removal, rescission |
| 3. Negligent management | Books not kept, reserves not funded, no audit, no budget, money leaking through carelessness rather than intent | Breach of the standard of care | Removal, court-ordered inspection, new board |
| 4. Opaque but lawful | The money is accounted for and spent legitimately; the board simply has not shown anyone | None – the cure is disclosure | A records demand |

The known facts are consistent with every one of the four. That is the honest position on 2026-09-15. Most homeowner suspicion of this kind resolves to state 3 or 4 once the books are opened. A material minority resolves to state 2. State 1 is rarer, and when it occurs it is almost always a single trusted person with sole control of the bank account over several years. The sequence below is designed to tell them apart cheaply, in order, without a lawyer until one is needed.

### Scale – the first forensic test

---

Every other test hangs off one number: what the association should be collecting.

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Units | 122 to 134 |
| Assessment | $295 to $335 a month |
| Expected annual assessment income | $432,000 at the floor, $539,000 at the ceiling – call it half a million dollars a year |

Against that income, a plausible honest budget for a 1972 full-service townhome community in DeKalb County carries these lines, in descending order of size: water and sewer for every unit, structure insurance, roofing and exterior maintenance, cable contract, grounds, pool and clubhouse, pest and termite, administration and any management fee, legal and collections, and a reserve transfer. Two of those lines have moved sharply and honestly:

- DeKalb County raised water and sewer rates by 10% in July 2025 and will raise them 10% every January from 2026 to 2034. A community that pays the water for every household absorbs that in full.
- Structure insurance on older multi-family buildings rose by double digits a year through 2023 and 2024 and has moderated to single digits for well-maintained, low-claim buildings in 2025 and 2026.

So a dues increase alone proves nothing. What proves something is the relationship between the income the units should produce and the income the statements report, then the relationship between the statements and the bank.

### What would make the case – the evidence ladder

---

The case is built in rungs, and each rung is cheaper than the one above it. Nobody climbs past a rung that came back clean.

**Rung one – refusal and lapse. Costs a letter.**

- The Secretary of State register shows the corporation administratively dissolved, or missing its 2024, 2025 or 2026 annual registration, while it went on collecting, fining and liening. The authority of a lapsed corporation to do any of that is a live question in Georgia, and from 2027 an unregistered association forfeits it outright.
- The written records demand in Appendix A is ignored, refused or answered with a spreadsheet and no source documents past the five business days. Under 14-3-1604 the refusal itself becomes a cause of action with the fees of the member riding on it.
- No annual financial statement can be produced at all – not even an officer-certified one under 14-3-1620.
- No annual meeting and no election in the last two years, with no minutes to show otherwise.

**Rung two – documentary anomaly. Costs the reading of what rung one produced.**

- Assessment income on the statements sits more than five per cent below units times dues with no receivables aging to explain the gap.
- A vendor, the manager or a payee on the check register shares a name, an address or a registered agent with a director or a relative of one on the Secretary of State register.
- No fidelity bond, no crime policy, or a declarations page the carrier will not confirm.
- Bank reconciliations missing, stale by months, or carrying a variance that grows rather than closes.
- Fines, late fees and attorney's fees together above roughly 10 per cent of income, or a lien pattern in the deed index that outruns the stated collection policy.
- "Miscellaneous", "legal" or "other" lines that do not trace to invoices.
- Operating and reserve money in one account, or reserve money moving without a minuted capital decision.
- Board minutes that never mention the contracts and payments the ledger shows.

**Rung three – proof. Costs an agreed-upon-procedures engagement with a CPA, roughly $5,000 to $15,000, shared across a coalition.**

- Bank statements obtained from the bank, not from the board, that do not tie to the ledger.
- Cheques or transfers to individuals, to a director, to the manager or to an entity the Secretary of State register ties to one of them.
- Fabricated invoices a vendor disowns when telephoned, as at Magnolia Walk in South Fulton.
- Altered statements, as at Rosewood in Virginia, or undisclosed accounts in the name of the association, as at Cedarbend in Utah.

Rung one is available to a single owner acting alone. Rung two needs only the documents and a weekend. Rung three is where a coalition and a professional enter, and it is entered on a number, not on a feeling.

### What would break the case – the disconfirming evidence

---

If the board, when actually tested, produces the following, the honest conclusion is under-managed or under-funded rather than corrupt, and the fight becomes a governance fight – better candidates, a management agreement pressure-tested, a reserve study commissioned – rather than a matter for the District Attorney.

- A complete budget for each of the last three years, circulated or not, with actual-to-budget variance that names its causes.
- Assessment income that reconciles to units times dues once the receivables aging is applied, with a delinquency rate under 10 per cent and every delinquent account traceable to a lot.
- Bank statements pulled from the bank that tie month by month to the ledger.
- Any CPA report – compilation, review or audit – for a recent year, commissioned by someone other than the person under suspicion, with a clean or merely qualified opinion.
- A reserve study, however old, with the reserve balance within a defensible distance of its recommendation, or an underfunding that is disclosed and attached to a funding plan.
- Current declarations pages for liability, property, directors-and-officers and fidelity cover, confirmed in force by a telephone call to the carrier.
- Contested elections and different people on the board over the years, however thinly attended.
- Vendors that come back clean on the Secretary of State register and contracts that show some evidence of bidding.
- Dues increases that trace to the two lines everyone can verify independently – the DeKalb water rate and the insurance renewal – and to roofs on 54-year-old buildings.
- A records demand that is met in substance, even if slowly.

Most suspicions of this kind end here. That outcome is not a loss. It converts a grievance into a governance programme with a known cost, and it leaves the operator holding the documents that make the next election winnable.

### The forensic tests

---

Ten tests a lay reader can run on the documents rung one produces. Thresholds marked with an asterisk are constructed, not published benchmarks, and are directional.

| Test | Arithmetic | Becomes suspicious when |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Expected revenue | Units × monthly assessment × 12, against the assessment-income line | Gap above 5% not explained by the receivables aging |
| 2. Delinquency | Assessments receivable ÷ annual assessments billed | Above 10% wants explaining; above 15% the community is unwarrantable for federally backed mortgages |
| 3. Punitive income | Fines plus late fees plus recovered attorney's fees ÷ total income | Above roughly 10%* |
| 4. Management fee | Annual management fee ÷ units ÷ 12 | Above roughly $40 a door a month for a community this size without documented scope |
| 5. Reserves | Reserve balance ÷ fully funded balance per the reserve study | Below 30% is critical; no study at all is itself the finding |
| 6. Officer against vendor | Every payee on the check register run through the Secretary of State register by name, officer and agent | Any shared name, address, officer or agent with a director, the manager or a relative |
| 7. Check register scan | Sort by payee, then amount | Recurring round numbers with no contract behind them, duplicate payees days apart, payments to individuals, gaps in the cheque sequence |
| 8. Bank against ledger | Bank ending balance each month against ledger cash after outstanding items | Any variance unresolved inside a month, or one that grows |
| 9. Insurance plausibility | Premiums paid against the declarations pages, then a call to the carrier | A policy the carrier will not confirm; directors-and-officers cover under about $700 a year |
| 10. Expense drift | Year-on-year change per line over three to five years | Any line outrunning general inflation of three to five per cent without a minuted cause, with "legal" and "miscellaneous" first* |

Test eight is worth the other nine. Two of the documented cases – a manager who fabricated the bank statements shown to the board, and a board that had never once looked at the actual bank account – were concealed by nothing more than the gap between what the books said and what the bank held.

### The pattern from the documented cases

---

Nine documented cases were read, five of them in Georgia. Mirror Lake in Villa Rica: a property manager wrote herself bonuses and vacation pay for six years, $498,815, caught when a new board looked at the bank account for the first time. Provincial Park in Carrollton: a 77-year-old treasurer, personal expenses and a forged bank document over three years, charged with theft by conversion and forgery. Magnolia Walk in South Fulton: a president reimbursed himself $7,000 on a fake invoice for cameras that were never installed, caught when an owner telephoned the vendor. Camelot in South Fulton: a director forged two signatures on a quitclaim deed to move a unit into her own name. Channing Cove in Rockdale: no criminal charge at all, only spreadsheets in place of source documents, bylaws altered without votes and fines without basis since 2011, ending in a $40,000 settlement to one owner in 2025. Beyond Georgia the management-company cases run larger and longer – $1.1 million across 70 associations in Virginia, $1.79 million over seven years in Minnesota, $2.1 million in Utah – because a manager holds standing, unsupervised access to many accounts at once and volunteer boards defer.

Three things hold across all nine. The person who steals is either a volunteer officer taking small sums for a short time or a professional manager taking large sums for years. The money moves by the dullest routes – self-authorised payments, fake invoices, transfers mislabelled as ordinary expenses, altered statements, accounts nobody knew existed. And not one case was caught by the annual process of the association. Every one was caught by a handover, a vendor, a bank or a lawsuit. That is the argument for doing this yourself rather than waiting for the board to find its own problem.

For Garden Walk the pattern points at two people before anyone else: whoever holds the signature card on the operating account, and whoever runs the office at 651 Garden Walk Drive. If a management company is on the coupon, it is the higher-risk actor on the statistics, and the failure of the board to check its work is what would have let it run.

### The sequence

---

The protocol, in order. Each step is a single pomodoro or names the next action that is. Where the matter stands on this ladder is state and lives in Notion.

0. **Seat the agency.** Standing is settled – you act as agent for a resident owner. Confirm the member is on the deed for a lot in Garden Walk, obtain the signed and notarised authorisation at the foot of Appendix A, confirm in writing that the member understands the exposure and check that their assessment account is current to the day.
1. **Pull the corporate record.** Search Garden Walk Homeowners Association at ecorp.sos.ga.gov. Record the exact name, control number, formation date, standing, registered agent, listed officers and the years of annual registration filed. Screenshot it. Ten minutes.
2. **Pull the governing documents.** Search deeds.dekalbcountyga.gov for the Declaration under the developer as grantor around 1972 and forward for amendments under the association. Read for three things: fee-simple lots or condominium; any express submission to the Property Owners' Association Act; the assessment, lien, special-assessment and meeting provisions. Buy the plat for the unit count. About $30 and an hour.
3. **Collect what the house already holds.** Coupon books, any budget or newsletter, meeting notices, the last dues increase letter, any fine or violation notice, the closing package from 1994 if it survives. Search the iCloud inbox for the association by name. Scan everything into one folder in personal Google Drive and index it in the Notion project.
4. **Ask informally first.** Walk to the office at 651 Garden Walk Drive or email the board address on gardenwalkneighborhood.com and ask for the current budget, the last two years of financial statements and the last annual-meeting minutes, without citing a statute. A board that hands them over has answered rung one. Note the date and the response either way.
5. **Serve the demand.** If step four produces nothing inside a week, send Appendix A by certified mail with return receipt and by email to the registered agent from step one, the board and any manager, with the inspection date at least five business days out. Keep the receipt.
6. **Read against the tests.** Run tests one through ten on what arrives. Enter every document and every finding in the evidence folder and the Notion project.
7. **Build the coalition.** With the findings, and not before, take the two or three sharpest numbers to the Facebook group and to neighbours door to door. The targets are five per cent of the votes for a special meeting under 14-3-702 and 10 per cent for a court petition under 14-3-810 – on 122 to 134 units that is seven and 14 owners. Also file individual records demands from three or four of them, so refusal cannot be dressed as one difficult owner.
8. **Compel if refused.** If the demand is refused or ignored past a reasonable time, file the 14-3-1604 petition in DeKalb Superior Court. Fees shift to the association unless it proves a reasonable basis for doubt. In parallel, file a complaint with the Property Owners' Associations Division of the Secretary of State within 180 days of the refusal.
9. **Escalate on the number.** Anomalies at rung two go to a CPA for agreed-upon procedures scoped to the tests, paid by the coalition. Proof at rung three goes three ways at once: a report at the DeKalb County Police precinct with the District Attorney to follow, a claim on the fidelity bond and a special meeting called under 14-3-702 with removal under 14-3-808 on the agenda, or a 14-3-810 petition if the meeting cannot be won.

### The lines not to cross

---

- **Pay the dues in full and on time throughout.** The association sues, it wins on procedure and it recovers its fees. Withholding turns the investigator into the defendant.
- **Name no one in writing until rung three.** "The board" and "the association" until the documents say otherwise. An accusation against a named person before the evidence is defamation exposure and it hardens the room.
- **Nothing on the Facebook group before step seven,** and then only numbers with their source, never adjectives.
- **Sign nothing from the association** acknowledging a balance, a fine or a payment plan without reading it against your own ledger.
- **Everything in writing.** Every conversation at the office gets a same-day email summarising it.
- **Confirm every citation** in `georgia-hoa-law.md` against the current code before it goes into any letter or filing. The file was compiled from search reproductions, not from the statute.

### Questions only you can answer

---

Each of these changes what happens next, and none of them can be found in the public record.

1. Is the resident who will sign the record owner – of 631 Garden Walk Drive or of another lot – with the deed in their name, and have they agreed in writing to carry the demand?
2. What did you actually see that made you say "pocketing" – a dues increase, a fine, a lien, a refusal, a rumour, an absent meeting, a person? Be specific about the document or the moment.
3. What association paper do you hold today – a coupon book, a budget, a newsletter, a meeting notice, a violation letter?
4. Is a management company named on the coupon or the payment portal, and if so which?
5. When was the last annual meeting you know of, and who was on the board then?
6. How many neighbours share the suspicion, and are any of them already owed an answer from the board?
7. Is the iCloud inbox where association mail arrives?
