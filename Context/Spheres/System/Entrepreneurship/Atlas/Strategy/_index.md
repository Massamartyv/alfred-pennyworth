## Strategic Priors

The following beliefs are baked into the venture thesis:

1. **Augmentation beats replacement.** Chiropractors do not switch EHRs easily. Atlas integrates with the EHRs of the market rather than competing with them.
2. **The defensible edge is chiropractic-specific clinical knowledge.** Generic medical AI hallucinates on Gonstead listings, misses diversified technique nuance, fumbles PI documentation. Knowledge curation is the moat.
3. **Distribution is the under-discussed problem.** Chiropractors do not buy SaaS the way oncologists do. The product needs an embedded channel from day one – pilot, association network, founder-led sales.
4. **Critique is load-bearing.** Clinical documentation is irreversible. Every output passes binary validation against the per-mission contract before release.
5. **The EHRs themselves will build AI eventually.** The window is the time it takes them to do it badly. Atlas wins by being chiropractic-native while they are health-system-generic.

## Open Strategic Questions

1. Which EHR is the initial integration target? (Likely Jane App based on Arlando profile; confirm post-discovery)
2. What is the wedge customer profile? (Sports / wellness modern practice vs PI-heavy practice vs Medicare-heavy practice)
3. Is the pricing model per-clinic flat fee, per-provider seat, per-note transaction or hybrid?
4. Does Atlas compete with or partner with EHR vendors as those vendors build their own AI?
5. What is the path to network effects? Is there one beyond curated knowledge accumulation?
