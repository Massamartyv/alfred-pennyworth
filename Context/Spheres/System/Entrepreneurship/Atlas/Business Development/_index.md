# Business Development – Atlas

EHR landscape, competitive positioning, chiropractic market behaviour, pilot expansion, channel architecture and partnership development. The thinking behind the work and every path from a working product to a paying clinic that is not a straight sale.

The distribution thesis at Atlas is shaped by the distribution problem in chiropractic SaaS: this market is relationship-driven, association-driven and trust-driven. Founder-led sales for the first 10 to 50 clinics sits in Marketing & Sales; the channel, partnership and pilot architecture that makes those sales possible sits here.

## Distribution Strategy

The phased channel architecture:

### Phase 1 – Pilot site

Arlando Parker Jr.'s clinic. No paid customers. Goal is to validate the product against real visits, generate testimonial-grade evidence and accumulate the first batch of curated clinical knowledge against real notes.

### Phase 2 – Founder-led sales

First 10 to 50 paid clinics. Operator-driven. Warm intros through Arlando network, chiropractic associations and the conference circuit. No SDR delegation. Pricing established by deal, refined to a published model after the first 25 clinics.

### Phase 3 – Network channel

Chiropractic association partnerships. State board education programmes. Podcast appearances. Conference sponsorships in second year. Goal is to be the AI layer of choice when a chiropractor asks an association which tool to use.

### Phase 4 – Partnership plays

EHR vendor partnerships – Jane App and Prompt EMR are likely partnership candidates (modern API surface, modern customer base, less competitive threat). Marketplace integrations. Co-marketing arrangements.

### Phase 5 – Distribution at scale

Once Atlas has a meaningful share of chiropractic AI documentation, distribution flips. The EHR vendors themselves want to ship Atlas because their customers ask for it. The conversation becomes acquisition versus continued partnership.

## Strategic Priors

The following beliefs are baked into the venture thesis:

1. **Augmentation beats replacement.** Chiropractors do not switch EHRs easily. Atlas integrates with the EHRs of the market rather than competing with them.
2. **The defensible edge is chiropractic-specific clinical knowledge.** Generic medical AI hallucinates on Gonstead listings, misses diversified technique nuance, fumbles PI documentation. Knowledge curation is the moat.
3. **Distribution is the under-discussed problem.** Chiropractors do not buy SaaS the way oncologists do. The product needs an embedded channel from day one – pilot, association network, founder-led sales.
4. **Critique is load-bearing.** Clinical documentation is irreversible. Every output passes binary validation against the per-mission contract before release.
5. **The EHRs themselves will build AI eventually.** The window is the time it takes them to do it badly. Atlas wins by being chiropractic-native while they are health-system-generic.

## Open Strategic Questions

1. Which EHR is the initial integration target? (Likely Jane App based on Arlando profile; confirm post-discovery)
2. What is the wedge customer profile? (Sports / wellness modern practice vs PI-heavy practice vs Medicare-heavy practice)
3. Is the pricing model per-clinic flat fee, per-provider seat, per-note transaction or hybrid?
4. Does Atlas compete with or partner with EHR vendors as those vendors build their own AI?
5. What is the path to network effects? Is there one beyond curated knowledge accumulation?
