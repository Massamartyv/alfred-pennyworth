# A Friend Of Mine

**Who** – A Friend Of Mine (AFOM) is a Melbourne-based graphic design studio. The Bayport project is a case study in the studio's approach to total brand identity and collateral production: every designed artefact – from business cards to proposal documents to printed matter – is governed by a rigorous set of production commitments applied consistently across every piece.

**Why aligned** – AFOM is the single most operationally load-bearing external reference in the Five Points brand fingerprint. The Cross-Layer Principles section names "Seven principles extracted from the A Friend Of Mine Bayport case study, applied to Five Points within the locked framework." These seven AFOM Execution Principles are not references to draw from – they are governing production rules embedded directly into Five Points operations. Every document Five Points produces is held to them.

The seven principles as applied to Five Points:

1. **Total colour commitment per piece.** Each artefact floods one dominant colour. Obsidian for the commissioning register. Parchment for the working register. Emerald for the continuation register. Burnt Orange kept strictly for eyebrows and marque detail.
2. **A mark that scales from 8px to 400mm.** The commissioned five-circle mark must hold identity at every rendering size.
3. **The wordmark lives on covers, not on letterhead.** Letterhead carries only the mark and a contact cluster.
4. **Asymmetric editorial spreads.** Full-bleed image on one page, narrow text column on the other. Never 50/50. Never centred.
5. **Corner-anchored contact typography.** Contact clusters live in one corner. Nothing runs the full width as a header or footer.
6. **Physical craft as signature.** Edge painting in the opposite colour to the face on every printed piece.
7. **Photography is either full-colour document or total monotone.** Never half-treatment.

**The proposition** – That a set of production commitments applied with total consistency across every artefact is what distinguishes a studio that has a brand from one that has a visual identity. The Bayport case study demonstrates this: every printed piece made for that engagement is immediately identifiable as belonging to the same body of work, not because they all look alike, but because they all carry the same set of decisions. The mark scales cleanly. The contact information is always in the same corner. The colour floods rather than accents. The edges are painted. These are not aesthetic choices – they are production contracts.

**Canon** – The A Friend Of Mine Bayport case study (the source document from which all seven principles are extracted). AFOM's broader portfolio of identity and collateral work.
