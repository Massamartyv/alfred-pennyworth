# Monocle

**Who** – Global affairs, business and culture magazine and media company founded 2007 by Tyler Brûlé. Long-form print journalism distinguished by a specific photographic register: the subject photographed in the context of their work, often from a distance, rarely filling the frame, usually engaged with an object or environment rather than addressing the camera.

**Why aligned** – Monocle is one of nine canonical photography references in the Five Points image library. Its specific contribution is named as "people-at-work register" – the grammar of how people appear in Five Points imagery. The fingerprint states: "When people appear they are in the context of their work, never centred, never eye-contact." This is the Monocle portrait grammar translated directly into Five Points policy.

In the Monocle aesthetic, the human figure exists in relationship to the work rather than as the subject of a personality photograph. A craftsman is photographed mid-action. A principal is photographed at their desk, reading, not performing. The camera is a witness, not an interviewer. This aligns precisely with the Five Points brand personality attribute of "Withholding – reveals on proximity, not from across the room."

**The proposition** – That a person is best understood in relation to what they make or where they work. The direct-gaze portrait performs the person; the Monocle-register portrait reveals them. In professional services brand communications – which Five Points produces and sets the standard for – this grammar communicates trust over aspiration: the principal is shown in their actual state of competence, not a constructed version of their personal appeal.

**Canon** – Monocle long-form feature photography (issues 1 through present). The characteristic wide shot: a figure in a workshop or studio, depth in the frame, objects in the foreground, the subject in the mid-ground engaged with their work. The typology interview: subject photographed in their environment rather than extracted from it. The city guides: local proprietors photographed in their establishments at the scale of the establishment, not cropped to their face.

**Pull for** – photography, art-direction, visual-direction, people-photography guidelines, thought-leadership-content, case-study-photography.

**Trend read** – Pending enrichment. The contextual portrait grammar has been adopted broadly in professional-services brand photography as a contrast to the influencer-aesthetic portrait, but Monocle's level of compositional discipline and environmental integration remains a high bar few brand photographers reach in practice.
