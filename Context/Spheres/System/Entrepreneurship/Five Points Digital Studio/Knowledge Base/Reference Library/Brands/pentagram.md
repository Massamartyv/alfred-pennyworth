# Pentagram

**Who** – The world's largest independent design consultancy, founded 1972, structured as an equal-partner practice of principals who each run their own studio within the collective. Covers graphic design, identity, architecture, product design and interactive work across five offices.

**Why aligned** – Pentagram earns its place in the Five Points corpus on three distinct grounds. First, the studio's partnership architecture is the direct structural model for Five Points – the fingerprint explicitly names "Pentagram-inspired partnership model" as the governance blueprint: principals, not headcount; sovereignty within a shared mark; scale without dilution of bespoke. Second, Pentagram is the editorial register arbiter for the studio, named in Decision Default 6 alongside Aesop: "When in doubt about register, ask what Pentagram or Aesop would do." Third, the Pentagram case study photograph – object-centric, architectural, quiet – is one of nine canonical photography references governing the Five Points image library.

**The proposition** – The proof that a design practice can be simultaneously independent, peer-structured and world-class. No managing partner above the partners. Each principal accountable for their own roster. The collective lends authority; the structure preserves autonomy. The brand does not belong to a founder or a holding group – it belongs to the principals who constitute it.

**Canon** – Pentagram founding as a collective of five partners in London (1972). The sustained five-partner-per-office model across decades of industry churn. The body of case study documentation: object on a neutral ground, single light source, architectural precision – a photographic grammar that treats every designed object as worth the same quiet reverence.

**Pull for** – positioning, proposition, visual-direction, photography, art-direction, offer-design, partnership-model references, editorial-register decisions, case-study layout.

**Trend read** – Pending enrichment. The partnership-model claim is structural rather than trend-sensitive – it does not date. The editorial register question is more current: Pentagram's design language continues to set a high floor for what composed, object-respecting design communications look like in the premium professional services space.
