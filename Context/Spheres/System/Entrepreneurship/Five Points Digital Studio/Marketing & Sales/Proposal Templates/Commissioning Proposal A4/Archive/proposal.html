02
What You Told Me

The conversation,
continued

You went out on your own in 2000 and ran eight years without a website, because the work spoke and the neighbourhood listened. Twenty-six years on, the phone rings more than one man can answer. You stay scheduled out. You cannot bring yourself to tell a caller no, because that is not how you are built.

You like the business small and you want it busy enough to hire help again, the way it was before 2008. You compete on quality and nothing else. You have watched the all-in-one companies become conglomerates, and you have no intention of becoming one or working like one. You have paid for marketing that returned nothing. Growing your reviews feels impossible. And the rainbow stripes are not decoration.

Here is what I heard underneath all of it: you are oversubscribed and underleveraged at the same time. More customers would make your week worse. Control over the demand you already have would change the business.

This document is that conversation, continued on paper.

Reconnaissance

What the evidence shows

Before writing a word of this, the studio spent a day reading your public footprint the way a new customer would. Three findings matter. I will walk you through all of it on the call.

Your current circuit, as found
01

Booking

Every direct competitor sampled offers a live booking path on its website. Yours offers a phone number and a form. For the customer searching at 7pm, that difference decides who gets hired – and every call that rings out is billed time lost at your own rate of $100 an hour.

02

Reviews

Your reputation is flawless everywhere it is visible – five stars on every platform sampled, customers who call you a unicorn. It is also nearly invisible. The private equity brand in your category shows 2,239 Google reviews. The problem was never satisfaction. It is capture: nobody asks your delighted customers at the right moment.

03

The house details

Two different phone numbers on one website. Hours that disagree with your own listings. A licence claimed but never displayed by number. Small things – and small things done right are your entire argument as a business.

You do not need more customers. You need a system that protects your time from the customers you already have.

The Two Prices

What yes costs,
and what no costs

The price of yes

$1,000 to $3,000, once.

Choose the door that fits. Each is complete in itself, priced before it begins and finished when the machinery demonstrably works. No subscriptions, no renewals, nothing that bills quietly.

Nothing is charged to commission. The studio builds first – you pay at the handoff demonstration, after you watch the booking take an appointment and a missed call answer itself by text.

The price of no

Declining costs nothing today. It costs what it cost last month: roughly a third of missed calls booking whoever answered instead, delighted customers never asked for the review, the house details unrepaired. And whatever a monthly subscription promises, a year of it is rent – gone the day the payments stop. What this studio builds, you own.
