# Marketing & Sales Quality Rubric

The four non-negotiable criteria that govern all creative output from this department. Every piece of work is evaluated against all four during the Critique phase. Failure on any single criterion sends the work back to Execution with specific revision notes.

---

## 1. Brand Alignment

**The question:** Does it feel like us – or like the client it represents?

### Excellent
- Voice, visual and messaging are indistinguishable from the brand's best existing work
- Reinforces and deepens the brand's positioning
- Someone familiar with the brand would recognise it instantly without seeing the logo

### Acceptable
- Broadly consistent with brand guidelines
- No obvious voice or visual contradictions
- Feels within the brand's territory but does not sharpen it

### Failing
- Contradicts established voice, tone or visual identity
- Could belong to any brand in the category
- Feels generic or defaulted rather than intentional

---

## 2. Originality

**The question:** Is it distinctive? Would you stop scrolling?

### Excellent
- A genuine point of view that could only come from this brand
- Avoids category conventions, template-feeling output and safe choices
- Surprises without confusing – fresh but comprehensible
- References culture in a way that is specific and earned, not decorative

### Acceptable
- Has a clear perspective but does not push into new territory
- Competent and professional but not remarkable
- Would not embarrass but would not be remembered

### Failing
- Reads like it was generated without a brief
- Relies on cliches, stock phrasing or category defaults
- Indistinguishable from competitor output
- Feels templated or interchangeable

---

## 3. Craft and Finish

**The question:** Are the details precise? Would a master notice nothing out of place?

### Excellent
- Typography is properly scaled, spaced and hierarchical
- Colour values are exact and intentional
- Copy rhythm is deliberate – sentence length varies with purpose
- Word choice is precise – every word earns its place
- Layout and spatial relationships feel designed, not defaulted
- Passes the grammar audit without exceptions

### Acceptable
- Technically correct and properly formatted
- No obvious errors in typography, colour or copy
- Functional but not elevated – the details are fine, not exceptional

### Failing
- Spelling, grammar or punctuation errors
- Inconsistent formatting or visual treatment
- Sloppy spacing, alignment or hierarchy
- Word choice is imprecise, redundant or lazy
- Feels rushed or unfinished

---

## 4. Emotional Resonance

**The question:** Does it make you feel something?

### Excellent
- Provokes a genuine emotional response: curiosity, desire, conviction, delight, recognition
- The audience sees themselves in it or aspires to what it represents
- Earns attention rather than demanding it
- Stays with the audience after they move on

### Acceptable
- Communicates clearly and pleasantly
- Does not repel but does not attract
- Information is delivered but feeling is absent

### Failing
- Feels transactional or clinical
- Actively boring or alienating
- Talks at the audience rather than connecting with them
- No hook, no tension, no reward

---

## Applying the Rubric

### Scoring

Each criterion is evaluated as **Excellent**, **Acceptable** or **Failing**.

- **All Excellent:** Release with confidence.
- **Mix of Excellent and Acceptable:** Release if no criterion is Failing. Flag Acceptable areas for future improvement.
- **Any Failing:** Return to Execution. Specific revision notes required for each Failing criterion. Do not release.

### Context sensitivity

The bar adjusts by stakes, not by convenience:

- **Client-facing deliverables:** All four criteria must be Acceptable or above. Excellent is the target.
- **Internal documents:** Brand Alignment and Craft and Finish still apply. Originality and Emotional Resonance may be relaxed for purely functional work.
- **Social content:** All four apply, but Craft and Finish accounts for platform conventions (brevity, informality).

### The Editor's responsibility

The Editor does not fix. The Editor evaluates. When work is returned to Execution, the revision notes must be specific:

- **Not:** "The copy needs work."
- **Instead:** "The headline is category-generic – it reads like any agency could have written it. Needs a sharper angle that only Five Points would take."

Vague critique produces vague revision. Precise critique produces excellent work.
