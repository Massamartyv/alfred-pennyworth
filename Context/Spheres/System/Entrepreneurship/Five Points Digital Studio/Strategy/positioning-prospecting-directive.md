## 1. The positioning (the niche, planted)

**Five Points designs operational intelligence for premium service businesses in the lifestyle, luxury, and stewardship sectors** — high-margin, relationship-driven operations that serve affluent clientele.

The core offer is **Human Construct**: operational intelligence design, delivered on a rolling three-slot capacity model.

### The strategic frame: the systems inflection point

Every business in this niche eventually hits the same wall. Around **$1M in annual revenue**, the founder-led model breaks. Manual processes that worked at small scale collapse under volume — inventory, compliance, pipeline, staffing, client data. The business is too big to run by feel and not yet built to run on systems. Owners feel this as chaos hiding beneath a polished surface.

That gap — between the excellence they *present* and the systems they actually *run on* — is what Five Points closes. We don't sell marketing. We design the operational intelligence that lets a premium operator scale excellence without losing control or quality.

This is the niche: **narrow enough to plant a flag, broad enough to never run dry.** One sensibility — operational intelligence design — applied across many premium verticals.

## 2. Ideal Client Profile (ICP)

**Revenue band:** $1M minimum annual revenue. Sweet spot $1M–$50M. *(Touchpoint A resolved 2026-06-02: ceiling raised from a working $20M to ~$50M. Floor locked at $1M.)*

**Why the floor matters:** Below ~$1M, there isn't enough margin or wiggle room to fund the work or to see meaningful absolute growth from it. We optimize for *absolute* lift, not percentage lift — it is more valuable to take a $1M operator to $5M than a $100K operator to $1M, even though the latter is a larger percentage. We want clients whose numbers can reflect the size of the jump we create.

**Business characteristics:**
- High margin
- Relationship-driven
- Serves affluent clientele
- Premium-priced, excellence-dependent
- Operationally complex beneath a polished surface
- At or approaching the systems inflection point

**Values filter (non-negotiable):** Five Points works with operators whose practices align with our own ethics. We screen *out*:
- Businesses built on extractive or exploitative models
- Anyone violently oppressing communities
- Anyone abusing the environment at scale for profit

We screen *for* operators who value ethical practice and stewardship of the earth as much as profit. This is a qualifier, not a nicety — it goes in the prospect scoring.

**Capacity reality:** Human Construct runs on a rolling three-slot model. This is the operational reason prospecting must be **signal-driven, not volume-driven** (Section 6). With three slots and a high-ticket offer, the cost of chasing low-signal leads is high. We need a small number of high-fit, high-readiness prospects — not a firehose.

## 3. Target verticals

All five are messaged **simultaneously**. Each gets positioning tailored to its specific operational anxiety, but all sit under the same umbrella: *premium service operators managing complexity at scale while protecting the personalized experience that justifies their price.*

Ranking below is **speed-to-deal and TAM**, not priority — priority is equal across all five.

### 3.1 Luxury Real Estate Agents — *speed: 1 (fastest) · TAM: very large (~2M+ licensed US agents + international)*

Decision-maker is the individual agent or small brokerage owner. Short sales cycle (weeks).

Operational pain points:
1. **Precision & data readiness.** Today's luxury buyer is younger, global, analytical — they've already researched flood zones, seller purchase price, insurance claims, roof age before the first call. An agent without those numbers organized and ready loses credibility instantly.
2. **Pipeline architecture & conversion velocity.** Managing buyers, sellers, and prospects across different decision stages without losing anyone to a competitor who showed up at the right moment.
3. **Messaging mismatch.** Marketing that still leads with square footage instead of lifestyle, wellness, sustainability, and smart-home integration is already behind. Luxury buyers want narrative fit, not specs.
4. **Staff retention & performance visibility** (brokerage level). No way to surface real performance data or systematize the local-market knowledge that makes top agents outperform.
5. **AI adoption friction & ROI justification.** They know AI exists but are paralyzed between wanting it and not being able to prove ROI or integrate it across fragmented systems.

### 3.2 Medical Spa Owners — *speed: 2 · TAM: solid (~6,000+ North America, growing)*

Single owner-operator decision, usually. Moderate cycle (4–8 weeks once they see the gap).

Operational pain points:
1. **The $1M scaling wall.** At ~$1M revenue, manual processes break — inventory, staff, compliance, and patient follow-up all need systems at once. This is the niche's inflection point in its sharpest form.
2. **Staff turnover & training.** ~40% of med spas cite staff shortage as their top challenge. Turnover kills operations and patient trust; you can't systematize training that was never documented.
3. **Regulatory compliance.** Laws vary widely by state. A single misstep can mean fines (up to ~$500K in some jurisdictions), disciplinary action, or loss of license. Compliance is fragmented and manual.
4. **Patient data & follow-up architecture.** As services expand into weight, hormones, longevity, owners must coordinate prescriptions, labs, and follow-ups — complex sequencing nobody's systematizing.
5. **Labor-cost optimization without losing the high-touch experience.** Redeploying labor strategically (the way clubs shift to grab-and-go) while protecting the personalized service that justifies premium pricing.

### 3.3 Golf Courses & Country Clubs — *speed: 3 · TAM: constrained (~16,000 globally, ~9,000 US)*

Decision-maker varies between GM and board — governance slows the cycle.

Operational pain points (seed — expand during messaging):
1. **Amenity complexity.** Each new member program adds unique booking rules, scheduling constraints, and billing mechanisms — operational drag that compounds.
2. **Labor optimization.** Industry-wide labor shortage forcing redeployment (grab-and-go, micro-markets, pre-prep) without degrading member experience.
3. **Fragmented systems.** POS, scheduling, billing, and member data living in disconnected tools — the integration problem.
4. **Sustainability as operational mandate.** Water, energy, pesticide, and labor-practice expectations are now table stakes, especially for attracting younger members.
5. **Underperformance diagnostics.** Clubs struggling to identify where margin leaks live and how to optimize against demand.

### 3.4 Homebuilders — *speed: 4 · TAM: decent but fragmented (mostly small builders)*

Owner is decision-maker but capital-constrained and risk-averse — longer cycle.

Operational pain points (seed — expand during messaging):
1. **Pipeline & timeline coordination** across long, multi-stakeholder build cycles.
2. **Supply chain & labor volatility** causing delays that ripple across the whole development pipeline.
3. **Cost-tracking precision** against tightening margins.
4. **Client communication architecture** across a months-to-years relationship.
5. **Decision-cycle drag** from cautious, capital-intensive operations.

### 3.5 Jet Rentals / Private Aviation — *speed: 5 (slowest) · TAM: tiny (~500–1,000 operators globally)*

Owner is decision-maker but operationally paranoid — trust-building is the whole game; long cycle.

**Strategic note:** We pursue this one as a long game *on purpose*. Out of ~1,000 operators we only need one relationship. The clientele behind a jet operator — people who travel privately — open doors across ultra-high-net-worth networks that ripple into every other vertical. One relationship here is disproportionately valuable. Build patiently; do not measure this vertical by speed.

Operational pain points (seed — expand during messaging):
1. **Scheduling & crew logistics** complexity.
2. **Maintenance tracking & compliance** in a highly specialized regulatory environment.
3. **Client data & discretion** — managing relationships with clients who demand precision and privacy.
4. **Real-time fleet & availability coordination.**
5. **Margin protection** in a high-cost operation.

## 6. Production Note 1 — SIGNAL-DRIVEN, NOT VOLUME-DRIVEN

**This is a hard gate, recorded so it is never lost.**

Before any prospecting or signal-detection system is installed and put into operation for Five Points, Alfred must validate that the system is **signal-driven, not numbers-driven.**

The system identifies prospects by *quality indicators that someone actually has the operational gap* — not by hitting a target count of outreaches. Examples of legitimate signals (final set to be decided when we design the system): a relevant job listing posted, a public expansion announcement, an operational-pain tell, a new-location opening, a leadership change, a tech-adoption signal.

The capacity reality reinforces this: three slots, high-ticket offer. We are looking for the few right operators, not the most operators. Any system that optimizes for volume or for hitting minimums fails this gate and does not ship until corrected.

**Alfred: do not deploy a prospecting/signal system to production without confirming it passes this gate.**
