## 1. Verdict

**The offer architecture is intellectually superb and commercially unproven. The thinking is finished; the machine is not built; the market has never been touched.** The three-door ladder is validated, elegantly named and correctly mapped to the Ascending Transaction Model. Underneath it, on the surfaces a buyer actually touches, the audit found: a dead domain, dead email, an undeployed site, a checkout estate carrying three contradictory generations of products, no rail for two of the three doors and zero card revenue in the entire history of the account.

The binding constraint on Five Points is not offer design. It is that the system keeps producing architecture instead of transactions.

## 3. The consultant panel

### The inversion read – what would guarantee failure

Four of the five failure conditions are the present state. Nobody can find the studio – the domain is dark. Nobody can pay the studio – two doors have no rail. Every sale requires the founder to personally perform the ask – the 25 July ruling moved all pricing into the sales room. The first paid proof never lands – zero charges. The fifth condition, the estate refining doctrine instead of transacting, is the standing pattern: between 7 July and 26 July the venture executed at least five doctrine passes – pressure test, three-door prune, Commission rename, Evolution governance reframe, price-privacy ruling – against zero market contacts. The failure list is not hypothetical. It is the current configuration, and the correct response is not more architecture.

### The incentive read – why the machine behaves this way

Architecture work is pleasant, controllable and praised at every gate. Market contact is unpleasant, uncontrolled and carries rejection risk. The system – operator and orchestrator together – has been optimising for what its incentives reward. The estate's own psychology file names the ask, the price and the cadence as the avoided acts, and the estate's own decision trail then acted the pattern out in real time: on 24 July the Three Doors decision was adopted with the explicit rationale "priced doors perform the ask on the operator's behalf"; the same decision explicitly rejected the no-public-ladder option because it "reinstates the avoided acts". Within a day, the 25 July ruling moved every price into the sales room – substantially the rejected option, reinstalling the founder as the required performer of every ask at every rung. Three tendencies point the same way – commitment to the doctrine corpus, discomfort avoidance, the authority of past research inviting one more study before acting. Per the house lollapalooza rule, three is the alarm.

This is not an argument that the ruling was wrong everywhere. It is an argument that it was wrong at the bottom of the ladder – see §5, Finding 2.

### The focus read – the cut was decided, not made

The 39-to-3 prune is precisely the 1997 product-grid move and it was correctly ruled. But a cut only exists where the buyer can see it, and on every surface a buyer touches the cut has not happened: Stripe still sells three generations at once, the deployed site still carries the pre-prune architecture, the offer files still speak Bronze and Gold. Meanwhile the whole widget – domain to site to checkout to confirmation email to delivery – is broken at the first link. The standard here is simple: no further strategy artefact ships until one stranger can travel the entire path and pay. Deciding what not to do was done; the not-doing remains.

## 4. What is right and must not be touched

1. **The decimal ladder** – $197 / $1,970 / $19,700 in tenfold steps. Market-validated at every rung by the pressure test, mnemonic, and structurally mirrors the market's documented failure sequence – train, scope, build.
2. **The door names.** The Survey, the Commission, Evolution – map it, commission it, compound it. Noun-true, task-free, marque-consistent. Among the finest naming decisions in the estate.
3. **The Priestley mapping.** Gift below, Platinum reserved above the $35,000 floor, principal-only, continuity beyond. The reserve discipline on Human Construct is exactly right.
4. **The private menu.** Thirty-nine offers demoted to internal delivery architecture behind three doors – right, and the quoting discipline document is genuinely strong.
5. **The Evolution formula.** Twenty percent of commission value annually, billed quarterly, quoted as a number never a formula – inside the 10–25% industry norm for asset stewardship, and the governance anatomy gives the fee a named deliverable.
6. **The $11,500 Custom Website Build commit** – benchmark-derived, one number, flex floor, revisit trigger attached. This is what a disciplined pricing decision looks like; it is the template the rest of the estate should copy.

## 5. Findings ledger – severity ordered

**Finding 1 – the commercial layer is dark. Severity: critical, operational.**
NXDOMAIN on the apex zone kills the site, all @fivepoints.studio mail – including systems@, finance@ and every Clay outbound reply path – and any checkout journey that begins at the domain. Known dark since at least 5 August; still dark on the 8th with a Camp session today. Nothing else in this dossier matters until the zone resolves. Fix: restore delegation at the registrar, repoint to the Vercel zone, verify MX, then execute the overdue Quintessence cutover.

**Finding 2 – the 25 July price-privacy ruling swept the wrong rungs. Severity: critical, strategic.**
The marque posture – price in conversation – is correct for the Commission, Platinum and Evolution: Rolls-Royce does not list the Phantom. It is wrong for the Survey and Camp, whose entire design function is self-qualification and self-serve conversion without the founder in the loop. The current configuration prices every rung in a founder meeting: throughput is capped at the founder's calendar, the ask is reinstated at every level and the sovereignty objective – the operation running without Marty – is inverted at the exact rung built to serve it. Recommendation: re-split. Survey and Camp priced and buyable publicly – the machine performs the ask. Commission and above priced in the sales room – the marque performs the mystique. Attach a Dalio-grade override log to the ruling either way: a measurable revisit trigger, for example "if qualified discovery calls from the unpriced site run below N per month by 30 September, the Survey price returns to the public page". The CWB decision carries exactly such a trigger; this ruling, which is larger, carries none.

**Finding 3 – the Survey promises less than its price was validated against. Severity: high.**
$1,970 was validated for diagnosis plus architecture roadmap plus one working automation shipped – the "something working" that separates the tier from the $500–$750 audit shelf and answers the trust law that deals die when price cannot be tied to a bounded unit of expert judgment. The live Survey product ships a document. At document-only scope the offer sits 2.6x above the audit shelf carrying the audit shelf's deliverable, and the folded Blueprint thickens the document without changing its category. Fix: restore one shipped working automation to the Survey stack – the clickable-prototype close the voice-of-customer corpus says approves projects within 24 hours – and name the duration and the principal's time cost on the offer surface. The value equation currently states no time-to-value and no effort term.

**Finding 4 – two doors have no rail. Severity: high.**
No Commission product, no Evolution product, no subscription or quarterly-invoice mechanism exists in Stripe. The sales room cannot take a card for the two largest doors without improvising, and Evolution – the studio's only compounding revenue mechanism, on a venture whose spine sentence is about compounding – cannot bill at all. The founding clients' bundled hosting windows expire roughly September–October with no Evolution commissioning on record: the first continuity revenue is already leaking. Fix: stage the Commission as a Stripe product with milestone payment links (50/25/25 per the Human Construct precedent), build Evolution as a quarterly subscription or scheduled-invoice rail and commission both founding clients into Evolution before their bundled windows lapse.

**Finding 5 – the Stripe estate contradicts the brand at checkout. Severity: high.**
Six active recurring retainer prices on a no-retainer venture, "base price scales to" ranges on a fixed-price venture, Bronze/Gold/Platinum public names on a three-door venture, two different products sharing the number 2.1, retired Camp term products still purchasable. Any buyer or auditor reading the account meets the studio the canon says never existed. Fix: archive every pre-door product and price in one sitting – roughly 30 archives, an hour of work. The catalogue lives on in the offer files; Stripe is a public surface and follows the private-menu channel rules.

**Finding 6 – the ladder's credit mechanic survives only in the Sessions pillar. Severity: medium.**
The pressure test's clearest adoption recommendation – the paid diagnostic credits in full toward the build, converting the engagement from expense into deposit – lives on in the two Sessions files at a clean 60-day standard: the Camp seat credits toward the $1,970 rung, the Command Session fee credits toward the $19,700 Build. It is absent from the door documents themselves: no Survey-credits-toward-Commission rule exists in the private-menu quoting discipline, the Three Doors decision or Stripe, and the older catalogue files still carry 90-day and 30-day windows. Fix: write the Survey-credits-toward-Commission rule into the quoting discipline at the Sessions pillar's 60-day standard. One sentence; it is the strongest conversion lever the research found.

**Finding 7 – the first Camp session opens today into a dark funnel. Severity: medium, time-boxed to this morning.**
One comped registrant, no paid seats, no resolving domain to sell the remaining nineteen. Run the session regardless – the cadence claim has to be paid for, the recording and Field Kit are assets, and one delighted comped attendee from a live prospect (Renegade Golf) is worth the three hours. But hold the weekly cadence honestly against the two-deep buffer rule: a weekly public commitment with no distribution engine behind it is the empty-buffer pattern. Either wire distribution this week – domain, Camp page, Pennyone syndication of the session cut – or re-announce the cadence as fortnightly until two sessions of demand sit in the buffer.

**Finding 8 – the offer catalogue does not know the doors exist. Severity: medium structurally, high for agent operations.**
The file-level sweep (appendix: `offer-catalogue-sweep-2026-08.md`) found zero occurrences of the Survey, the Commission, Evolution or the 20% formula anywhere in the 62-file Product Development tree – while the Growth index instructs that "all pricing must reference Product Development/ offer files – never quote from memory". An agent obeying that instruction quotes the dead architecture: 59 of 62 files still speak Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum, ten files price monthly retainers, the root index codifies "the retainer sweet spot ($5K–$8K/mo)" as doctrine, Human Construct is documented as a four-tier public ladder with open-enrolment Bronze against its principal-only reserve status, Evolution reads $3,000/month flat in three files and the offer count is wrong three ways – prose 39, registry table 41, disk 46 distinctly priced offers. Adjacent drift: the Growth index still names the retired "Bronze AI Fluency Workshop" as the front door and carries a third ICP variant; the ATM memory carried the dead Evolution price until corrected today; the 2-versus-3-hour workshop note in the pressure test remains open, with Camp shipping 3. Fix: one reconciliation session – either rewrite the catalogue's front matter to door vocabulary, or place a single governing README at the tree root stating that the catalogue is the private delivery menu, tier names are internal codes and door pricing lives in the quoting discipline. The second is an hour's work and stops the bleeding.

**Finding 9 – one number without a benchmark, one repositioning without a mandate. Severity: low.**
The $10,700 enterprise session floor has a documented home – the Command Session offer file, Enterprise Rung 1, filed 8 July – but no benchmark trail behind the number; it sits plausibly inside the validated $3,000–$12,000 team-delivery band, so a one-line basis note in the Decision Log settles it. The sharper item: the 25 July decision's framing that "the studio presents as a digital marketing house at heart with AI supercharging the systems" contradicts Layer 0's invariant – the asset-and-commission position, "not a retainer shop, not a deliverables vendor" – and rode into canon inside a pricing decision. Strike it or reconcile it through the fingerprint amendment process. Positioning changes do not travel as passengers.

The $25,000-a-month first degree is one Commission, three Surveys and a functioning Camp – **provided the Survey converts without founder throughput and Evolution actually bills.** Both provisos are currently false, which is why Findings 2 and 4 outrank every pricing question. Evolution is the compounding rung: at 20% annually, every $100K of cumulative commissioned work adds roughly $20K of annual recurring governance revenue – the enterprise-value line the spine sentence promises. It compounds only if commissioned; it is currently unbillable.

## Part II – the transcript pass

Source: Corner Office Podcast episode, Corey of Return My Time on the AI assessment business, supplied by the operator 2026-08-08. The transcript describes an independent operator running the commodity version of the Survey rung. Treated as a single practitioner account – Tier 3 evidence, one voice, incentivised to sell the dream – but dense with operational detail that converges on the estate's own validated findings, and independent convergence is the strongest signal the pressure test found.

### What the transcript independently validates

1. **The ladder shape and the climb.** His pricing walked free → $200 → $500 → $1,000, with the explicit regret that cheap entry "shoots you in both feet" because the $1,000 anchor makes the $3,500 upsell "in the ballpark". This is the ATM logic arriving by lived experience, and it confirms the refusal to enter at $200 – he is still climbing toward the shelf the Survey already occupies.
2. **Bought-in buyers ascend.** "They're paying you to be upsold" – his observed psychology at $1,000 – is Finding 6's credit mechanic and the entire Priestley premise, confirmed from the field.
3. **The naming instinct.** He renamed audit to assessment because buyers hate being audited. The estate made the same move a register higher – the Survey. One residue: the free gift rung still carries the name "AI Audit" on the /audit route; consider whether the gift inherits the softer register too. Operator's call, one line, low stakes.
4. **The ICP behaviour gate.** His fit rule – teams of five to 50, owner not solo, works across industries – matches the lookalike engine's AI-active operator profile and the pressure test's behaviour-over-vertical finding (C8 resolves in the same direction).
5. **The revenue-tied upsell rule.** His "find the upsell most closely tied to revenue" is the Two Prices rule stated from the other side of the table. His flagship example – a speed-to-lead agent justified by an $11,000 average deal at a 40% tour close rate – is arithmetically the inaction ledger.

### What the transcript teaches that the estate should adopt

1. **The Intake Construct.** His diagnostic runs with zero founder time – a voice agent conducts the discovery conversation, callable at any hour, and the transcript pipes to an agent chain that drafts the report. This is Finding 2's sovereignty argument solved in the wild, and for a studio whose thesis is Constructs it is also the best sales artefact conceivable: the thing interviewing the prospect is the thing the studio builds. The Quintessence branch already carries stepped intake questionnaires; the Survey's discovery layer becomes a Construct, the founder appears at the walkthrough – the moment where the marque lives. This lifts the Survey ceiling from three a month to ten without touching the founder's calendar.
2. **The report anatomy.** His deliverable leads with quantified financial impact – hours reclaimed times owner rate minus tool cost – then an effort-versus-impact matrix isolating quick wins, named tools with prices, a four-day implementation plan and a booked walkthrough, delivered inside 48 hours. Import the anatomy wholesale into the Survey document spec, rendered to the A-series marque standard, with the financial-impact block at the top as the hook. The Survey currently states no turnaround; his 48 hours is the bar.
3. **One productised first Commission.** His discipline – specialise in a single upsell before widening – names what the estate already possesses: the speed-to-lead and lead-recovery build exists twice in the estate (Renegade Golf, McCauley) with scripts and proposals written. Make it the named bread-and-butter Commission for service operators; the catalogue's breadth stays behind the private menu.
4. **Offline founder-authority channels.** His seven no-audience channels – the local AI meetup hosted in a partner's office, door-knocking as conversation, free assessments to a CEO peer group for testimonials, standing office hours – require no domain, no site and no ad spend. Given Finding 1, they are the only channels currently operational, and every one of them feeds Camp Pennyworth directly. The realtor-office partnership pattern maps cleanly onto the existing switchboard and dial-block infrastructure.

### What the transcript warns of – and what to decline

**The commodity floor is forming under the Survey.** This episode hands thousands of listeners a free template, a tool list and a seven-channel acquisition playbook for a $1,000 assessment. The moat cannot be the assessment itself. It must be what a Corey-class operator cannot ship: the marque document, the Construct depth, the owned-asset handover and Evolution governance behind it. Two consequences. First, Finding 3 sharpens – a document-only Survey at $1,970 now competes against a multiplying cohort of $1,000 document sellers, so the one-installed-quick-win close or the visible marque experience must carry the doubled price; restore the working artefact. Second, the recovery sequence gains urgency – his cohort is being taught to move this quarter.

**Decline the rest of his stack.** White-label SaaS resale and recurring software margins are the retainer temptation in new clothes and fail the asset canon; template deliverables fail the marque; his volume posture is the opposite of the reserve discipline that holds the Platinum floor. The transcript is a map of the downmarket mirror – proof the upmarket bet is right, provided the machinery that justifies the premium becomes visible to buyers, which returns every thread to Finding 1.
