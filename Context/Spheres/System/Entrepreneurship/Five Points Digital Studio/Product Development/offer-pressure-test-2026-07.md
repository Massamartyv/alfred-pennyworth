## 1. Verdict

**The ladder survives the pressure test. All three price points are market-validated — but each carries a condition, and the ladder works only as the public conversion machinery in front of the existing architecture, not as a replacement for it.**

| Tier | Verdict | The one condition that matters |
|---|---|---|
| $197 workshop | Validated. Median for comparable single-session workshops is ~$200; an independent operator (Foundari) converged on exactly $197 with no visibility into Five Points | Must be sold on personal authority (founder as instructor) with a value stack — the agency brand alone has no proof of conversion at this price |
| $1,970 engagement | Validated — genuine white space. Audits cluster $500–$750; pilots and builds start $1,500–$5,000; nothing owns the gap between | Must be outcome-framed and fixed-scope; a block of hours at this price triggers the exact distrust pattern that kills deals |
| $19,700 build | Validated — sits inside the $15K–$50K "first serious implementation" consensus, above Atlanta boutiques ($5K–$10K), below the enterprise floor (HatchWorks $25K+) | Must be scoped as a multi-system architecture with fixed price and timeline, and positioned as Gold — not Platinum — to preserve the $35K Human Construct floor |

The three-rung shape itself has evidentiary backing beyond pricing: NBER 2025 shows untrained workforce is a quantified adoption barrier (train it away — workshop), the Journal of Marketing 2025 shows automation underperforms without expert scoping precisely in higher-ticket contexts (scope it — engagement), and McKinsey 2025 shows two thirds of AI-using organizations are stuck in pilot mode (build it — architecture). The ladder is not just a pricing device; it mirrors the documented failure sequence of the market it sells into.

## 3. Tier-by-tier evidence

### 3.1 The $197 workshop

**Market position.** Across 11 reliably priced single-session comparables (Maven workshops, independent operators), the distribution runs $77–$500 with a median near $200 and quartiles at roughly $150 and $370. $197 sits just below median — neither bargain nor premium. The strongest single data point in the entire mission: Foundari, an independent agency running an AI-workshop-for-business-owners offer, independently priced its middle tier at exactly $197.

**Format consensus.** 2–4 hours, live, single session, recording included, plus a named "action plan" or implementation-direction deliverable. Multi-week cohorts price $500–$1,000 as a distinct tier above; $7–$27 self-paced tripwires are a different category below (and the one $197 must visibly not resemble — this band carries the heaviest "is this a scam" skepticism, from buyers already burned by info-product courses).

**Structural findings.**
- Value-stacking is near-universal at this band. A bare $197 ticket with no "here is what you get / total value / your price" breakdown will read as under-justified against category norms.
- Agencies are nearly absent at this price point. It is occupied by solo creators and community hosts trading on personal authority or borrowed institutional credibility. This is upside (an open lane) and risk (no proof agency positioning converts here). The workable template is the personal-authority model: founder as named instructor, agency as the machinery behind him — which also matches the live outbound signal that AI-active founders respond to the operator's personal profile.
- The same deliverable sold to a team reprices dramatically: team workshop sessions run $3,000–$12,000 flat. Buyer, not format, sets the price. A private/team edition of the identical workshop is a second SKU waiting at 15–60x the seat price.
- The market's cleanest $197 comparable (AI for Business) currently runs the workshop free to fund a $97/month membership behind it. Two live playbooks exist: charge $197 as a screened, profitable front end, or give it away and monetize continuity. Charging is the better fit for a premium posture and for the paid-gate-screens-serious-buyers philosophy that Marshland Software argues explicitly — but the choice should be made deliberately, and it is reversible.
- Internal note: a "$197/seat Bronze AI Fluency Workshop" already exists in `Growth/_index.md` – now `Product Development/_index.md`. The hypothesis is not new — it is an existing instinct, now externally validated.

### 3.2 The $1,970 one-to-one engagement

**Market position.** The best-populated, most price-transparent band, and it brackets $1,970 without occupying it. Diagnostics cluster tightly: $500 (That'sGonnaHelp audit), $500 (Innovative Automations 3-hour block), $750 (Promptful half-day audit), $750 (Marshland AI Audit). Builds and pilots start above: $1,500+ (Promptful custom agents), $2,900 (That'sGonnaHelp pilot), $5,000 (Ciphernutz sprint floor). $1,970 sits in the seam — priced above every audit-only offer captured, below every named pilot. To justify the premium over a $750 audit, it must deliver more than diagnosis: diagnosis plus architecture roadmap plus something working.

**Buyer psychology.** Voice-of-customer data places $1,200–$2,800 per engagement squarely inside normal "test a vendor" spend — one buyer spent $16,000 across ten consultants to find two worth keeping. Vendor discovery is a budgeted cost of doing business for this ICP; the engagement tier is where that budget lands. The evidence-backed pitch: the Journal of Marketing 2025 finding that automation underperforms without expert scoping, specifically in higher-ticket considered-purchase contexts. The $1,970 pays for the scoping that determines whether the $19,700 build actually pays.

**The ladder mechanic that recurs across the field:** paid diagnostics credit toward the next tier (Marshland, That'sGonnaHelp, Promptful). Adopt it — the credit converts the engagement from an expense into a deposit.

### 3.3 The $19,700 build

**Market position.** Two disconnected markets share the "AI automation agency" label. Atlanta-local boutiques build single systems for $5,000–$10,000 (iORSO) or $7,500 one-time (AutomateNexus). The one enterprise-visible Atlanta firm, HatchWorks, floors at $25,000 and typically runs six figures. The reference-guide consensus zone for a first serious implementation is $15,000–$50,000. $19,700 lands cleanly inside it: high enough to signal a full multi-system architecture rather than a single-workflow pilot, low enough to duck the enterprise cost structure. The scope claim must do the differentiating — a visibly larger architecture (multiple connected systems, owned by the client) than the boutique single-system builds, or the price reads as 2.6x AutomateNexus for the same thing.

**The cautionary tale sitting at this exact price.** The corpus contains a $46,000 build that took 7–8 months for 3 pages, a "5x longer, invoiced 220% over quote" thread with 119 upvotes, and the "How long is a piece of string?" timeline answer that a buyer remembered for years. Deals at this tier die from unbounded scope, not from price. Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline and "you own everything" (a named differentiator for AutomateNexus and Ciphernutz, and a live objection in the category) are not marketing choices at this tier; they are the difference between closing and becoming someone's Reddit post.

## 4. Reconciling the ladder with the Priestley ATM

The apparent conflict — $19,700 ladder top vs. the operator-gated $35,000 suite-wide Platinum floor — dissolves on inspection, because the two structures answer different questions. The ladder is conversion machinery; the ATM is offer architecture. Mapped onto Priestley's rungs:

| Priestley rung | Ladder element | Price | Visibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gift | AI Audit (existing) | Free | Public |
| Product for Prospects | Group workshop | $197 | Public |
| Product for Clients (entry) | One-to-one engagement | $1,970 | Public |
| Product for Clients (core build) — Gold | Architectural build | $19,700 | Public |
| Platinum — Human Construct | Reserved, application-only | $35,000–$50,000 | Client-only, unlisted |
| Continuity | Evolution retainer | $3,000/month | Client-only |

The decimal ladder becomes the entire public face — three numbers, one page, radical clarity — while Platinum keeps the reserve and gravity the Priestley model prescribes for it ("does not appear on the public site" is already the documented visibility rule). $19,700 is a Gold-rung price and never claimed otherwise, so the $35K Platinum floor survives untouched. This also means the 39-offer catalogue is not retired by this proposal: it becomes the internal delivery menu and custom-scoping reference behind three public doors. The evidence does not support maintaining 39 public offers (the field's strongest converters run 3–4 public price points on a single page — That'sGonnaHelp is the cleanest example, and hidden pricing is the norm only among enterprise-postured firms), but it equally does not demand deleting the catalogue's delivery substance. Recommendation: Tier B Stripe writes stay frozen until this structural decision is taken at the gate.

A second reconciliation the operator should note: public pricing itself is a competitive weapon here. Among Atlanta Band C peers (HatchWorks, The Automators, WSI) pricing is hidden behind contact forms. A published three-number ladder is a local differentiator and matches the productized-operator norm — and it is the posture consistent with pricing from information rather than scarcity.

## 5. Health, wealth, relationships — the outcome-language ruling

The operator's instinct to boil every offer down to how it improves health, wealth or relationships is supported by the voice-of-customer data, with one decisive refinement: **the market speaks health first.**

- **Health (operations, systems) dominates the corpus by a wide margin.** Founders describe the pain as "I am the glue holding this together," "repetitive tasks done differently each time," "she had become the integration layer, and she was the bottleneck." The felt problem is operational chaos and founder-as-bottleneck — business health.
- **Wealth (revenue, pipeline) arrives second-order** — it is how buyers quantify the operations failure once asked: "the real leakage happens between inquiry, qualification, offer, follow-up and the second follow-up." Sales sequencing: lead with the health pain, prove the wealth impact.
- **Relationships is the weakest organic signal** and appears as reputation-defense and customer retention, not audience-building. If used, frame it as trust protected and customers kept, not community grown.

Ruling for offer copy: every tier carries one outcome sentence in the buyer's dominant frame — the workshop restores command of the tools (health), the engagement turns leaking pipeline into a scoped plan (health proven in wealth), the build installs the system that runs without the founder in the loop (health, quantified in wealth, protecting relationships). The services underneath do not change.

## 6. ICP — anchor, challenged

The mission was mandated to anchor on documented ICPs with explicit license to contradict. The contradiction found is not marginal; it is the loudest finding in the dossier.

**What the documents say.** The locked positioning directive (2026-06-02): $1M–$50M firms in five luxury verticals (real estate, med spas, golf clubs, homebuilders, private aviation). Two other live documents carry different bands and different buyers, including Human Construct's individual-founder persona. Full contradiction log in the source inventory (C1–C3, C6).

**What the evidence says.**
- Zero luxury-vertical prospects have converted. The med-spa-adjacent cold pool is marked "CONFIRMED DISPROVEN" in the venture's own lookalike engine as of 2026-07-07 — 0 of 19 connection requests accepted.
- The responders who do engage are AI-active, owner or founder-level, Atlanta over-indexed — closer to the Human Construct individual-founder persona than to any luxury-vertical firm.
- All three actual clients (two churches, one home-services trade) match no documented ICP cleanly.
- The voice-of-customer corpus fractures at roughly $1M revenue: above it, $5,000–$8,000/month retainers read as normal vendor spend, especially for buyers with prior vendor scar tissue; below it, the identical number reads as exploitative.

**What this means for the ladder — and why the ladder is the answer rather than the casualty.** A vertical-locked ICP requires betting on who the buyer is before meeting them; the venture's own data shows that bet missing. The ladder inverts the problem: it lets buyers self-qualify by behavior. $197 admits the AI-curious founder pool that is actually responding to outbound; $1,970 filters for budget-holding seriousness; $19,700 selects for the roughly-$1M-plus segment with enough at stake — the same segment the retainer band needs. The psychographic filter already documented in the offer suite ICP (mission-driven, growth-oriented, AI-curious, investment-minded) plus a revenue qualifier applied at the build tier is a better gate than any vertical list in evidence.

**Decision surfaced, not taken:** whether to formally revise the locked positioning directive. That is an operator call with its own consequences for the prospect map and outbound machinery. This dossier's finding: the directive's vertical bet is contradicted by the venture's own live data, and the ladder functions with or without it — but resourcing (prospect-map effort, Instantly sequences) currently flows toward the disproven bet.

## 7. Pricing from information — the evidence kit

The operator's mandate: articulate pricing from information transference, not scarcity. The kit below survives an informed buyer checking the sources. Six clean anchors carry everything: NBER w34984 (2025, ~750 executives), Brynjolfsson/Li/Raymond (QJE 2025, field experiment), McKinsey State of AI (March 2025), US Chamber Empowering Small Business (August 2025, N=3,870), ICF Global Coaching Study (2025), BLS Productivity and Costs. Full corpus with authority ratings: `.working/offer-pressure-test/value-evidence/evidence-corpus.md`.

**Workshop ($197).** 36% of executives name an untrained workforce as their AI adoption blocker (NBER 2025) — the workshop sells against a documented gap. Novice users gain 34% productivity from guided AI use versus near-zero for the untrained (QJE 2025). One automated bookkeeping workflow saves 12 hours/month (Intuit, April 2025) ≈ $5,000–$6,000/year of owner time against a $197 seat.

**Engagement ($1,970).** Median advisory ROI 7:1 (ICF 2025): $13,790 attributable value on $1,970. Guided AI initiatives return 5.8x within 14 months (McKinsey 2025). Automation without expert scoping underperforms precisely in higher-ticket contexts (Journal of Marketing 2025) — the engagement is the scoping.

**Build ($19,700).** 88% of organizations use AI somewhere; roughly two thirds are stuck in pilot (McKinsey 2025) — competitors have dabbled, not built. Site-performance conversion data (~3x at the 5-second mark; honestly dated 2017–2019) plus a named month-7-payback redesign case. The honest caveat travels with the pitch: no single study isolates the ROI of a full SMB digital-architecture build; the argument is a stack of adjacent, real evidence, and saying so is itself a trust signal with this distrust-primed ICP.

**The blacklist is part of the kit.** The 529% coaching ROI, the $4.53 training ROI, the Learning Pyramid, the undated Stanford credibility stat and the recycled email-ROI figures are flagged do-not-cite in the source inventory. A buyer who Googles a zombie stat and finds the debunk loses the whole pitch; this ICP demonstrably does its homework.

## 8. Trust architecture

The voice-of-customer synthesis reduces to one law: **deals die when the buyer cannot connect the price to a specific, verifiable, bounded unit of expert judgment.** Every collapsed-deal story features unbounded scope, an unverifiable credential or a deliverable indistinguishable from free-tool output. The category carries an extra tax — "if you're an 'AI automation agency,' nobody in the real world knows what you actually do." Countermeasures, each traceable to a named buyer demand in the corpus:

1. Fixed scope, fixed price, fixed timeline at every tier — printed, not negotiated.
2. Outcome names, not process names — "clients don't buy automation; they buy a filled calendar" (an operator who doubled prices on this reframe alone).
3. Business-literate diagnosis before technical talk — the vendor who asked business-model questions first won the $16K buyer's trust "within ten minutes."
4. Demonstrated working output early — "people approve projects within 24 hours just by seeing a real clickable prototype."
5. You own everything — IP and code ownership stated plainly.
6. Willing to say "don't buy" — the Marshland posture; the audit that can conclude AI is not worth your money right now is the audit that gets believed.
7. Named human, named references, video call — anti-AI-laundering proof this buyer now demands by default.

## 9. Proposed offer structure (pending gate)

The proposal, assembled from the evidence. All copy below is directional, not final.

**Public ladder — one page, three numbers, health/wealth/relationships outcome language:**

- **Tier 1 — The Workshop, $197/seat.** 3 hours, live, small group, founder-taught. Recording plus a written action plan per attendee. Value-stacked presentation. Outcome sentence in the health frame. Private team edition of the same workshop, $3,000–$5,000 flat, unlisted but quotable — the market prices team delivery at 15–60x seat price.
- **Tier 2 — The Engagement, $1,970.** Two weeks, one-to-one, fixed scope: business-literate diagnosis, an architecture roadmap, and one working automation shipped (the "clickable prototype" close). Credits in full toward Tier 3 within 90 days. Outcome sentence: health pain, wealth-quantified.
- **Tier 3 — The Build, $19,700.** 6–8 weeks, fixed scope and timeline, multi-system architecture (not a single workflow), client owns everything, capped concurrent slots. Payment staging at milestones. Outcome sentence: the system that runs without the founder in the loop.

**Above the ladder, unchanged and unlisted:** Human Construct Platinum ($35,000–$50,000, application-only, client-only) and Evolution ($3,000/month continuity). The ladder feeds them exactly as the ATM prescribes.

**Behind the ladder:** the 39-offer catalogue survives as the internal delivery menu and custom-scope reference. Tier B Stripe writes remain frozen pending this gate.

## 10. Risks and open questions

1. **Agency-brand conversion at $197 is unproven market-wide.** Mitigation is built in (founder-as-instructor), but the first two workshop runs are the real test. Cheapest possible validation of the whole ladder.
2. **The $1,970 tier's white space cuts both ways** — nobody owns that shelf, possibly because the market splits buyers at $750 audits and $2,900 pilots. The one-working-automation deliverable is the hedge; watch whether buyers try to negotiate it down to the audit tier.
3. **Cannibalization of the $497 Blueprint rung.** The canonical Priestley Blueprint (~$497) and the $197 workshop both occupy the low front end. Options: retire Blueprint into the workshop's action-plan deliverable, or hold Blueprint as the async product and the workshop as the live one. Not resolved by this research; flagged for the gate.
4. **The ICP directive decision (§6)** — resourcing currently flows toward a disproven vertical bet. Independent of the ladder decision but adjacent to it.
5. **Human Construct's dual identity (conflict C1)** — must resolve before the ladder's Tier 3 copy is written, because what the build ladders into depends on which Human Construct is real.
6. **Six primary-source verifications** queued before any statistic reaches public collateral.
