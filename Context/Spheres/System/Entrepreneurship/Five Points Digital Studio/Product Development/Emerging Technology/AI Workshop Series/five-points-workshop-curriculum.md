# Five Points AI Fluency Workshop — Complete Curriculum Framework

## Pedagogical Foundation

This curriculum is built on principles drawn from the highest performing education systems in the world. The pedagogical backbone combines three traditions.

**Finland: Trust, autonomy, and depth over breadth.** Finnish education prioritizes deep understanding of fewer concepts over surface coverage of many. Every session in this curriculum is designed so that participants master one layer of competency before advancing to the next. There are no throwaway topics. If it is in the session, it earns its time.

**Singapore: Concrete to pictorial to abstract (CPA progression).** Singapore's math framework moves learners from tangible, hands on experience to visual models to abstract reasoning. This curriculum adapts that progression for AI education. Participants first experience AI doing something real in front of them (concrete), then map that experience onto their own business visually (pictorial), and only then develop the abstract frameworks to evaluate AI opportunities independently (abstract).

**Japan: Lesson study and structured problem solving.** Japanese pedagogy centers on presenting a carefully chosen problem, giving learners time to struggle with it productively, and then facilitating a structured discussion that surfaces multiple solution paths. This curriculum uses this approach in every guided exercise: present a real business scenario, let participants wrestle with it, and then debrief as a group to extract principles.

### Shared Design Principles Across All Tiers

**Mastery based progression:** Each session builds on the previous one. No session assumes knowledge that was not established in a prior session within the same tier.

**Applied learning over lecture:** The ratio of facilitator talking to participant doing should never exceed 60/40 in any session. At Silver and above, it should approach 40/60.

**Scaffolded complexity:** Bronze introduces concepts and builds awareness. Silver applies concepts to individual businesses. Gold embeds solutions into operations. Platinum transforms entire organizations.

**Platform agnostic:** The curriculum teaches thinking frameworks, decision models, and evaluation criteria. It does not teach specific tools. When demonstrations are necessary, the facilitator uses whatever tool best illustrates the concept, but the learning objective is the concept, not the tool.

**Retrieval practice:** Every session after the first opens with a brief retrieval exercise (three to five minutes) where participants recall and apply something from the previous session. This is the single most evidence backed learning technique and costs almost nothing in session time.

**One takeaway per session hour:** Participants should be able to articulate one clear, actionable takeaway for every hour of session time. If they cannot, the session covered too much or not enough was applied.

## BRONZE — AI Fluency Foundations

**Format:** Single live virtual session, two hours, up to 50 participants
**Price:** $250 per seat ($100 recording only)
**Frequency:** Monthly
**Facilitator:** Founder
**Competency level at entry:** AI aware but not AI literate. They have heard the terms. They may have tried a chatbot. They do not have a framework for evaluating AI opportunities in their business.
**Competency level at exit:** AI literate. They can identify where AI fits in their business, evaluate tools without depending on a vendor, and articulate a 90 day action plan.

### Session Arc (120 minutes total)

The two hour session follows a three act structure modeled on the CPA progression.

---

**Act 1: Concrete (40 minutes) — "See it work."**

The goal of Act 1 is to demolish the abstraction barrier. Participants need to see AI doing real work in a real business before they can imagine it in their own.

**Segment 1.1 — Opening and Context Setting (10 minutes)**

- Facilitator welcome and session overview
- Establish the ground rules: no stupid questions, this is not a sales pitch, and if you leave without three actionable ideas it is the facilitator's fault not yours
- Quick pulse check: "On a scale of one to five, how confident do you feel about AI in your business right now?" (Use a live poll tool. This creates a baseline and an engagement hook.)
- Frame the session promise: "In two hours, you will have a framework for evaluating any AI tool or opportunity, you will identify your top three AI opportunities, and you will know exactly what to do next."

**Segment 1.2 — Live Workflow Demonstration (20 minutes)**

- Facilitator walks through a real AI workflow from the Five Points operation, end to end
- This is not a hypothetical. It is the actual system that Five Points uses to operate: how AI assists with content creation, client communication, research, or automation
- The demonstration should be narrated in plain language. Every step should include: what the AI is doing, why it is doing it, what the human is still responsible for, and what would happen without AI (the before state)
- Pause twice during the demonstration to ask participants: "Where in your business does something similar happen?" This plants the seed for Act 2

**Segment 1.3 — Debrief and Framework Introduction (10 minutes)**

- Introduce the Five Points AI Fluency Framework: a simple mental model for evaluating any AI opportunity
- The framework has four lenses:
  1. **Repetition:** Is this task done repeatedly with predictable inputs and outputs?
  2. **Volume:** Is there enough volume that automating it would meaningfully save time or money?
  3. **Judgment:** Does the task require nuanced human judgment that AI cannot reliably replicate (yet)?
  4. **Risk:** What happens if the AI gets it wrong? Is the downside manageable or catastrophic?
- Walk through the demonstration workflow again, this time through each of the four lenses, showing how it scored high on Repetition and Volume, low on required Judgment, and low on Risk
- Distribute the AI Fluency Framework as a one page reference (part of the Starter Kit)

---

**Act 2: Pictorial (45 minutes) — "Map it to your business."**

The goal of Act 2 is to move from the facilitator's business to the participant's business. This is where the value becomes personal.

**Segment 2.1 — Guided Mapping Exercise (25 minutes)**

- Participants open the AI Opportunity Worksheet (provided in the Starter Kit)
- The worksheet guides them through four steps:
  1. List the five to 10 tasks you or your team spend the most time on each week
  2. Score each task on the four framework lenses (Repetition, Volume, Judgment, Risk) on a scale of one to five
  3. Calculate a simple AI Readiness Score for each task (Repetition + Volume minus Judgment minus Risk)
  4. Rank the tasks by score. The top three are your highest impact AI opportunities
- Facilitator circulates verbally (calling on participants or monitoring chat) to check progress, answer questions, and share patterns across the group
- At the 15 minute mark, facilitator pauses to share two to three anonymized examples from the group: "I am seeing a lot of you identify [common pattern]. That is one of the most impactful places to start because..."

**Segment 2.2 — Small Group Discussion (10 minutes)**

- Break participants into virtual breakout rooms of four to five people for 10 minutes
- Prompt: "Share your top AI opportunity with the group. What does it look like today? What would it look like if AI handled 80% of it?"
- This peer learning moment is critical. Participants hearing other businesses' opportunities expands their own thinking. This is the Japanese lesson study principle: multiple solution paths surface richer understanding

**Segment 2.3 — Group Debrief (10 minutes)**

- Bring everyone back. Facilitator asks two to three groups to share what they discussed
- Facilitator synthesizes the patterns: "Across this group, the three most common AI opportunities are [X, Y, Z]. Here is why those consistently show up and here is how businesses like yours are already solving them."

---

**Act 3: Abstract (30 minutes) — "Build your plan."**

The goal of Act 3 is to turn insight into action. Participants leave with a concrete plan, not just inspiration.

**Segment 3.1 — The AI Evaluation Checklist (10 minutes)**

- Introduce a simple checklist for evaluating any AI tool or vendor:
  1. What specific task does this tool automate or augment?
  2. What data does it need access to, and am I comfortable with that?
  3. What happens when it makes a mistake? Who catches it?
  4. What does it cost relative to the time or money it saves?
  5. Can I test it with low risk before committing?
- Walk through two quick examples: one tool that passes the checklist and one that fails, explaining why

**Segment 3.2 — Personal Action Plan (10 minutes)**

- Participants fill in the 90 Day AI Action Plan section of the Starter Kit:
  1. My top AI opportunity (from the mapping exercise)
  2. The first step I will take this week to explore it
  3. The tool or approach I will research first
  4. Who on my team needs to be involved
  5. My 30 day checkpoint: what does progress look like?
- This is done individually in silence. No group work. The plan is personal.

**Segment 3.3 — Q&A and Close (10 minutes)**

- Open Q&A
- Facilitator resurfaces the opening poll: "Now that we have spent two hours together, where are you on that confidence scale?" (Run the poll again. The before and after comparison is a powerful emotional close and provides testimonial data.)
- Close with the upsell path (warm, not pushy): "If you looked at your AI Opportunity Map and thought 'I know what needs to be built but I do not want to build it myself,' that is exactly what the Silver and Gold tiers are for. We build it for you. The link is in the chat if you want to learn more."
- Reminder about the Starter Kit, recording access (30 days), and the async video audit bonus for the first 20 registrants

### Deliverables

- AI Fluency Starter Kit (PDF): the four lens framework, AI Opportunity Worksheet, AI Evaluation Checklist, 90 Day Action Plan template, curated tool recommendations (platform agnostic), and a self assessment scorecard
- Session recording (Google Drive link, 30 day access)
- Bonus (first 20 registrants): 15 minute async Loom video audit of one workflow

## SILVER — AI Implementation Sprint

**Format:** Four virtual sessions over two weeks, 90 minutes each, max eight businesses
**Price:** $1,500 (individual) / $2,500 (teams of up to five)
**Facilitator:** Founder
**Competency level at entry:** AI literate (Bronze graduate or equivalent). They know where AI could help. They need someone to build it and show them how to maintain it.
**Competency level at exit:** AI operational. They have functional automations running in their business, understand how those automations work (without needing to build them), and have a prioritized roadmap for the next 90 days.

### The Sprint Philosophy

Silver is not a teaching engagement. It is a strategy and delivery engagement with education embedded. Five Points builds the automations. The participant's job is to provide the business context and learn how to monitor and maintain what was built. Think of it like buying a Ferrari: the participant does not need to understand the engine to drive it, but they do need to understand the dashboard.

### Session 1: Orientation and Workflow Mapping (90 minutes)

**Week 1, Session A**

**Opening Retrieval (5 minutes):**
- If participants attended the Bronze workshop: "What was your top AI opportunity from the workshop? Has anything changed since then?"
- If new participants: brief overview of the AI Fluency Framework (four lenses) using a condensed version of the Bronze Act 1

**Segment 1: Deep Dive into Each Business (50 minutes)**
- Each participant (or business) gets six to seven minutes to present their business operations
- Facilitator uses a structured interview format:
  1. "Walk me through a typical week in your business. What are the biggest time sinks?"
  2. "Where do things break down or fall through the cracks?"
  3. "What does your tech stack look like? What tools do you use daily?"
  4. "If you could wave a magic wand and automate one thing tomorrow, what would it be?"
- Facilitator maps each response against the four lenses in real time (shared screen) so participants see the framework being applied to their actual business
- Other participants are encouraged to listen and note similarities to their own operations

**Segment 2: Collaborative Prioritization (25 minutes)**
- Facilitator synthesizes patterns across the group
- For each business, facilitator identifies the top two to three automation opportunities and explains why they rank highest
- Participants validate: "Does this feel right? Is there anything I missed about how this process works in your business?"

**Segment 3: Sprint Plan and Expectations (10 minutes)**
- Facilitator outlines what happens between sessions: Five Points will build the top priority automation for each business and deliver it within three business days
- Explain the delivery model: "You do not build this. We do. Your job between now and Session 3 is to give us access to the tools we need and answer any clarifying questions via the group chat."
- Set up the private group chat (Slack or similar)

**Homework (between sessions):**
- Provide Five Points with login credentials or API access to the tools needed for the automation build
- Answer any clarifying questions that come through the group chat

---

### Session 2: Strategy Deep Dive and Second Wave Identification (90 minutes)

**Week 1, Session B**

**Opening Retrieval (5 minutes):**
- "What is the one automation we are building for your business right now? Can you describe what it does in one sentence?"

**Segment 1: AI Opportunity Map Construction (45 minutes)**
- Facilitator works with each business to build their full AI Opportunity Map
- This is a visual document showing the top 10 workflows ranked by impact, feasibility, and AI readiness
- The four lens framework is applied systematically to each workflow
- Facilitator introduces a fifth evaluation criterion for this tier: **Integration Complexity.** How many systems need to talk to each other for this automation to work? This helps participants understand why some high impact opportunities are better tackled in sequence rather than all at once.

**Segment 2: Peer Learning Round (20 minutes)**
- Each business shares their top three opportunities with the group
- Facilitator facilitates cross pollination: "Business A, your client follow up challenge is very similar to what Business C described. Here is how the same automation pattern could work for both of you."
- This is the Japanese lesson study moment: multiple businesses seeing how the same principle manifests differently in different contexts

**Segment 3: Second Wave Prioritization (20 minutes)**
- For each business, facilitator identifies the second automation to be built (to be delivered before Session 3)
- Facilitator explains the dependency logic: "We are building this one second because it feeds into the first one. Once both are running, the compound effect is [X]."

**Between sessions (Five Points work):**
- Five Points builds and tests automations for each business
- Delivery within three business days of Session 2
- Documentation drafted for each automation (how it works, what to monitor, what triggers it)

---

### Session 3: Automation Review and Education (90 minutes)

**Week 2, Session A**

**Opening Retrieval (5 minutes):**
- "Before we look at what was built, can you describe what you expected the automation to do? Let us see how the reality compares."

**Segment 1: Live Demonstration of Completed Automations (50 minutes)**
- Facilitator walks through each business's completed automation(s) on screen
- For each automation, the demonstration covers:
  1. **What it does:** Plain language explanation of the workflow from trigger to output
  2. **How it works:** Visual walkthrough of the automation logic (without requiring participants to understand the technical layer)
  3. **What to watch for:** The three to five key indicators that the automation is running correctly (the dashboard analogy)
  4. **What to do if something breaks:** Simple troubleshooting decision tree. "If you see [X], do [Y]. If that does not fix it, message us."
- Each business gets eight to 10 minutes
- Participants are encouraged to ask questions specific to their automation

**Segment 2: Monitoring and Maintenance Education (20 minutes)**
- Facilitator teaches the universal monitoring framework:
  1. Set a weekly check in (five minutes) to review automation outputs
  2. Know the three failure signals for your automation type
  3. Keep a simple log of any anomalies (even if they resolve themselves)
  4. Know when to escalate (to Five Points or internal IT)
- This education is provided AFTER the automation is built and demonstrated. Participants understand the system concretely before learning to maintain it abstractly.

**Segment 3: Q&A and Refinement Requests (15 minutes)**
- Open floor for questions, refinement requests, and "what about [edge case]?" discussions
- Five Points takes notes on any adjustments needed (to be implemented before Session 4)

---

### Session 4: Optimization, Roadmap, and Graduation (90 minutes)

**Week 2, Session B**

**Opening Retrieval (5 minutes):**
- "Has your automation run since our last session? What happened? Anything surprising?"

**Segment 1: Refinement Review (20 minutes)**
- Facilitator reviews any adjustments made since Session 3
- Quick demonstration of changes for affected businesses

**Segment 2: 90 Day Roadmap Construction (35 minutes)**
- Facilitator works with each business to build a prioritized 90 day roadmap
- The roadmap uses the AI Opportunity Map as the source material and sequences the next three to five automations by impact and dependency
- For each roadmap item, facilitator notes: estimated impact, estimated complexity, and whether it can be self served or requires Five Points support
- This is the bridge to the upsell: the roadmap naturally reveals the scope of remaining work, and participants can decide whether to self serve or engage Gold tier for the build

**Segment 3: Peer Accountability and Commitments (15 minutes)**
- Each business shares their number one 30 day commitment with the group
- Facilitator facilitates peer accountability pairings: "You two have similar roadmaps. Exchange contact info and check in with each other in 30 days."

**Segment 4: Close and Next Steps (15 minutes)**
- Recap of what was built, what was learned, and what comes next
- Deliverable handoff: AI Implementation Playbook (digital workbook), automation documentation, AI Opportunity Map (polished version delivered within five business days)
- Warm upsell: "Your roadmap has [X] more automations identified. If you want Five Points to build them, the Gold tier is designed exactly for that. Here is the link."
- Feedback collection: "What was the most valuable part of this sprint? What would you change?"

### Deliverables

- Functional automation(s) built and deployed by Five Points
- AI Opportunity Map (polished visual document, top 10 workflows ranked)
- AI Implementation Playbook (digital workbook with decision frameworks and documentation)
- 90 day prioritized roadmap
- Automation documentation (how each automation works, monitoring guide, troubleshooting)
- Session recordings (Google Drive, 90 day access)

## GOLD — AI Transformation Day

**Format:** Full day virtual intensive (six hours), one business, up to eight participants from the leadership team
**Price:** $5,000
**Facilitator:** Founder
**Competency level at entry:** AI literate to AI operational. The leadership team understands AI conceptually and is ready to commit to organizational implementation.
**Competency level at exit:** AI strategic. The leadership team has a shared understanding of AI's role in their organization, has seen functional automations built for their business, and has a six month implementation roadmap.

### Pre Session (Completed Before the Intensive Day)

**Discovery Call (60 minutes, scheduled one to two weeks prior):**
- Facilitator conducts a deep discovery call with the primary stakeholder
- Covers: business model, team structure, tech stack, operational pain points, strategic goals, previous AI or automation attempts
- Five Points completes the Workflow and Operations Intake assessment
- Five Points identifies the top five to seven automation opportunities and pre selects the two to three that will be built during the post session delivery window

**Pre Session Deliverable to Participant:**
- Brief summary of what Five Points learned and the preliminary automation candidates
- Request for any remaining system access needed for the build

### The Intensive Day (Six Hours)

The day follows a morning education and afternoon strategy arc. The education is front loaded so the team has shared language before the strategic decisions are made.

---

**Hour 1: Foundation and Alignment (Education)**

**Segment 1.1 — Opening and Context Setting (15 minutes)**
- Welcome the team, set the tone: "Today is about your business. Everything we cover will be specific to how your organization operates and where AI can create the most impact."
- Quick round table: each team member shares their role and their biggest operational frustration in 60 seconds
- This round table serves a dual purpose: it gives the facilitator insight into each team member's perspective, and it surfaces pain points that may not have come up in the discovery call

**Segment 1.2 — AI Fluency Framework for the Team (30 minutes)**
- Condensed version of the Bronze Act 1, tailored to this specific business
- The live demonstration uses examples from the client's own industry (or analogous businesses) rather than the Five Points workflow
- Introduce the four lenses (Repetition, Volume, Judgment, Risk)
- Apply the framework to one of the pain points raised in the round table: "You mentioned [X]. Let us score it right now." This makes the framework immediately real.

**Segment 1.3 — Team Pulse Check (15 minutes)**
- Facilitate a brief discussion: "Where do you see the biggest AI opportunity in your department?" Each team member answers.
- Facilitator captures these on a shared board (Miro, FigJam, or similar) organized by department
- This becomes the raw material for Hour 2

---

**Hour 2: Organizational Workflow Mapping (Strategy)**

**Segment 2.1 — Cross Departmental Workflow Map (45 minutes)**
- Facilitator guides the team through mapping their core business processes across departments
- The map follows the value chain: how does a lead become a client, how does a client get served, how does the service get delivered, and how does the relationship get maintained?
- For each stage, the team identifies: the tools used, the people involved, the handoff points, and the friction or failure points
- The facilitator scores each stage on the four lenses in real time

**Segment 2.2 — Priority Stack (15 minutes)**
- Facilitator synthesizes the map into a ranked list of automation opportunities
- The team votes on the top priorities using a simple impact versus effort matrix
- Facilitator reveals the two to three automations that Five Points has pre selected based on the discovery call: "These are the ones we plan to build for you. Here is why they rose to the top."
- Team validates or adjusts. This is collaborative, not prescriptive.

---

**Hour 3: Deep Dive into Priority Automations (Education + Strategy)**

**Segment 3.1 — Automation Architecture Walkthrough (30 minutes)**
- For each of the two to three priority automations, facilitator maps the workflow visually:
  - Trigger: what kicks it off
  - Logic: what decisions need to be made
  - Action: what the automation does
  - Output: what the team sees
  - Monitoring: what to watch for
- The team sees exactly what will be built, in plain language, before any building happens
- Questions and refinements are encouraged here: "Is this how it actually works, or is there a nuance I am missing?"

**Segment 3.2 — Build Scope Confirmation (15 minutes)**
- Facilitator confirms the final scope of the two to three automations
- Establishes delivery timeline: functional automations delivered within three business days of the session
- Identifies the team member who will be the primary point of contact for testing and feedback

**Break (15 minutes)**

---

**Hour 4: AI Strategy for the Organization (Strategy)**

**Segment 4.1 — The AI Maturity Model (20 minutes)**
- Introduce a simple three stage AI maturity model:
  1. **Assisted:** AI handles individual tasks (email drafting, data entry, scheduling). Most businesses start here.
  2. **Augmented:** AI is embedded in workflows and makes decisions within defined parameters (lead scoring, content personalization, automated reporting). This is where the Gold automation build takes them.
  3. **Autonomous:** AI manages entire processes end to end with human oversight at checkpoints only (full marketing automation, self optimizing operations). This is the Platinum vision.
- Plot the client's current state on the model
- Plot where the two to three automations being built will move them

**Segment 4.2 — Six Month Roadmap Construction (40 minutes)**
- Facilitator works with the team to build a prioritized six month implementation roadmap
- The roadmap sequences automations by dependency, impact, and the maturity progression
- For each roadmap item: estimated impact (hours saved or revenue generated), estimated timeline, and whether it requires Five Points support or can be maintained internally
- The roadmap is designed as a living document that will be delivered as the AI Integration Roadmap

---

**Hour 5: Team Alignment and Change Management (Education)**

**Segment 5.1 — How AI Changes Roles, Not Replaces Them (20 minutes)**
- Facilitate a discussion about what changes for each team member when the automations are live
- This is the change management layer that most AI training skips
- For each automation: "This used to be [person's] responsibility. Now AI handles the [repetitive part]. What [person] gets back is time to focus on [higher value work]. Let us talk about what that looks like."

**Segment 5.2 — Monitoring and Governance Framework (20 minutes)**
- Establish who owns each automation (the human accountable for its output)
- Establish the check in cadence: weekly five minute review per automation
- Establish the escalation path: what to do if an automation produces unexpected results
- Establish the feedback loop: how the team communicates refinement needs to Five Points during the 30 day support window

**Segment 5.3 — Team Q&A (20 minutes)**
- Open floor for questions, concerns, and "what about..." scenarios
- This is deliberately placed near the end so the team has maximum context before asking questions

---

**Hour 6: Action Planning and Close (Strategy)**

**Segment 6.1 — Individual Commitments (15 minutes)**
- Each team member writes down: "The one thing I will do differently starting Monday because of today."
- Share around the table. This creates individual accountability and collective buy in.

**Segment 6.2 — Post Session Expectations (15 minutes)**
- Recap the delivery timeline: automations delivered within three business days, post session walkthrough scheduled for the following week
- Outline the 30 day support window: how to reach Five Points, expected response time, what qualifies as a support request versus a new build
- Preview the bonus automation: one additional build delivered within 14 days

**Segment 6.3 — Feedback and Close (15 minutes)**
- Feedback collection (structured): most valuable moment, least valuable moment, what would you change
- Closing message: "Today was about giving your team a shared language, a shared vision, and real tools that are already being built. The hardest part of AI adoption is not the technology. It is getting your team aligned. You just did that."

**Break / Buffer (15 minutes)**

### Post Session Deliverables (Delivered by Five Points)

- Two to three functional automations (delivered within three business days)
- Post session walkthrough (60 minutes virtual, scheduled within seven days of the intensive)
- AI Integration Roadmap (six month prioritized implementation plan, delivered as branded PDF within five business days)
- Session recordings (Google Drive link, 90 day access)
- Bonus: one additional automation build (delivered within 14 days)
- 30 day async support window

## PLATINUM — The AI Ecosystem Retreat

**Format:** Two day on location intensive at the client's office or curated venue, up to 12 participants from one organization
**Price:** $15,000
**Facilitator:** Founder plus support team (contractor or specialist as needed)
**Competency level at entry:** Varied across the team. Leadership may be AI literate. Department leads and team members may range from AI unaware to AI curious.
**Competency level at exit:** Organization wide AI fluency. The leadership team has a 12 month AI roadmap. Five to seven priority automations are built and deployed. The team can maintain and extend the ecosystem with ongoing support.

### Pre Retreat Phase (Two Weeks Before)

**Week 1: Discovery and Assessment**
- Comprehensive digital audit of the organization
- Stakeholder interviews (30 minutes each) with department leads
- Workflow and Operations Intake assessment for each department
- Tech stack audit and integration feasibility analysis

**Week 2: Preparation and Pre Read**
- Five Points completes the assessment and identifies the top 10 to 15 automation opportunities across all departments
- Pre selects the five to seven automations to be built on Day 2
- Delivers a pre retreat brief to the client: summary of findings, preliminary priorities, and the retreat agenda
- Participants receive a short pre read document (two to three pages) introducing the AI Fluency Framework and the day's objectives. This ensures everyone arrives with baseline context regardless of their starting point.

### Day 1: AI Fluency Immersion (Eight Hours Including Breaks)

Day 1 is education and strategy. No building happens on Day 1. The entire day is designed to bring every participant, regardless of starting point, to a shared level of AI literacy and organizational alignment.

---

**Morning Block (Four Hours)**

**Hour 1: Opening and Organizational Alignment**
- Welcome, introductions, and ground rules
- Facilitator presents the assessment findings: "Here is what we learned about your organization over the past two weeks. Here is where we see the biggest opportunities and the biggest gaps."
- Team discussion: "Does this match your experience? What did we miss?"
- This opening is critical because it demonstrates that Five Points already understands the business deeply. It builds trust immediately and eliminates the "let me tell you about us" phase.

**Hour 2: AI Fluency Framework for the Full Team**
- Full version of the Bronze Act 1 and Act 2, tailored entirely to the client's industry and operations
- The live demonstration uses scenarios drawn directly from the assessment findings
- Every example is relevant to someone in the room. This is not a generic presentation.
- The four lens framework is introduced and applied to three to four real workflows from the organization

**Hour 3: Departmental Workflow Mapping**
- Break participants into departmental or functional groups
- Each group maps their core workflows using the four lenses
- Facilitator rotates between groups, guiding the mapping process and asking probing questions
- This is the Japanese lesson study model at organizational scale: each department wrestles with their own workflows while the facilitator ensures the framework is applied consistently

**Hour 4: Cross Departmental Synthesis**
- Groups present their findings to the full team
- Facilitator maps the cross departmental connections: "Marketing's lead handoff connects to Sales's follow up process, which connects to Operations's onboarding workflow. These are not three separate automations. They are one ecosystem."
- Priority ranking exercise: the full team votes on the top automation opportunities using an impact versus effort matrix
- Facilitator reveals the pre selected five to seven automations and aligns them with the team's priorities

**Lunch Break (60 minutes)**

---

**Afternoon Block (Four Hours)**

**Hour 5: AI Strategy and Maturity Planning**
- Introduce the AI Maturity Model (Assisted, Augmented, Autonomous) at organizational level
- Plot the organization's current state by department
- Plot where the Day 2 builds will move each department
- Facilitate a discussion: "Where do you want to be in 12 months? What needs to be true for that to happen?"

**Hour 6: Change Management and Role Evolution**
- Department by department discussion: how AI changes workflows and what each team member's role looks like post implementation
- Address fears and concerns directly: "AI is not replacing anyone in this room. It is giving you back the time that is currently being consumed by [specific repetitive tasks]."
- Establish ownership: for each automation to be built on Day 2, identify the human owner and the monitoring cadence

**Hour 7: 12 Month Roadmap Construction**
- Collaborative exercise: the team builds the 12 month AI roadmap together
- The roadmap is organized in three phases:
  1. **Foundation (Months 1 to 3):** The five to seven automations built during the retreat plus immediate quick wins
  2. **Expansion (Months 4 to 8):** Next wave of automations identified in the assessment, sequenced by dependency and impact
  3. **Optimization (Months 9 to 12):** Refinement, integration of advanced AI features, and measurement of cumulative ROI
- For each phase: specific automations, responsible team members, estimated impact, and success metrics

**Hour 8: Day 1 Close and Day 2 Preview**
- Recap of the day: what was learned, what was decided, what comes tomorrow
- Day 2 preview: "Tomorrow, we build. By the end of the day, five to seven automations will be live in your business."
- Assign any overnight preparation tasks (system access, data exports, etc.)

---

### Day 2: Implementation Day (Eight Hours Including Breaks)

Day 2 is all execution. Five Points builds. The team learns by watching, testing, and providing feedback in real time.

---

**Morning Block (Four Hours)**

**Hour 1: Build Sprint 1 (Automations 1 and 2)**
- Five Points team builds the first two priority automations live
- The team observes and provides real time input: "Is this the right trigger? Is this the correct output format?"
- Facilitator narrates the build in plain language: what is happening, why, and what the team will see when it is done

**Hour 2: Build Sprint 1 Continued and Testing**
- Complete and test automations 1 and 2
- The designated human owner for each automation runs through the workflow end to end
- Facilitator walks the owner through the monitoring dashboard and the troubleshooting decision tree

**Hour 3: Build Sprint 2 (Automations 3 and 4)**
- Same process for the next two automations
- By this point, the team has seen the pattern and can anticipate the workflow architecture before the facilitator explains it

**Hour 4: Build Sprint 2 Continued and Testing**
