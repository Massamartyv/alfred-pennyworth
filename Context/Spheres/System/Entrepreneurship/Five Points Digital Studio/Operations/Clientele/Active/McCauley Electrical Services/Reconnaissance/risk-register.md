# McCauley Electrical Services – Risk Register

Honest, not performative. Each risk carries its mitigation.

## R1 – Burned-buyer scepticism

He spent roughly $2,000 on an agency or website and got nothing. Any proposal that
smells like the last one dies on arrival – and the failure is undiagnosed, so we
do not yet know which mechanism he distrusts.
**Mitigation:** The audit is structurally the opposite of what burned him – small,
bounded, guaranteed, refundable, and it ends with him owning a report either way.
Ask what the $2,000 bought during the intake, before any build is scoped.

## R2 – Size mismatch against the studio model

He sits under the $1M-$10M, 10-50-team ICP band. A proposal scoped like a
mid-market retainer reads as generic and confirms his wariness.
**Mitigation:** Scoped project staircase – $497 audit, decision, then a bounded
build. No retainer language anywhere in the first commission. The mismatch is
named honestly in the dossier rather than stretched.

## R3 – The competing quote closes first

A $2,500 monthly quote is live on his table. Every day the proposal is not in his
inbox, someone else's is.
**Mitigation:** Proposal staged within 24 hours of gate approval. The comparison
is drawn inside the proposal on ownership terms – their subscription rents
activity, this commission builds an asset he keeps, at a fraction of one month of
their fee.

## R4 – The capacity paradox

Any work that adds demand – ads, SEO push, content – makes his stated problem
worse. A conventional growth pitch would be malpractice here.
**Mitigation:** Scope discipline written into the audit: intake, triage,
scheduling and review capture. Lead generation is explicitly excluded in the
engagement terms and the proposal says so in plain language.

## R5 – Mishandling the identity

The rainbow-stripe, LGBTQ-proud identity is personally held and publicly absent.
Treated as a marketing angle, it patronises him and poisons the relationship.
**Mitigation:** It stays out of the first commission entirely. It is noted as a
future brand chapter that he initiates or approves explicitly. If raised at all
in the proposal, it is one line acknowledging what he said, nothing more.

## R6 – Solo-client bandwidth stalls the engagement

A slammed solo operator has no hours for forms or homework. Friction kills cold
engagements quietly.
**Mitigation:** The working call doubles as scoping; his total time investment
is one conversation plus one handoff demonstration. Nothing in any door
requires homework from him.

## R7 – Unknown platform blocks the booking embed

Host, WordPress plan tier and page builder are unknown. Some tiers and builders
restrict third-party scripts, which would block an on-page booking widget – the
centrepiece of every door. He has also expressly ruled out a rebuild, so the
work must land on whatever the platform turns out to be.
**Mitigation:** Platform identified at the working call before commissioning.
Fallback path priced into the scope: a platform-hosted booking page linked
prominently from the site and the Google profile satisfies the booking-live
guarantee on any tier. The make-good guarantee covers either route.
