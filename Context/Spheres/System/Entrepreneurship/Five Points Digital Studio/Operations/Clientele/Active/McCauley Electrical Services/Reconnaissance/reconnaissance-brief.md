# McCauley Electrical Services – Reconnaissance Brief

Fused from the operator's discovery-call notes of 2026-07-09, post-call
clarifications and the company reconnaissance sweep. Full evidence with citations
in `reference-material.md` and the two research briefs it points to.

## Prospect Profile

- **Company:** McCauley Electrical Service, Inc. – mccelec.com
- **Stage:** Established 27 years. Founded 1999, incorporated 2002, BBB A+ file
  open since 2001.
- **Revenue estimate:** Unconfirmed; solo-operator scale, inferred under $1M.
  Service pricing per Angi: $50 trip charge plus $100 per hour.
- **Team:** Solo or near solo. Had his first hire before the 2008 crash; the crash
  shrank the business back and it stayed that size by a mixture of preference and
  circumstance.
- **Decision-maker:** Patrick McCauley, President. Sole and final.
- **Lead source:** Cold call by the operator, 2026-07-09. No inbound trail.

## Situation Summary

In his words, from the call: he went out on his own in 2000 and ran to 2008 with
no website at all, on word of mouth. The phone now rings so much that he cannot
tell clients he cannot get someone. He stays scheduled out. He navigates on
quality. He likes smaller business and scale tends to be a problem – yet he wants
his calendar booked out solidly enough to justify hiring help again, like he had
before the crash. He has paid $800-1,000 monthly for marketing and called it
pennies; he has paid up to $2,000-2,500 at peak; roughly $2,000 of it produced
nothing. The all-in-one companies are becoming conglomerates. Growing reviews
feels impossible. And unprompted: the brand is rainbow stripes – LGBTQ proud.

The paradox that defines the engagement: he is oversubscribed and underleveraged
at the same time. More demand would hurt him. Control over the demand he already
has would change his business.

## Signal Strength

Moderate-strong. He took a cold call and gave it real time, volunteered his
numbers, his history and his identity, and asked for a proposal – with a
competing quote at $2,500 monthly already on his table. The competing quote cuts
both ways: he is actively shopping, and he is actively comparing. What won the
call was the operator's investigative register – he said so. The proposal must
be the same conversation continued on paper.

## Competitive Landscape

Two layers, both real:

1. **The conglomerates he named.** Wrench Group – Atlanta-headquartered, Leonard
   Green backed, ~7,300 employees – runs BriteBox Electrical in his exact category
   and metro: 2,239 Google reviews at 4.8, live online booking. Apex Service
   Partners entered Atlanta late 2024. The squeeze is accelerating.
2. **The well-reviewed independents.** Pat Murphy – 680 reviews, Bray – 601,
   McCall – 509 and others own the round-ups for "electrician Atlanta", Decatur
   and Midtown. McCauley appears in none of the three searches run – including
   Midtown, where he holds a dedicated landing page.

Against both layers his review sentiment is flawless and his volume is an order
of magnitude short. Every competitor fetched runs online booking; he runs none.
