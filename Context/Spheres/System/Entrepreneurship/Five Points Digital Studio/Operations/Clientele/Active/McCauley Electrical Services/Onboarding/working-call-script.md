# Working Call Script

The present-live companion to the Commissioning Proposal. The Discovery Kit's
call-spine wins the first call by investigating; this wins the second by
presenting. Same principle, inverted: the discovery call sells nothing, the
working call sells everything - and it sells by walking the client through the
build before it ever shows a price.

The arc is fixed; the wording is yours. Fill the variables key, keep the
proposal on screen, and run the beats in order. Talk less than half the call.

## Why present, never email

A proposal emailed is a proposal opened to the price page. The client decides on
one number, in isolation, with no idea what earns it. A proposal presented is a
sequence: they hear their own goals repeated back, they watch you show your
homework, they follow the build as a process - and only then, with the value
already sitting in the room, do they see the number. The decision gets made on
trust, and price falls to second. This is the whole game. If the client says
"just email it," you present first and email after. Every time.

## The Arc

**0. Before you dial.** Proposal open, screen-share tested. Discovery notes reread,
the three objectives in front of you in their words. Value math within reach for
the price beat. The goal of the call is not to close today - it is to be the best
conversation about their business they have had with anyone in your category.

**1. Frame - buy permission to delay the price.** [~2 min]
> "Good to see you, {FIRST_NAME}. Here is how I would like to use the time: I will
> walk you through what I heard from you, what I found, and exactly what I would
> build and why. I will show you the numbers at the end, once they will actually
> mean something. Stop me anywhere. That work for you?"

*Why: you set the order out loud and earn the right to hold the price back. Now
they will not jump ahead, because you told them the number is coming.*

**2. Mirror what they want.** [~5 min - proposal page 2]
> "Before anything, I want to make sure I actually heard you. Three things. One,
> {OBJECTIVE_1}. Two, {OBJECTIVE_2}. Three, {OBJECTIVE_3}. Have I got that right,
> or is any of it off?"

[Then stop. Let them talk. Correct on the spot.]

*Why: this is the trust hinge. They must feel understood before they will believe
the build. If they correct you, you have just improved the proposal live and
earned more trust doing it.*

**3. Show your homework.** [~7 min - proposal page 3]
> "So I spent a day looking at {COMPANY} the way a new customer would. Two things
> stood out, and both are fixable. First, {FINDING_1}. Second, {FINDING_2}. The
> demand you want is out there right now. It is just being caught by someone else."

*Why: proof of competence, and it makes the build feel necessary rather than
optional. You are not selling yet - you are diagnosing, and the diagnosis sells
for you.*

**4. Walk the build as a process.** [~12 min - proposal pages 4 to 6]
[Share each offer page. Describe the process and the outcome. Do not say the
price yet.]
> "Here is how I would build it. Everyone starts at {ENTRY_OFFER} - {ENTRY_COMPONENTS}.
> We look first, then build the first working piece together."
> "From there you own the pieces of the engine, added as you grow - {BUILD_MENU}.
> Each one is a fixed project you own outright, not a subscription."
> "And when you want the whole thing built as one system and handed to you as an
> asset, that is {PREMIUM_OFFER} - the machine that finally lets you {OBJECTIVE_1}."

*Why: walking the process is what makes them think "this is far more than the last
person, who just emailed a price." You are selling the how. The how is what earns
the what.*

**5. Value first, then the price, then the reversal.** [~8 min]
[Only now. This is the beat the whole call has been building to.]
> "Let me show you what this is worth before I tell you what it costs. Right now
> there is {VALUE_GAP} - work one man could bill and is not. On these prices,
> {VALUE_RULE}, conservatively. One extra job a week covers most of this in a month."

[Then the cost of no:]
> "And doing nothing is not free. {COMPETITOR} rents you a thinner version forever -
> the day you stop paying, it is gone. And the brand that out-reviewed you is
> taking that demand while we talk."

[Then the number, immediately followed by the reversal:]
> "So here is the number. {ENTRY_OFFER}. And the terms are built so you are never
> out on a limb: {PAYMENT}. Your deposit buys my obligation to deliver, not a
> promise - you hold the balance until you have watched the machine work. If it
> does not work, I keep building at no charge until it does."

*Why: value first shrinks the price before it lands. The risk reversal answers the
burned-buyer fear before they can raise it. Say the number, then stop talking.*

**6. The ask, and the silence.** [close]
> "I would start you at {ENTRY_OFFER}. If that feels right, we {NEXT_STEP}. Want to
> do it?"

[Then say nothing. Let the silence sit until they fill it.]

*Why: after the ask, the first to speak concedes. Sit in it. The pause is not
awkward to them - it is them deciding.*

## Objection Bank

- **"Just email it to me."** → "Happy to, and I will right after this. I wanted you
  to have the walk-through first, because the last person just emailed a price, and
  that is exactly the trap. Now you have seen the whole thing - I will send it as
  your reference."
- **"It is a lot."** → "Against what it puts on your calendar, it is a tenth of the
  value - and you do not pay the balance until you have watched it work. Is it the
  number that feels like a lot, or the risk? Let us talk about whichever one it is."
- **"I got a quote for $2,500 a month."** → "I saw that. The difference is rent
  versus ownership. That is rent - the day you stop paying, it is gone. This you
  own. Three months of their bill and you would own ours outright."
- **"Let me think about it."** → "Of course. What is the one thing you would want to
  be sure of before you would say yes? Let us put it on the table now, while I am
  here to answer it."
- **"Can you guarantee results?"** → "I guarantee the machine works before you pay
  the balance - booking taking appointments, calls answered, rankings moving - or I
  keep building at no charge until it does. That part is on me, not you."

## After the Call

- Send the recap the same day: one short email - their three objectives in their
  own words, the offer they leaned toward, the next step and its date. Attach the
  proposal now. They have seen it; it is a reference, not a price sheet.
- If they committed: send the 50% commission invoice and the build kickoff time.
- If not yet: send the proposal and the single next-step date you agreed out loud.
  A working call without an agreed next step is a leak.

## The Register

Value before price, always. Process before number. The client decides on trust,
and trust is built by being the one who understood them, showed the work, and
carried the risk. Present it live or do not present it at all.
