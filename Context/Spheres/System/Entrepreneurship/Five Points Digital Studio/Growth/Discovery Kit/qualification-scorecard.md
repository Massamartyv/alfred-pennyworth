# Qualification Scorecard

Run after capture, before the dossier. Four axes, scored honestly. The scorecard
informs the ICP section of the dossier; the Direction gate decides.

## The Four Axes

**1. Firmographic fit** – against the stated ICP: $1M-$10M revenue, 10-50 team,
professional services and healthcare.
- Strong: inside the band
- Moderate: adjacent – wrong vertical, right scale, or the reverse
- Weak: solo or micro operator
- Note it plainly. A weak firmographic score shapes the engagement, it does not
  necessarily kill it.

**2. Problem fit** – does a named pillar solve their named friction?
- Strong: the fork answer plus wand answer map cleanly to one offer family
- Moderate: the friction is real but diffuse
- Weak: their problem is not one the studio solves – say so and walk

**3. Budget fit** – their history against the entry rung, not the sweet spot.
- What have they actually paid monthly, and at peak?
- Rule from McCauley: a burned buyer with sub-$2,500 history is an Audit-first
  prospect. Never open with a retainer on a burned buyer.

**4. Register fit** – did the investigative approach land? Did they open up?
- A prospect who wants a vendor, not an investigation, is a poor fit for how the
  studio works regardless of the other three axes.

## Routing

| Pattern | Route |
|---|---|
| Oversubscribed, control-side fork answer | Operational Intelligence – Audit first |
| Demand-starved, growth-side fork answer | Market Presence family – sized to budget axis |
| Identity-shaped gap, brand absent or wrong | Creative family – usually second chapter, not first |
| Site is the named friction | Development family – scoped project |
| Burned buyer, any pattern | Entry rung with a guarantee, credit forward. Never a retainer first. |

## The Line That Holds

The staircase exists so nobody has to be sold twice. Each step earns the next or
the engagement ends cleanly. Scoring a prospect into a bigger first ask than
their pattern supports is how the studio becomes the agency that burned them.
