# Cold Call Script — The Five Verticals

**CTA:** The Operational Intelligence Diagnostic (see `operational-intelligence-diagnostic.md`)
**Spine:** The six-beat arc (see `vertical-messaging-umbrella.md`)
**Drawn from:** the Five Points Sales Conversation Framework, Sam McKenna's Show Me You Know Me discovery script, the Hormozi value equation, with Chris Do and Myron Golden underneath.
**Send rule:** No live calling against a real list without operator approval per Touchpoint G.

---

## The bearing — read before you dial

We do not sell. We serve. Every contact is a diagnostic, and the only goal of a cold call is to earn one honest conversation — never to convince. Four moves carry it, and none of them push.

- **Show them you know them.** Open with something specific and true about their world, not a word about what Five Points does. Then hand them the floor. Be the most informed and least pushy person who called them this month.
- **Ask, do not assert.** Never push an opinion about their business. Ask the question that lets them feel the gap, then go quiet. Seventy percent them, thirty percent you. "Tell me more about that" is the strongest sentence you own.
- **Let the cost of standing still do the persuading.** People move to avoid loss far more than to chase gain. You never tell them what the gap is costing — you ask, and let them say it. Their number, in their words, moves them. Yours would not.
- **Offer the transformation, not the mechanism.** When you name what is on the table, name the after-state — the business that runs without living in their head — not the systems that get them there. Raise the dream outcome, make it cost almost nothing to find out: twenty minutes, free, nothing to prepare, the findings kept either way.

The posture that keeps all of it from reading as desperate is **reluctance.** You almost did not call. You will say so plainly if it is not a fit. You build for three at a time. After every question, and after the single ask, you stop talking and let the silence carry the weight.

**Delivery note.** These are beats to internalise, not a monologue to read. Most of the call is them talking. Let contractions fall naturally when spoken — the no-contraction rule governs written copy, not your voice. Composed and unhurried. The calm is the credibility.

## The script

### 1. Opener — reluctant, honest, permission

> "Hi [First Name], Martavious Spicer with Five Points Digital Studio. I will be straight with you — this is a cold call, and I almost did not make it, because you are clearly busy. I am not going to pitch you anything. I had one genuine question, and if it is not a useful one, I will let you go. Fair?"

### 2. Show them you know them — then hand over the floor

> "I have been paying attention to [signal]. [The SMYKM observation — see your vertical below.] I would rather hear it from you, though — when something like that happens, what actually has to get rebuilt underneath, before the part out front catches up?"

Then stop. Let them talk. This is the call. Seventy-thirty.

### 3. The cost — drawn out, never named by you

> "And while that is being worked out — what does it quietly cost you? The odd deal that slips, the hours, something that never shows up on a report?"

Silence. Let them put a number or a name on it. Whatever they say is the thing that sells the diagnostic — not you.

### 4. The transformation — offered as observation, not pitch

> "What I notice is that the ones who get the layer underneath right stop living inside the machine. The standard holds whether or not they are in the room. That is the only thing I would put twenty minutes of your time against."

### 5. The invitation — de-risked, reluctant, one ask

> "So I am not going to sell you on a call. If it is useful, I will spend twenty minutes showing you three places that gap is costing you right now — your real numbers, not a demo — and you keep those three either way, whether or not we ever speak again. If I do not think we are a fit, I will tell you that first. Worth twenty minutes, or shall I let you get back to it?"

### 6. Close — light, two doors, then quiet

> "Good. I will send two times — are you better early or late in the week?"

Book it. Confirm the email for the hold and the five intake questions. End warm and brief.

## By vertical — the observation, the cost question, the after-state

**Luxury Real Estate**
- *Show you know them:* "A team at your level does not change houses, or scale that fast, lightly. From outside it looks like a serious step."
- *Cost question:* "The modern buyer turns up already holding the data — flood zones, roof age, the last sale price. What does it cost you on the days an agent is not as prepared as the buyer?"
- *After-state:* "The teams who get this right have every agent show up as prepared as their best one, without anyone living in the pipeline."

**Medical Spa**
- *Show you know them:* "Opening a second location is the moment most owners feel the ground shift, even when it looks effortless from outside."
- *Cost question:* "Inventory, follow-up and compliance all stretch at once across two sites. What does that stretch cost you before it ever shows up in a review?"
- *After-state:* "The spas who get past that wall add rooms and services without the founder holding it together by hand."

**Golf and Country Clubs**
- *Show you know them:* "A club investing in the member experience at that level is making a real statement about where it is headed."
- *Cost question:* "Every amenity brings its own booking and billing, usually across tools that do not speak. What does that friction quietly cost — in margin, or in younger members who expected seamless?"
- *After-state:* "The clubs who integrate it stop taxing the experience to run the operation."

**Homebuilders**
- *Show you know them:* "That kind of throughput tests the seams of any operation, however good the finished homes look."
- *Cost question:* "A build is dozens of stakeholders across months. What does one slipped handoff cost you by the end of a job?"
- *After-state:* "The builders who get the coordination layer right protect the margin without it living in one person's head."

**Private Aviation** *(the long game — softer throughout)*
- *Show you know them:* "Growth in this business is a quiet kind of pressure, because the whole thing rides on coordination staying flawless."
- *Cost question:* "Crew, maintenance compliance, real-time availability — what would a single lapse cost a reputation built over years?"
- *After-state:* "The operators who systematise it scale the discretion instead of risking it." *(Close with "whenever the timing suits," never a this-week ask.)*

## Objections — remove the reason, then let them decide

The bearing never shifts: calm, no defending, no pushing past a no. You are taking pressure off, not applying it.

**"I am not interested."**
> "Completely fair, and I expected that — you do not know me. One thing before I go: is the timing wrong, or is this genuinely handled? If it is handled, I will leave you be." *(If not handled — )* "Then can I ask, if the systems underneath were quietly costing you, how would you even know? That is the whole of what the twenty minutes answers."

**"Just send me information."**
> "I could, but honestly a brochure would waste your time — it tells you about us, and you do not need that. The twenty minutes tells you about you. Shall I hold one slot, and you decide after? If it is not worth it, you have lost nothing."

**"We already have someone."**
> "Good — that puts you ahead of most, and I am not here to replace anyone. If the layer underneath were leaking, though, how would you know from the inside? The twenty minutes answers that, your team keeps the findings, and if it confirms they have it handled, that is a good outcome too."

**"Is this a sales call? How much?"**
> "It is not, and I know that is what everyone says. There is nothing to buy in the twenty minutes. If a build ever made sense, we would talk through value before any number — but that is later, and only if you want it."

**"I do not have time."**
> "I assumed not, and that is rather the point — the busier you are, the more those gaps cost while you are looking elsewhere. I am not asking for now. Twenty minutes later in the week, nothing to prepare."

**"Call me back next quarter."**
> "I can. So I do not waste the call — what would need to have changed by then for this to be worth a proper look?" *(Listen, note it, set a soft date.)*

## Gatekeeper

Respect them — they decide whether you reach anyone. Enlist, never bulldoze.

> "Hi, I am hoping you can help me. I am trying to reach [First Name], and I suspect you might be the person who really keeps that office running. Martavious Spicer, Five Points. It is about [signal — the new office, the expansion], and I promise it is not a vendor pitch. Is [First Name] the right person, or is it really you I should be speaking with?"

Give the gatekeeper the same honest one-liner you would give the principal.

---

## The take-away — when to close the door

If after the invitation it is still no, close clean and leave it open:

> "Understood, and no problem at all. I will leave you my details. If the timing ever changes, the twenty minutes stands. Good luck with [signal]."

Then move on. Never a third ask. The composure on the way out is what reopens the door later. There are 396 names behind this one.
