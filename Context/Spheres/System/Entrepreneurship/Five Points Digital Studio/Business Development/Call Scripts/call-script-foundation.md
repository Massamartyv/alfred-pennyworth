# Foundation Repair – Audit Call Script

**Offer:** $1,970 one-time audit. Three phases: 45-minute discovery meeting, audit document (money leaks, hours leaks, task inventory), presentation meeting with implementation plan.
**Goal of the call:** book the 45-minute discovery meeting. Always capture the cell number.

---

## Pre-dial (60 seconds)

- Review count
- Inspectors/estimators on the team page
- Financing offered on the site (means they close big tickets)
- Owner name from the site or license record

## If the gatekeeper answers

> "Hey – is [Owner] around? … It's Marty. He's not expecting me – it's about the business itself, not booking a job. Thirty seconds of his time."

## Open

> "Hey [Name] – Marty. Quick one. [Company] keeps coming up when I look at foundation work in [Metro], so I figured I'd go straight to the owner."

## Money line

> "Your whole business is the inspector's close rate. At a twenty-grand average ticket, one point of close is a hundred and fifty grand a year. Most owners know their revenue to the dollar – almost none know close rate by inspector, by lead source, by month. That's the audit."

## The ask

> "Here's how it works – three steps. First, forty-five minutes with me: I map how the company actually runs. Leads, inspections, closes, where the hours go. Then I go build the audit on my own time – you don't lift a finger. Then I come back and walk you through the document: where the money leaks, where the hours leak, and the plan to fix both. The document's yours either way."

## Objections

**"My guys close fine."**

> "Maybe so – what's the number? If you know it to the point, I'll hang up happy and you've lost nothing. If you're guessing, that guess is the most expensive number in your business."

**"We're slammed."**

> "If you're slammed, you're about to hire another inspector. The audit tells you whether you actually need another body – or a better close rate on the ones you have."

**"Is this a sales call?"**

> "Yeah, it is – and I'll respect your time more than most of them do. Thirty seconds, then tell me to get lost if it's not worth it."

**"We've already got a marketing company / we're in a peer group."**

> "Keep 'em – I'm not marketing and I'm not a mastermind. This is an audit: your pricing, your close rates, your cash cycle. A second set of eyes on your own numbers. Guys pay Nexstar four grand a month. This is two grand, once."

**"How much?"**

> "Nineteen-seventy, one time. No retainer, no contract, and the report is yours forever. And look – you're already paying the other price. You just haven't seen the invoice yet."

**"Send me something."**

> "Happy to – what's your cell? I'll text the one-pager right now while we're on… got it? Good. If it reads right, what's Thursday look like?"

**"Not interested."**

> "Fair enough. One thing before I go – do you know your close rate, to the point? If that number ever starts itching, you've got mine."

## Booking discipline

- Book the 45 minutes while still on the phone: "What's Thursday look like?"
- Capture the cell. Text the confirmation immediately after hanging up.
- If they are mid-inspection: get the cell, ask for a better hour, call back then. Do not push.
- Log the outcome after every dial.

## Voicemail (15 seconds, then always text)

> "Hey [Name], Marty Spicer. [Company] keeps coming up on foundation work in [Metro]. I audit foundation companies – I find where the money leaks. In your numbers, one time, no retainer. Texting you now – this is my cell."

## Follow-up text

> "Marty here – just left you a voicemail. One-liner: one point of inspector close rate at a $20K ticket is $150K a year. Worth a look? First step is a 45-minute sit-down – I bring what I found."
