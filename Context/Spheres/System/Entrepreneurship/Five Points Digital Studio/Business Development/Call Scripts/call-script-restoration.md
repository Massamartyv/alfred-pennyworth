# Restoration – Audit Call Script

**Offer:** $1,970 one-time audit. Three phases: 45-minute discovery meeting, audit document (money leaks, hours leaks, task inventory), presentation meeting with implementation plan.
**Goal of the call:** book the 45-minute discovery meeting. Always capture the cell number.

---

## Pre-dial (60 seconds)

- Independent only – skip franchise brands (Servpro, PuroClean, 911 Restoration, ServiceMaster)
- IICRC certification listing
- 24/7 emergency line (staffed after-hours means scale)
- Review count; owner name from the site or license record

## If the gatekeeper answers

> "Hey – is [Owner] around? … It's Marty. He's not expecting me – it's about the business itself, not booking a job. Thirty seconds of his time."

## Open

> "Hey [Name] – Marty. I know you might be standing in two inches of water right now, so I'll be thirty seconds."

## Money line

> "Restoration companies are profitable on paper and broke at the bank – the margin sits in carrier receivables for ninety-plus days. The audit maps your cash cycle and your carrier mix: which programs actually pay you, and which ones quietly bleed you for the volume."

## The ask

> "Here's how it works – three steps. First, forty-five minutes with me: I map how the company actually runs. Jobs, carriers, collections, where the hours go. Then I go build the audit on my own time – you don't lift a finger. Then I come back and walk you through the document: where the money leaks, where the hours leak, and the plan to fix both. The document's yours either way."

## Objections

**"We're on programs – the TPA handles all that."**

> "Programs buy you volume and sell your margin. I'm not telling you to leave them – I'm telling you to know exactly what each one costs you, so the mix is a decision instead of a default."

**"Is this a sales call?"**

> "Yeah, it is – and I'll respect your time more than most of them do. Thirty seconds, then tell me to get lost if it's not worth it."

**"We've already got a marketing company / we're in a peer group."**

> "Keep 'em – I'm not marketing and I'm not a mastermind. This is an audit: your pricing, your cash cycle, your carrier mix. A second set of eyes on your own numbers. Guys pay Nexstar four grand a month. This is two grand, once."

**"How much?"**

> "Nineteen-seventy, one time. No retainer, no contract, and the report is yours forever. And look – you're already paying the other price. You just haven't seen the invoice yet."

**"Send me something."**

> "Happy to – what's your cell? I'll text the one-pager right now while we're on… got it? Good. If it reads right, what's Thursday look like?"

**"Not interested."**

> "Fair enough. One thing before I go – do you know what each carrier program actually costs you? If that number ever starts itching, you've got mine."

## Mid-loss discipline

- If they are on a loss: do not push. Get the cell, book a slot, off the phone in under a minute. The restraint is the impression.

## Booking discipline

- Book the 45 minutes while still on the phone: "What's Thursday look like?"
- Capture the cell. Text the confirmation immediately after hanging up.
- Log the outcome after every dial.

## Voicemail (15 seconds, then always text)

> "Hey [Name], Marty Spicer. I know you might be mid-loss, so I'll be quick. I audit restoration companies – I find where the money leaks, starting with carrier receivables. One time, no retainer. Texting you now – this is my cell."

## Follow-up text

> "Marty here – just left you a voicemail. One-liner: your margin is sitting in carrier receivables 90-plus days – I find the cash. Worth a look? First step is a 45-minute sit-down – I bring what I found."
