# Roofing – Audit Call Script

**Offer:** $1,970 one-time audit. Three phases: 45-minute discovery meeting, audit document (money leaks, hours leaks, task inventory), presentation meeting with implementation plan.
**Goal of the call:** book the 45-minute discovery meeting. Always capture the cell number.

---

## Pre-dial (60 seconds)

- Manufacturer tier: GAF Master Elite or Owens Corning Platinum (volume-gated, from the directory)
- Review count and service area
- LSA badge or active ads
- Owner name from the site or license record

## If the gatekeeper answers

> "Hey – is [Owner] around? … It's Marty. He's not expecting me – it's about the business itself, not booking a job. Thirty seconds of his time."

## Open

> "Hey [Name] – Marty. Thirty seconds. I saw [Company] is Master Elite – that's the top two percent of roofers in the country, so I know you're doing volume."

*(No tier? Fall back to review count: "[X] reviews in [Metro] – you're clearly doing volume.")*

## Money line

> "You're buying leads at three to six hundred apiece. Most roofers close one in four sits, and nobody can tell them where the other three went. One point of close rate at your ticket size is six figures a year. That's what I find – in your numbers, not in theory."

## The ask

> "Here's how it works – three steps. First, forty-five minutes with me: I map how the company actually runs. Leads, sits, closes, where the hours go. Then I go build the audit on my own time – you don't lift a finger. Then I come back and walk you through the document: where the money leaks, where the hours leak, and the plan to fix both. The document's yours either way."

## The exit card (play when they sound seasoned)

> "One more thing – PE is buying roofing companies at four and five times earnings right now. Every dollar of profit I find isn't a dollar. It's five, the day you ever sell."

## Objections

**"It's storm season – call me in January."**

> "That's exactly why I'm calling now. You're generating more data this month than you'll see all winter. I do the work, you give me forty-five minutes – and by January you'll know what to fix before the slow season starts eating cash."

**"Is this a sales call?"**

> "Yeah, it is – and I'll respect your time more than most of them do. Thirty seconds, then tell me to get lost if it's not worth it."

**"We've already got a marketing company / we're in a peer group."**

> "Keep 'em – I'm not marketing and I'm not a mastermind. This is an audit: your pricing, your close rates, your cash cycle. A second set of eyes on your own numbers. Guys pay Nexstar four grand a month. This is two grand, once."

**"How much?"**

> "Nineteen-seventy, one time. No retainer, no contract, and the report is yours forever. And look – you're already paying the other price. You just haven't seen the invoice yet."

**"Send me something."**

> "Happy to – what's your cell? I'll text the one-pager right now while we're on… got it? Good. If it reads right, what's Thursday look like?"

**"Not interested."**

> "Fair enough. One thing before I go – do you know where your other three sits go? If that number ever starts itching, you've got mine."

## Booking discipline

- Book the 45 minutes while still on the phone: "What's Thursday look like?"
- Capture the cell. Text the confirmation immediately after hanging up.
- If they are on a roof or mid-build: get the cell, ask for a better hour, call back then. Do not push.
- Log the outcome after every dial.

## Voicemail (15 seconds, then always text)

> "Hey [Name], Marty Spicer. Saw you're Master Elite – top two percent, so you're doing volume. I audit roofing companies – I find where the money leaks. In your numbers, one time, no retainer. Texting you now – this is my cell."

## Follow-up text

> "Marty here – just left you a voicemail. One-liner: one point of close rate at your ticket size is six figures a year. Worth a look? First step is a 45-minute sit-down – I bring what I found."
