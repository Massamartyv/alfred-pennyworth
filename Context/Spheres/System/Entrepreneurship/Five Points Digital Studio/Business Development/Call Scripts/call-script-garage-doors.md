# Garage Doors – Audit Call Script

**Offer:** $1,970 one-time audit. Three phases: 45-minute discovery meeting, audit document (money leaks, hours leaks, task inventory), presentation meeting with implementation plan.
**Goal of the call:** book the 45-minute discovery meeting. Always capture the cell number.

---

## Pre-dial (60 seconds)

- Review count and rating
- LSA "Google Guaranteed" badge, or active ads in the Meta Ad Library
- Trucks visible in website/Facebook photos
- Owner name from the site or license record

## If the gatekeeper answers

> "Hey – is [Owner] around? … It's Marty. He's not expecting me – it's about the business itself, not booking a job. Thirty seconds of his time."

## Open

> "Hey [Name] – Marty. You don't know me, so I'll be quick. I was looking at [Company] – [X] reviews in [Metro], you're clearly running real volume."

## Money line (rotate across the block)

**A – Missed-call leak.** Use on shops with the LSA badge or active ads – they are paying for every call that rings out.

> "When a spring snaps, homeowners call down the list till somebody answers. Every call your office misses or mishandles is a four-hundred-dollar job handed to the next name on the stack. I audit how much of your own demand you're actually keeping."

**B – Ask the ticket.** Use on bigger shops with office staff, where the owner sits at arm's length from the trucks.

> "Quick question – you know what yesterday did in revenue. Do you know yesterday's average ticket, by tech? Most owners nail the first and go quiet on the second. That silence is where I work."

## The ask

> "Here's how it works – three steps. First, forty-five minutes with me: I map how the shop actually runs. Calls, dispatch, tickets, where the hours go. Then I go build the audit on my own time – you don't lift a finger. Then I come back and walk you through the document: where the money leaks, where the hours leak, and the plan to fix both. The document's yours either way."

## Objections

**"We're slammed."**

> "If you're slammed, you're about to buy a truck or hire a tech. The audit tells you whether you actually need capacity – or just better tickets on the calls you already run."

**"Is this a sales call?"**

> "Yeah, it is – and I'll respect your time more than most of them do. Thirty seconds, then tell me to get lost if it's not worth it."

**"We've already got a marketing company / we're in a peer group."**

> "Keep 'em – I'm not marketing and I'm not a mastermind. This is an audit: your pricing, your close rates, your cash cycle. A second set of eyes on your own numbers. Guys pay Nexstar four grand a month. This is two grand, once."

**"How much?"**

> "Nineteen-seventy, one time. No retainer, no contract, and the report is yours forever. And look – you're already paying the other price. You just haven't seen the invoice yet."

**"Send me something."**

> "Happy to – what's your cell? I'll text the one-pager right now while we're on… got it? Good. If it reads right, what's Thursday look like?"

**"Not interested."**

> "Fair enough. One thing before I go – how much of your own demand are you actually keeping? If that number ever starts itching, you've got mine."

## Booking discipline

- Book the 45 minutes while still on the phone: "What's Thursday look like?"
- Capture the cell. Text the confirmation immediately after hanging up.
- If they are mid-job: get the cell, ask when the board cools, call back then. Do not push.
- Log the outcome after every dial.

## Voicemail (15 seconds, then always text)

> "Hey [Name], Marty Spicer. Saw the [X] reviews – you're running real volume. I audit garage door companies – I find where the money leaks. In your numbers, one time, no retainer. Texting you now – this is my cell."

## Follow-up text

> "Marty here – just left you a voicemail. One-liner: every missed call in this trade is a $400 job handed to the next name on the stack. Worth a look? First step is a 45-minute sit-down – I bring what I found."
