# Cohort briefs

Four cohorts, four framings of the same free-value instrument. Read the
relevant section before drafting.

---

## COHORT A — Owner-operator practices and clinics

Aesthetic surgery, amputation prevention, behavioural health, group
counselling, a fitness studio. Different clinical worlds, one shared
condition: **the owner is the runtime.** Revenue stops when they stop.

**The instrument:** the Owner Dependency Index. Twelve questions, one
number, scored against how much of the business runs without them. It is
also the largest discount applied when a practice sells, which every one
of them will eventually do and none has measured.

**The ask:** help break it before it goes public.

**What they care about:** their calendar, their front desk, the enquiries
that never called back, and eventually getting out.

**Never mention:** patients, clinical outcomes, or anything that could be
read as commentary on their care. The subject is the business only.

---

## COHORT B — Fractional executives and professional-services principals

Fractional CFOs and COOs, a law firm managing partner, niche consultancies.
They sell their expertise by the hour and have no leverage, which they
know and find uncomfortable.

**The highest-value cohort.** Not because they spend most, but because
what works for them gets resold to their own client books. One convert
here is a distribution channel.

**The instrument:** the Practice Leverage Score. Same architecture,
reframed around whether their delivery is a system or a performance.

**The ask:** professional critique. These people diagnose businesses for a
living, so asking them to diagnose a diagnostic is a real compliment and
a real request at once.

**Never:** explain their own business back to them. They will know more
than the research does. Ask, do not tell.

---

## COHORT C — Founder-led brands, media and consumer

CPG, luxury travel, creative agencies, media brands. Marty's native
territory and his warmest relationships. Slowest to convert, most likely
to reply.

**Instagram is the channel here.** Follow, let a day pass, engage with
something real, then message.

**The instrument:** the Brand Operations Audit. Where the brand promise
and the operational reality come apart.

**The ask:** a look at it as someone who has built one.

**Watch:** this cohort detects a pitch faster than any other, because they
receive several a day. If the message is not genuinely interesting on its
own, do not send it.

---

## COHORT D — Institutional and civic principals

Nonprofit CEOs, a development authority, major construction, a utility, a
college president, enterprise SaaS.

**Long cycles. Procurement gates. Patience.**

Do not run the scorecard play here. It is calibrated to owner-operators
and will read as naive to someone running a $30M budget. The right move
is a genuine question about something they are publicly working on, with
no ask attached at all. Relationship first, over months.

**LinkedIn only.** Personal Instagram will read as unserious.

---

## COHORT E — Early-stage founders

Real founders, unproven budget. Twenty-seven of them.

**Free value only. No pitch, now or later this quarter.**

They are peers, not prospects. Some will become clients in two years and
some will refer someone next month. Treat them as neither and the network
compounds on its own.
