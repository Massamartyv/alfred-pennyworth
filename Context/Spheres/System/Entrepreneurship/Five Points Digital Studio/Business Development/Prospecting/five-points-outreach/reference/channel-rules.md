# Channel rules

## Why 15 and not 100

Marty asked for 100 in a day. Three reasons that number does not survive
contact with reality, and one thing to do about it.

**1. The pool is 68.** Reaching 100 means adding names already judged not
to be buyers. Every one of those dilutes the average and costs the same
effort as a real one.

**2. Platform limits are real and enforced.** Instagram restricts DMs to
non-followers aggressively, and a burst of near-identical messages from
an account with no prior DM history is the exact pattern its spam systems
are built to catch. LinkedIn caps messages to non-connections and weights
against accounts with low reply rates. A restricted account cannot be
appealed quickly, and losing the network mid-campaign costs more than the
campaign is worth.

**3. Quality does not survive the volume.** The entire premise is that
these messages do not read as outreach. That requires real research and a
real hook per person. At 100 a day the research becomes a template with
a name swapped in, which is the thing this strategy exists to avoid.

**What to do instead:** 15 a day, sustained. That is 68 in five days,
every one of them properly researched, with the network intact at the end
and reply rates that make the next round easier.

## Per channel

**LinkedIn** — Default for cohorts A, B and D. Connection request with a
note, or a direct message where already connected. All 68 are first-degree
connections, so direct messages are available throughout. Under 300
characters for connection notes.

**Instagram** — Better for cohort C, where the brand is the person and the
DM is the native register. Follow first, let a day pass, engage with
something genuinely, then message. Cold DMs from an unfamiliar account go
to requests and stay there.

**On using the personal account.** Sensible while Five Points has no
presence. Worth naming as a real decision rather than a stopgap: for
cohort C it is probably better than a company account, because it is a
person with taste writing to another person with taste. For cohort D,
institutional and civic, it will read as unserious. Use LinkedIn there.

## Never

- No automated sending. Every message goes by Marty's hand.
- No identical message to two people. Ever.
- No follow-up sooner than 10 days, and no more than one.
- No connection request to anyone marked `hold` or `passed`.
