# Asset Governance Doctrine

The lens through which Five Points holds every engagement after the build ships. Adopted by operator ruling 2026-07-22. The lens arrived through Evan Brown of BlackRock in conversation with the founder – the institutional-finance framing of stewardship, translated into the house canon. The provenance is part of the authority: this is how the largest asset manager in the world describes the ongoing care of assets under management, applied to the operating infrastructure Five Points commissions.

---

## The reframe

What the market calls a maintenance fee, an institution calls governance. The difference is not cosmetic. A maintenance fee is a cost the owner tolerates – money out, in exchange for the machine not breaking. Governance is a fiduciary discipline the owner can audit – the named terms under which a commissioned asset is kept sound, kept honest and kept compounding.

Five Points does not sell maintenance and never has – the companion service is Evolution, and the fingerprint forbids the retainer frame outright. This doctrine gives Evolution its internal anatomy. Evolution is the governance of the commissioned asset. That sentence is the whole reframe.

## The three pillars

Governance decomposes into three named commitments. Every Evolution engagement carries all three.

### 1. Infrastructure

The commissioned system itself, kept sound. Uptime, dependencies, integrations, security posture, the platform decisions that keep the asset load-bearing as the operation around it grows. The stewardship of the thing that was built.

### 2. Maintenance

The ongoing upkeep and – critically – the named terms of that upkeep. What is covered, at what cadence, to what standard. Precision is non-negotiable here as everywhere: the governance terms are written, not implied. An owner should be able to point at the document and know exactly what the fee governs.

### 3. Data integrity

The pillar most houses skip, and the one that carries the fiduciary weight. Verifying that the data the system reports matches reality – that the numbers on the dashboard reconcile with the numbers in the world. A system that runs but lies is worse than a system that is down, because the owner steers by it. Data integrity is checked as part of preparing every governance review, maturing toward a standalone audit discipline as the roster and tooling grow.

## The governance review

Governance carries a built-in accountability rhythm. Quarterly, the studio cuts the data and delivers a review with three movements:

1. **The success analysis.** Performance measured against the expectations named at commissioning. The commissioning document states what the asset was built to do; the review answers whether it is doing it. Assertions, not vibes – each expectation satisfied or not, with the evidence shown.
2. **The integrity reconciliation.** The data verified against reality before it is presented. The review never reports a number the studio has not checked.
3. **The competitive benchmark.** The owner's position measured against their competitors. The asset does not exist in a vacuum; the review places it in the market it was built to win.

The quarterly cadence is deliberate. Quarterly cuts are the rhythm of fund management – it is what a principal with capital under management already expects from the people who steward it. The review is delivered as a branded document in the house register with a live walkthrough; the ritual of the reading matters as much as the document.

The operating procedure lives at `Operations/SOPs/quarterly-governance-review.md`.

## Commercial framing

The Evolution fee is presented as what it is: a governance fee. One number – 20% of commission value annually, billed quarterly, per the private menu discipline – itemised in the commissioning document against the three pillars and the quarterly review, so the principal sees exactly what the fee governs. The word governance does positioning work: it borrows the gravity of institutional stewardship and it is the truthful name for the discipline. The fee stops being a cost the owner tolerates and becomes a relationship the owner can audit.

The Two Prices rule applies as everywhere – the governance quote carries the cost of yes and the ledger of no. The ledger of no for governance is specific: an ungoverned system drifts, its data quietly diverges from reality and the owner steers by instruments no one has calibrated.

## What this doctrine touches

- **Foundation** – this document. The lens itself.
- **The brand fingerprint** – the Evolution terminology row gains the governance framing by amendment. Client-facing language: Evolution is the name of the door; governance is the name of the discipline.
- **Operations** – the quarterly governance review SOP and its cadence across the active roster.
- **The offer suite** – Evolution proposals and commissioning documents itemise the three pillars. The governance frame strengthens rather than replaces the asset canon: governance is how an asset compounds after the build.
