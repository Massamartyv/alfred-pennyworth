# Paradigm and Paradigm Farms - Mark Construction Brief

Specification for drawing the two primary marks. Written for a designer or
illustrator executing the work. Direction is fixed; execution is open within
the constraints below.

## 1. Concept the marks serve

Satoyama, the tended border. The governing sentence: the most alive ground is
the ground someone keeps.

The two marks are two views of one subject.

| Mark | Owner | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Thumbprint | Paradigm | Tended ground seen in plan. Ridges and furrows, contour-following, never gridded |
| Tree rings | Paradigm Farms | The same ground seen in section. The record of the tending, year by year |

The mark does not mean identity in the biometric sense. It means kept land,
mapped. Every construction decision below serves that reading and not the
authentication one.

## 2. Source material

The reference photograph supplied by the operator is a phone capture in raking
sunlight. It establishes the pattern and the character. It is not a capture
suitable for tracing.

Required before drawing begins:

- A rolled ink impression on smooth card, taken at least five times, cleanest
  impression selected
- Flatbed scan of the selected impression at 1200 dpi minimum, grayscale, no
  sharpening applied
- A second impression taken as a plain vertical press, not rolled, for
  comparison of core position

Pattern classification (whorl, loop, arch, tented arch) is to be confirmed from
the ink impression, not assumed from the photograph.

## 3. Construction principles

The operator has specified golden ratio construction. The following is the
version that works and the version that does not.

### Does not

Overlaying Fibonacci circles on a drawn mark, or generating the ridge flow from
a golden spiral. Ridge spacing in an adult print is approximately uniform at
0.4 to 0.5 mm and is not a logarithmic progression. Forcing one produces a
synthetic result, which is the opposite of the brief.

### Does

Phi governs proportion and placement. The hand governs deviation.

1. Bounding envelope is an oval at 1:1.618, vertical
2. The core sits at the phi division of the vertical axis, not at the center
3. The dominant ridge flow follows a logarithmic spiral whose growth rate is
   derived from phi, applied to the flow path only
4. Ridge spacing remains optically even across the field, as in life
5. Minimum three ridge endings and two bifurcations are retained from the
   actual impression, in the positions where they actually occur

Governing test: if a viewer can see the golden ratio, the construction has
failed. It must be recoverable by a designer and invisible to everyone else.

## 4. Ridge reduction

A real print carries 40 or more visible ridges. The primary mark carries 11 to
15. The reduction is the design act, not a compromise of it.

Reduction rules:

- Preserve the flow topology. A whorl stays a whorl
- Preserve the delta or deltas. They are the structural anchors of the pattern
- Remove ridges from the outer field first, working inward
- Never remove a retained minutia
- Stroke weight is uniform. No tapering, no calligraphic modulation

## 5. Reduction tests

The mark ships only when it passes all of the following.

| Context | Size | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Favicon | 16 px | Reads as a whorl, not as a smudge |
| App icon | 24 and 32 px | Core and outer flow both legible |
| Woven or embroidered label | 20 mm | Minimum 1 mm between ridges at final scale. No fill-in |
| Deboss on leather | 25 mm | Single-line variant only |
| Foil on board | 40 mm | Full mark |
| Wall or signage | 3 m viewing distance | Full mark, no added detail |

The embroidery test is the binding constraint. Apparel leads the products
pillar, so a mark that fills in at 20 mm on cloth is not usable regardless of
how well it performs on screen.

## 6. The Paradigm Farms mark

Same envelope, same stroke weight range, same optical density. The two must sit
beside each other and read as siblings without either dominating.

- Concentric closed rings rather than open flow
- Ring widths must be derived from a published dendrochronological series
  rather than invented. The International Tree-Ring Data Bank holds
  chronologies suitable for this. The mark is then a real record, which is the
  entire point of the concept
- Species and series to be selected and documented on this file once chosen
- Pith placed off-center at the phi division, matching the core placement in the
  thumbprint
- Retain at least one growth check or narrow-ring sequence. The bad years are
  what make it a record rather than a pattern

## 7. Color and finish

Black on white for the master. The mark is monochrome in every application.

Palette lives in the world around the mark, never in the mark itself. Approved
substitutions for the black: ink #241B17, cocoa #3E2A22. Approved grounds:
bone #E8E3DC, shell #F3EFE9.

## 8. Prohibitions

None of the following ever appear on or around either mark.

- Gradients, drop shadows, bevels, glows
- A rounded container, badge, or app-icon squircle
- Any biometric or scanner idiom: corner brackets, scan lines, pulse rings,
  progress arcs, unlock states
- Pairing with fingerprint-unlock interface language in product or marketing
- Japanese visual signifiers of any kind. The concept is inherited, the
  costume is not
- Any use of the tree-ring mark as a target, dartboard, or radar motif

## 9. Deliverables

1. Master vector drawing of each mark, outlined, at three optical sizes:
   macro, standard, micro
2. Embroidery-reduced variant of each
3. Single-line variant of each for deboss and foil
4. Lockup set: mark with wordmark, horizontal and stacked, with clear-space
   rule expressed in ridge widths rather than in x-height
5. Construction drawing showing the envelope, the phi divisions, and the flow
   spiral, for archive
6. Source impression scans, retained

## 10. Handoff

Direction from the art director layer. Execution routes to the `design` skill
or to a named illustrator. Typography is decided separately and is not a
dependency of this work.

Type specimen for the surrounding system: see the Ridges and Furrows specimen
page, September 2026.
