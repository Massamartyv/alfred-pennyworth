# Molly Maid

**Who** – A residential cleaning franchise operating at the commodity tier of the US home-services market. One of the two named anti-references for Lillie and Lynette in the revenue battle plan.

**Why aligned** – This card exists to draw a line, not a lesson. Molly Maid names the position Lillie and Lynette categorically refuses: franchise commodity cleaning, volume over standard, price competing downward. The brand is not poorly run – it is correctly run for the market it serves. That market is not this one. The anti-reference is precise: the pricing tier, the visual language, the operational model and the client relationship are all defined-against, not drawn-from. The $250 minimum engagement guardrail in the battle plan is a direct structural refusal of this tier.

**The proposition** – Commodity throughput. Speed and price over standard and relationship. The franchise model scales by replication of a minimum viable clean, not a hospitality-grade experience. The client is a transaction, not a guest.

**Canon** – The Molly Maid brand at scale in US residential cleaning: uniform pricing, franchise playbook, coupon-driven acquisition. Named explicitly in the battle plan as the anti-reference alongside The Cleaning Authority.

**Pull for** – positioning, proposition, offer-design, restraint. Summon this card when drafting pricing tiers, defining the floor of the brand promise, or writing any language that touches what Lillie and Lynette is not.

**Trend read** – Pending enrichment. The commodity-cleaning franchise tier remains structurally unchanged. The anti-reference is durable.
