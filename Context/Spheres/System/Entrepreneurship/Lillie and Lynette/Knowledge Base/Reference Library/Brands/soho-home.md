# Soho Home

**Who** – The retail arm of Soho House, selling the furniture, linens, tableware and home accessories that appear throughout the members clubs. Named in the Lillie and Lynette revenue battle plan with the signal: "you slept in it, now you own it."

**Why aligned** – That six-word quotation is the clearest possible articulation of the L&L Objects mechanism. Soho Home does not have to explain the feeling of its products; the feeling was delivered first, in the clubs, during stays and evenings already associated with ease and belonging. The product purchase is the extension of a felt experience, not the beginning of one. This is the emotional and commercial architecture Lillie and Lynette is building at the domestic scale: the formulary enters the kept home as an amenity, it becomes part of the home's character, and it becomes purchasable when the client reaches for it.

**The proposition** – Objects that carry a world already experienced. Soho Home's retail proposition rests entirely on the hospitality experience being real and well-loved first. The brand does not have to manufacture aspiration – the clubs manufactured it. The home goods inherit that warmth by association and by consistency of quality. The register is relaxed luxury, layered interiors, materials that suggest life lived in rather than staged.

**Canon** – The Soho House member clubs as the originating experience. The Soho Home retail catalogue – linens, glassware, tableware, furniture. The brand's expansion into the home as an extension of the hospitality identity.

**Pull for** – offer-design, merchandising, ritual, positioning, narrative. Summon this card when articulating the emotional rationale for the Objects amenity sequence, writing any copy that positions the formulary transition from service amenity to retail product, or briefing the Phase 4 e-commerce positioning.

**Trend read** – Pending enrichment. Soho Home's model was active at the time of the Direction session. The hospitality-to-retail structural logic is durable.
